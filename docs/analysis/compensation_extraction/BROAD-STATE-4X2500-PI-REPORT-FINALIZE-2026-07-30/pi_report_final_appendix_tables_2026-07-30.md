## Mechanism pressure and side audit

| mechanism | side_conclusion | pressure_direction | current_evidence | pathway | limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-base compensation | Safety-heavy among resolved records; non-safety examples confirmed | Upward safety total-compensation pressure; potentially gap-widening | 1,528 resolved safety spans versus 226 non-safety spans; 1,964 additional spans remain side-unclear. | Longevity, shift, hazard, specialty, certification, stipend, allowance, and overtime-related provisions add compensation outside base schedules. | No matched total-compensation estimator; the distribution is evidence coverage, not prevalence. |
| Base schedules and listed rates | Mixed | Observed safety advantage in Shreve/Cammack; non-safety advantage in Canastota | 2,687 direct value signals; 661 explicit schedule tags; six schedule-tagged spans fully normalized; one validated and three conditional local comparisons. | A municipality can assign different base-rate levels and progression ladders to occupations in the same period. | Entry/top/unlabeled rates, rank, employment status, and bargaining scope must match. |
| Implementation, retroactivity, and payment form | Safety-heavy documentary layer; comparative sign not established | Timing/realization effect; recurring increases compound, lump sums do not | The audit distinguishes recurring base increases, retroactive base payments, one-time lump sums, delayed implementation, and unresolved payment form. | Earlier effective dates and retroactivity preserve foregone pay; delayed implementation postpones cash realization; recurring base changes feed future schedules. | Few same-city, same-cycle safety/non-safety timing pairs survive exact-role review. |
| Automatic raises, COLA/CPI, and steps | Safety-heavy audited claim layer, with fire and non-safety counterexamples | Upward pressure on the covered side; gap direction depends on the paired unit's rule | The rescue produced 416 quantitative mechanism records, but the report-use audit removes misclassified insurance, occupation, and proposal fragments before comparison. | A scheduled percentage, index rule, or step increase mechanically raises the covered schedule between effective periods. | A percentage in a source is not necessarily an enacted wage increase; payer, base, period, and recurrence must be verified. |
| Bargaining and dispute resolution | Safety-heavy in volume; direct wage-linked evidence remains mixed/unclear | Potential upward or constraining pressure depending demands, offers, award, and settlement | 1,716 audited spans: 77 direct wage-linked, 1,578 context, 61 false positives. Safety 714, non-safety 292, mixed 39, unclear 671. | Collective bargaining sets terms; interest arbitration/factfinding resolves impasse; settlement fixes value and timing; grievance arbitration usually enforces existing terms; strike rules alter leverage. | Institutional presence does not identify the sign or size of a wage effect. |
| Rank, step, specialization, and classification | Mixed | Can widen or narrow listed-rate differences depending schedule location | Canastota's Year 1 police rate is below its code-enforcement rate, while higher police steps could reverse the contrast; Alburtis shows how command rank can inflate a nominal gap. | Separate ladders change both starting pay and the speed/ceiling of progression. | A police entry step is not equivalent to an unlabeled, actual, or maximum non-safety rate. |
| Market, recruitment, retention, and staffing | Safety-heavy among resolved direct spans, but present on both sides | Upward pressure on the occupation targeted by a market adjustment or retention response | Among 83 direct wage-pressure spans, 33 are safety, four non-safety, and 46 unclear. Brewster dispatch is a strong non-safety example. | Employers raise base pay, add market adjustments, bonuses, or stipends, or revise schedules to recruit or retain workers. | 51 rated-positive false positives were removed; rationale does not prove realized staffing or wage effects. |
| Bounded local listed-rate differentials | Mixed | Safety advantage in Shreve/Cammack/Alburtis; non-safety advantage in Canastota | Shreve is PI-usable; Cammack and Canastota are conditional; Alburtis is limits-only. | Ordinances and schedules encode occupation-specific base-rate outcomes; they show where a differential exists but not which upstream mechanism produced it. | Named-position documentary contrasts are not final wage-gap estimates. |
