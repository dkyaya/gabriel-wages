# Lane 004 — Position/Schedule Matching and Bounded-Gap Audit

## Conclusion

The matched-structure stage was completed: it produced 2,712 municipality-cycle groups, 65 groups containing both safety and non-safety evidence, and 303 candidate record pairs. The comparison layer is therefore no longer merely hypothetical. It supports a narrow set of current documentary comparisons and a larger, explicitly tiered review queue.

The prior automated label `ready_for_bounded_review` is a structural screen, not a substantive comparability verdict. Twenty-seven of the 303 pairs pass the four coded compatibility flags for pay basis, effective period, unit/rank/step, and base/non-base status. Subsequent source-level validation found that occupation labels and schedule positions still require inspection: some supposed safety records contain non-safety roles such as code enforcement, construction, zoning, elected officials, or administrative positions. Four rescued candidates received candidate-level validation; one is PI-report usable and three remain conditional.

## Strongest current comparison

Shreve, Ohio is the cleanest outcome-side comparison. The same 2024 ordinance assigns part-time police officers $22.00 per hour and a part-time utility clerk $16.00 per hour. On that source-grounded hourly basis, the police rate is $6.00 higher, or 37.5 percent above the clerk rate. The ordinance is direct evidence that local salary-setting produced a higher scheduled rate for the police position. It does not identify which upstream mechanism—bargaining, scarcity, job requirements, or political priority—produced the rate difference.

## Conditional comparisons

- Cammack Village compares maximum authorized hourly rates: $25.00 for a part-time patrolman and $20.00 for an administrative assistant. It shows a safety-side ceiling 25 percent higher, but not necessarily an actual-pay difference; the mayor could set pay below the ceiling, and enactment fields require confirmation.
- Canastota compares a police Year 1 step at $23.91 per hour with a code-enforcement rate at $24.82. The non-safety rate is $0.91 higher. This counterexample shows why schedule location matters: an entry police step can sit below a non-safety professional rate even when other non-safety positions in the same salary schedule are lower.
- Alburtis compares the Chief of Police with an administrative assistant. The large arithmetic difference is not a bargaining-unit comparison because the ordinance places the Chief outside the police bargaining unit. It belongs in limits/appendix discussion as evidence that rank and bargaining status can mechanically inflate a nominal safety/non-safety contrast.

## Operational comparison method

Each pair should pass these gates in order:

1. Same municipality and overlapping effective cycle.
2. Same pay basis, or a document-supported conversion with hours recorded.
3. Base-to-base or like-for-like non-base component.
4. Same schedule location: entry-to-entry, step-to-same-step, or max-to-max.
5. Comparable employment status: full-time to full-time or part-time to part-time.
6. Explicit occupation and role family; command ranks remain separate from line positions.
7. Comparable rank, tenure, grade, or an explicit blocker.
8. Same legal status: actual rate to actual rate, or authorized ceiling to authorized ceiling.
9. Comparable bargaining-unit status.
10. Candidate-level exact-span/source validation before analytic use.

Police and fire positions need not be substantively identical to clerical, public-works, or other non-safety positions. The design requires a disciplined cross-occupation comparison within city-cycle. The appropriate estimand is therefore the difference between aligned schedule locations—not an assertion that the jobs have identical duties. Entry police should be compared with entry non-safety positions, maximum patrol rates with maximum non-safety rates, and command roles with separately defined managerial strata.

## Readiness result

- 27 pairs pass all four upstream coded compatibility flags and are appropriate for bounded candidate review.
- 87 pairs have moderate normalization quality and require one or more manual field repairs.
- 189 pairs remain not ready because one or more core comparison dimensions are unresolved.
- The focused rescue produced 185 near-gap-ready records and 11 records ready for matching.
- Candidate-level validation currently yields one PI-usable bounded comparison and three conditional comparisons.

The data therefore support bounded local statements now, but not a pooled wage-gap estimate. The immediate optimization target is the 27 structurally compatible pairs, screened against exact role and schedule-position text, followed by the 185 near-gap-ready records with explicit blocker repair.
