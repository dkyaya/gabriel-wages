# Bounded Wage-Differential and Mechanism Integration Audit

## What the four candidates show about wage setting

**Shreve: ordinance-based base-rate differentiation.** The same ordinance places part-time police at $22.00 per hour and a part-time utility clerk at $16.00. The observed difference is tied directly to the municipality's salary schedule. It is evidence of an outcome-side wage-setting decision that favors the safety position. The document does not isolate whether bargaining power, recruitment pressure, duties, or political preference explains the decision.

**Cammack Village: authorization ceilings can widen the available pay range.** The ordinance lists a $25.00 maximum for a part-time patrolman and $20.00 for an administrative assistant. The safety-side ceiling is 25 percent higher. This is a gap-widening authorization mechanism if the municipality uses the ceilings similarly, but actual pay can be lower and the packet's enactment requires confirmation.

**Canastota: schedule location and occupational choice can reverse direction.** The police Year 1 rate is $23.91, below the $24.82 code-enforcement rate. This is current non-safety-advantage documentary evidence for the selected positions. It also demonstrates that a safety/non-safety conclusion changes when the non-safety comparator and schedule location change. Entry-to-entry or like-tenure matching is needed before treating it as an occupational gap.

**Alburtis: command rank and bargaining status can create a misleading large contrast.** The Chief's $33.57 rate is far above the administrative-assistant rate, but the Chief was outside the police bargaining unit. The nominal difference therefore combines occupation, command rank, and bargaining status. It is useful as a warning about gap construction, not as evidence of a bargaining-unit safety premium.

## Mechanism implication

The four cases show three distinct pathways:

1. A salary ordinance can assign different base rates across occupations within the same cycle.
2. Authorized maxima can create different wage ceilings even before actual wages are observed.
3. Rank, step, role, and bargaining status can mechanically change the sign or size of a comparison.

The cases do not establish a single universal safety premium. They show that local wage differentials exist in both directions and that the observed sign is partly a product of wage-setting architecture. This is precisely why mechanism analysis and schedule-location matching must accompany any later pooled estimate.
