Marietta City Council moves forward on labor negotiation
By Janelle Patterson February 22, 2018 3 min read
Trending
MARIETTA -- Marietta City Council voted unanimously to move on to the next step in labor negotiations with the city's firefighters Tuesday.
According to state law, safety service providers, i.e. police and fire, are unable to strike in protest of labor contracts because it is illegal to disrupt safety-service.
Instead, a negotiation sequence is set up by the State Employment Relations Board to allow for a new contract to be formed between bargaining units every one to three years.
The process, which can end at any point if the city and bargaining unit come to an agreement, begins first with negotiations in-house, then if an impasse is reached a mediator is brought in.
"A mediator can go back and forth between the two and make non-binding suggestions," explained Safety-Service Director Jonathan Hupp.
Then if an agreement cannot be reached during mediation a fact-finder is sent by the State Employment Relations Board to hear proposals from both parties before making a recommendation.
Council voted Tuesday to not accept that recommendation, moving the process on to the appointment of a conciliator. If city administration and the Internation Association of Firefighters #442 cannot come to an agreement before pitching their sides to the conciliator, that individual will make the final decision on each issue in question between the two parties.
The issues noted in the fact finder report include dispute on pay rates, wages and step increases, additional compensation for added certifications, longevity pay, vacation and sick leave pay rates, health insurance contributions and coverage, and uniform purchase allowances.
"The conciliator is only there to listen to both positions on each issue and item by item pick one," said Hupp. "You go into a meeting with a fact finder expecting to win some and lose some, then the report, if accepted becomes the groundwork in its entirety for the next contract. (But) this governing body voted to refuse his report."
The report leans more to the demands of the IAFF for pay rate increases over the duration of the contract, lowered rates for probationary firefighters and other notations on issues of insurance and allowances, which Hupp said do not take into account the bigger financial picture of the city's coffers.
"In the end, these are still my guys and we're trying to take care of each of the city's employees," said Hupp. "And at any point, if we can work this out before SERB sends a conciliator then we don't need the state's further involvement."
IAFF had reportedly accepted the recommendation, according to Hupp, so both responses will be sent back to SERB by next Wednesday. Hupp could not give a timeline for when a conciliator could expect to be selected.
