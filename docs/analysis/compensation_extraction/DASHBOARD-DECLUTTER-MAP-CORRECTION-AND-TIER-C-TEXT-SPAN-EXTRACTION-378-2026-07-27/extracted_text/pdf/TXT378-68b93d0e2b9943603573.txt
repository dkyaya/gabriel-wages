PROPOSED AMERICAN RESCUE PLAN ACT (ARPA) GRANT AWARDS - Resolution
authorizing the disbursement of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding grant awards to
eligible not-for-profit organizations.
Charles B. Strome, III, City Manager, provided an overview of the application process for not-for-profit
organizations and small businesses eligible in the first tranch of the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA)
grant awards. He addressed the outreach efforts which included town hall meetings and community leader,
as well as the process for reviewing applications received by the Director of Community Engagement.
Those not-for-profit grant applications that were denied due to ineligibility (if portions of the applications
were unacceptable) may be resubmitted in the second tranch. Few applicants received the total sum for
which they applied. Some decisions were based on eligibility; and some applicants may be granted a
partial amount and may reapply at a later date - to be carefully reviewed by the Federal Government. Notfor-profits can be continued in the second tranch. He said that certain Capital projects may be funded with
ARPA money.
Additional information will be provided at a future date in order to permit further review by City Council.
Charles Curtis, Director of Community Engagement, explained the general application process. Questions
and responses followed.
Subject item was held pending further review.
City Manager Strome stated that two Members of City Council (Council Members Tarantino and Hyden),
owners of small businesses in the
Downtown area, will recuse themselves from discussions as they relate to small businesses.
Council Members Tarantino and Hyden recused themselves from the following discussion.
Rita Azrelyant, Assistant to the City Manager, addressed the eligibility of small businesses and specific
instructions, as certain criteria must be met.
The loss of revenue during the pandemic years 2019 and 2020--financial impact--must be proven, and tax
returns were required. Small businesses must prove financial hardship during Covid. Federal regulations
apply, and the Federal Government determines eligibility. Certain items included in applications were
ineligible for funding; and those businesses initially deemed ineligible in the first tranch may re-apply in
the second tranch provided eligibility is proven.
Further information will be provided regarding specific applications.

October 18, 2022

PROPOSED LEGISLATION RE:
CIRCUIT SHUTTLE EXTENSION
AGREEMENT FROM OCTOBER 31, 2022, THROUGH DECEMBER 31,
2022 - Resolution authorizing the City Manager to execute an extension of the
contract with the Circuit for the period October 31, 2022 to December 31, 2022 in
the amount of $110,934.34.

INTRODUCTION
Originally, CircuitNR was intended to circulate residents around the Downtown to facilitate lastmile transport to and from the train station and other services. In 2020, with the onset of COVID,
the City funded CircuitNR with Cares Act funds to facilitate access for the City’s most
vulnerable population - the elderly, the disabled and low-income individuals and families to
testing sites and vaccine appointments. The expansion increased from three (3) to six (6)
vehicles and service was expanded to transport residents to and from Covid related services as
well as medical appointments and essential services such as pharmacies and supermarkets
throughout the City. Starting September 6th, they initiated a pilot service to Parkside Place
Apartments. These funds will enable the City to continue the service through December 2022.
RECOMMENDATION
The Department of Development is requesting the City Council authorize the City Manager to
execute an extension of the contract with the Circuit for the period October 31, 2022 to
December 31, 2022 in the amount of $110,934.34. The funding should be charged to account:
CD19-41707-2708.

greenhouse wings, offices, a lobby and storage in Hudson Park (the “Greenhouse Facility”); and
WHEREAS, the Hudson Park Children’s Greenhouse, Inc. (HPCG) is desirous of
obtaining operational control over the Greenhouse Facility for the purpose of managing yearround programs and activities geared toward young children to be offered at the Greenhouse

over the Greenhouse Facility to the HPCG entity; and be it further
RESOLVED, that the City Manager is authorized to execute a license agreement with
Hudson Park Children’s Greenhouse, Inc., under the terms and conditions agreeable to the City
Manager and approved by the Corporation Counsel/Deputy City Manager.

CONSOLIDATION STUDY - Resolution approving participation in a study
finance by the County of Westchester relative to consolidation of the New
Rochelle, Larchmont, Pelham and Pelham Manor Fire Departments.

The City of New Rochelle agrees to participate in a study financed by the County of Westchester
regarding consolidation of the New Rochelle, Larchmont, Pelham, and Pelham Manor Fire
Departments. The goal of the study is to seek ways to provide these emergency services in a
more financially sound way that shares existing resources, and is safer for firefighting personnel
and the communities they serve. The County would request that the consultant provide
alternative proposals for consideration and not be locked into one concept.
It is understood that by agreeing to be part of the County-financed study and provide information
as requested, the City of New Rochelle (or its Fire Department) is not bound to follow any
recommendations made by the study in any way.

and amending Section 133-1, Enumeration of Fees, of the Code of the City of
New Rochelle. (Intro. 10/18/22; Public Hearing
).

Introduction:
New zoning for the Pelham Road corridor is currently under review as a result of a petition
submitted by MCRT Investments, LLC. The zoning as amended by staff includes requirements
for payments into the City’s Waterfront Access Fund.
The Waterfront Access Fund was established in 2016 as a result of the Watermark Pointe
rezoning on Davenport Neck in the R1-WF-10 district. As noted in the resolution (No. 201 of
2016), the fund pertains to development projects that are adjacent to the City’s waterfront. As
discussed below, staff recommends actions to expand the scope of the Waterfront Access Fund,
including its renaming to the Coastal Resiliency and Waterfront Management Fund to better
reflect it potential uses.
Recommendation:
Staff recommends that Council adopt a set of resolutions to expand the scope of projects where
this fee could apply, such that properties that are not directly on the water, but benefit from
certain zoning incentives, can contribute to this fund where appropriate. This should include the
area of the proposed Pelham Corridor Waterfront Development Floating zone, which covers the
seaward side of Pelham Road from Water Street to the municipal boundary with the Village of
Pelham Manor, as well as the Downtown Overlay Zone.
The potential use of the funds should be defined as well. The proposed zoning prepared by staff
stipulates the following, amended to reflect the renaming of the fund:
Fees shall be used for funding waterfront public access amenities; local shuttle
services to access City beached and parks; improvements to City-owned

17

waterfront parks and marina; provision of public parking for public amenities;
dredging and other regenerative activities associated with waterway access and
management or any other activity specified by the Coastal Resiliency and
Waterfront Management Fund.
Additionally, staff recommends corresponding changes to Chapter 331 Zoning and
Chapter 133 Fees.
Staff recommends that Council set a public hearing on the proposed amendments and refer
same to the Planning Board, and Westchester County Planning Board. It is also
recommended that the City Council declare itself Lead Agency for the purposes of an
environmental review pursuant to the State Environmental Quality Review Act (SEQRA).
Finally, staff recommends that the Council approve this amendment pending the outcome
of the public hearing and responses from the City’s Planning Board, and the Westchester
County Planning Board.
