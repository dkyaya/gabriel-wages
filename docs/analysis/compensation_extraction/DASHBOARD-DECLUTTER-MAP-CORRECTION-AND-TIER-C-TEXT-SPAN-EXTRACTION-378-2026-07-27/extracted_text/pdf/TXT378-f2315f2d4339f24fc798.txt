PROPOSED LOCAL LAW, INTRO. NO. 1, RE: COLD WAR VETERANS'
EXEMPTION Local Law No._____ A local law, Intro No. _1_, to amend Article
IX, Cold War Veterans Exemption, Section 288-30, Amount of exemption;
limitations, of Chapter 288, Taxation, of the Code of the City of New Rochelle
relative to increasing veterans exemptions. (Intro. 4/12/22; Public Hearing
5/10/22)

Background:
New York State Real Property Tax Law (NYS RPT) section 458-b provides limited exemption
from real property taxes for real property owned by persons who rendered military service to the
United States during the cold war (defined as September 2, 1945, to December 26, 1991),
provided such property meets the requirements set forth in the law. This section was revised to
increase the limits of the Cold War Veterans Exemption.
Article IX Cold War Veterans Exemptions, Section 288-30, Limitation of Exemption, of Chapter
288, Taxation of the code of the City of New Rochelle states the limits as follows:
The Cold War Veteran’s Exemptions: 288-30 A.
An applicant receiving the Cold War Veteran’s Exemption receives the lesser of 15% of their
assessment or the capped exemption amount of $27,000, multiplied by the current equalization
rate (27,000 x .0234= 632AV).
Limitations would be increased the lesser of 15% of their assessment or the increased capped
amount of $75,000 multiplied by the current equalization rate (75,000 x .0234= 1,755 AV).
The Cold War Disabled Veteran’s Exemptions: 288-30 B.
An applicant receiving the Disabled Veteran’s Exemption receives the lesser of half of their
Veterans Disability Rating or the capped exemption amount of $90,000 multiplied by the current
equalization rate (90,000 x .0234= 2,106AV).
Limitations would be increased to the basic Alternative Veteran would receive the lesser
of half of their Veterans Disability Rating or the increased capped amount of $250,000
multiplied by the current equalization rate (250,000 x .0234= 5,850 AV).

PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO CHAPTER 9-99 OF THE CODE OF THE
CITY OF NEW ROCHELLE RE:
THE ECOLOGY AND NATURAL
RESOURCES ADVISORY COMMITTEE - Ordinance amending Article
XXVII, Ecology and Natural Resources Advisory Committee, Section 9-99,
Committee Established, of the Code of the City of New Rochelle.

Background:
Council established the Ecology and Natural Resources Advisory Committee (ENRAC) in 2019
to advise council and staff on matters relating to the ecological health, beauty and sustainability
of New Rochelle’s natural environment. The initial legislation called for the committee to
include nine members, though this was later amended to increase that figure to fifteen. Most
recently, Council merged ENRAC with the Energy Conservation Committee. The combined
committees now function as one under ENRAC. Committee members are appointed by the City
Manager.
Review:
ENRAC is an excellent resource for a variety of initiatives, and offers the ability to serve as a
committee to satisfy requirements for some of these. For example, the committee was recently
designated by council to serve as the City’s Climate Smart Community Task Force, which will
enable to New Rochelle to further its climate change efforts through a state program.
Other initiatives are on the horizon that will require ENRAC’s assistance, and it will be
important to ensure that the subcommittees that are formed satisfactorily address those that are
most vulnerable to climate change and environmental injustice.
Recommendations:
To address the concern noted above, staff recommends that Council amend the code once again
as it pertains to ENRAC to increase the allowable membership to eighteen. Further, it is
recommended that Council direct the City Manager to fill these new positions with
recommendations from African American Advisory Committee, Hispanic American Advisory
Committee and the Youth Bureau.

TASK 2: UPDATED ANNUAL GHG INVENTORY
AKRF will prepare two annual emissions GHG inventories for 2019 and 2020 based on the two accounting
approaches performed for the 2018 Annual GHG Inventory: a direct approach and a consumption-based
approach. The direct approach presents emissions at the location from which they are emitted and allocated
to the City of New Rochelle where the activity occurs. The consumption-based approach associates the
emissions with the activity or consumption leading to the emissions and allocates them to the location where
the consumption activity occurred. For the consumption-based approach, emissions associated with energy
production and transport, i.e. energy-cycle emissions, will also be included.
Emissions will be included for the three sectors: Residential & Commercial Buildings, Transportation, and
Residential & Commercial Waste. Any additional information identified in the project-kick off meeting
will be incorporated into the calculation spreadsheet.
TASK 3: ANNUAL NARRATIVE REPORTS
AKRF will prepare two annual narrative reports to present the results of the annual GHG inventories for
both the 2019 and 2020 years. Emissions will be presented from both accounting approaches included in
the 2018 report and inventory. The reports will discuss the consistency with the City’s sustainability goals
specified in the GreeNR Sustainability Plan and any subsequent updates and will include currently planned
projects that are anticipated to impact future annual GHG inventories.
TASK 3: CLEARPATH DATA INTEGRATION
AKRF will transfer the GHG emissions information prepared under Task 2 into the ClearPath Tool under
the account for the City of New Rochelle. This task will be performed using the final annual GHG emissions
inventories for 2019 and 2020.

Assem-

Section 1. Legislative findings and intent.
The legislature finds
that there is a severe shortage of parking for residents of the City of
New Rochelle in the residential area of Sutton Manor. The residents
living in the Sutton Manor neighborhood are suffering interference to
the tranquility and quiet enjoyment of their neighborhood by reason of
visitors and users of the nearby New Rochelle Station, by shoppers,
diners and users of New Rochelle's downtown area, and by owners of
commercial vehicles from the villages, towns, boroughs and cities neighboring Sutton Manor. Each of the foregoing individuals park in the
Sutton Manor as a free and convenient alternative to paid, permit parking in and around New Rochelle and its surroundings. This use has caused
significant safety issues, refuse and sanitation problems, traffic
hazards, congestion, air and noise pollution and other disturbances
which cumulatively have severely deteriorated the peace, tranquility and
quality of life of the residents of this neighborhood. The legislature,
therefore, hereby declares the necessity of this act to authorize the
City of New Rochelle in the county of Westchester to adopt a residential
parking permit system in accordance with the provisions of this act.
§ 2. The vehicle and traffic law is amended by adding a new section
1640-r to read as follows:
§ 1640-r. Residential parking system in the city of New Rochelle. 1.
Notwithstanding the provisions of any law to the contrary, the city
council of the city of New Rochelle may, by adoption of a local law or
ordinance, provide for a residential parking permit system and fix and
require the payment of fees applicable to parking within the area in
which such parking system is in effect in accordance with the provisions
of this section.
