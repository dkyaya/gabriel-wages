Explanatory Statement - (continued)
1-The failed EnCap project and tax appeals:
The most important policy of the Township of Lyndhurst Board of Commissioners is to continue to stabilize property taxes.
The net 2016 Budget is currently established at $39,475,425.50, with the 2015 Budget established at $38,667,757.15; an increase of $807,668, 2.1% decrease.
This net 2016 budget increase is due solely to tax refunds generated by the tax assessor's office, $850,000. The tax assessor works for the Bergen County Board of Taxation which is supervisied by the State of New Jersey Division of Taxation.

If these tax refunds did NOT occur no net 2016 Budget or Tax increase would be necessary.
We continue to face the task of financing the failed EnCap project. In summary, this failed project had two serious, negative consequences on Lyndhurst.
First, the Township lost $121,000,000 in assessed value (tax base). This is $2,252,415 annually (in 2012 tax dollars).
Second, the Township was forced to refund prior years taxes paid, and more importantly, we did not receive tax payments from the NJMC, when the NJMD took over the EnCap property.
All of this adds up to the following payments in the 2016 Budget.
EnCap:
Total Original Direct EnCap Debt
$16,971,779
Excludes Interest
2010 Tax Appeal Refunding Ordinance
$3,750,000
Two payments Remaining: 2016 and 2017
Total
$20,721,779
Amount to be paid in 2016
$2,037,357
Excludes Interest
Accordingly, eight (8) tax points in 2016 are dedicated to pay for the EnCap and related issues. In addition, several large commercial properties have appealed their tax assessments.
2-The Lincoln School Redevelopment:
On March 8, 2016 the Lyndhurst Board of Education and Lyndhurst Board of Commissioners adopted a resolution and signed an interlocal services agreement enabling the redevelopment of the Lincoln School.
This school was deemed inappropriate for school use in the 1961 Master Plan. The Township is acquiring the school for replacement value. A replacement school will be built on school property at Matera Field.
So as to finance the acquisition of the Lincoln School and redevelop this important property the 2016 budget includes a $2,500,000 appropriation for the down payment on the bond ordinance for the construction of the replacement school.

The total (or gross) 2016 Budget is therefore $41,989,675.50. However, the township has accumulated surplus of $2,500,000 to avoid any taxpayer impact from this investment in the Lyndhurst schools..

The Lyndhurst Board of Commissioners presents the following Capital Improvement Program showing the planned, preliminary capital project listing for the next six years, including
2016, with a focus on road reconstruction. It must be stressed that this Capital Improvement Program does NOT confer authority to spend any funds but rather oulines a listing of
upcoming capital needs. Due to the EnCap meadowlands development debacle, caused by the New Jersey Meadowlands Commission (NJMC) and as indicated on Sheet 3 (d) of
this budget, the Township is funding $2,036,357 in principal payments in 2016 to pay down the December 31, 2015 outstanding debt of $18,404,422. This initially caused the
Township's financial rating to be dramatically reduced. However, due to the implementation of strict budgetary controls and other financial procedures our financial rating has
rebounded and is now the "best ever". The year 2014 was the third consecutive year that no bond ordinances (new debt) was authorized. The Township had an increase in capital
project authorizations in 2015 so that certain roads in need of resurfacing may be improved. Further, the Township has implemented a "pay as we go" capital financing plan which
places the approval of certain capital projects directly in this operating budget. We are continuing this process for most equipment acquisition and facilities projects in 2016 and the
future. However, because of the need for street and road improvements some debt needs to be authorized for the most significant projects. This capital plan also anticipates the
financing of the redevelopment (replacement) of the Lincoln School. Finally, a fire apparatus is scheduled to be purchased in late 2016 or early 2017.
