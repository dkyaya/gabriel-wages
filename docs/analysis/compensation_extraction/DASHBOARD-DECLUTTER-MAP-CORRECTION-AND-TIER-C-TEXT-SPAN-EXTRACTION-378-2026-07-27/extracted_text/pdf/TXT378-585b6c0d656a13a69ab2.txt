2013 Capital Improvement Projects
4. To: City Council
When we met at the City Council work session at the end of December of 2012, a number of capital
improvements projects for the 2013 calendar year were discussed in some detail. Several of these
projects need to be advanced as soon as possible in order to take advantage of early season bidding
that will result in the City receiving more favorable bid prices on these projects. Having these projects
ready to bid in the near future will result in allowing the City to do more with less.
So if the City is going to advance a paving project other than the 2nd Ward Community Development
Block Grant (CDBG) Paving Project, we need to identify the streets that you wish to pave in each ward
as soon as possible. Construction cost estimates and a listing of streets in each Ward that need to be
paved were previously provided to you. As A group, I hope collectively we can have some serious
discussion on repaving South Park and Electric Avenues as they are in deplorable condition. This would
be in addition to streets that need to be paved in the other Wards. There also can be savings realized
in making the paving project one very large project, including the CDBG Paving Project as there are
economies of scale.

6. To: City Council
Earlier this year, on February 14, 2013, the Mayor requested the Council’s guidance on the how

Mayor’s office and the Council merely approves or vetoes, the Council looked forward to
working directly with the Mayor to solve these issues.

Mayor’s office, to install a garbage tote system in an inter-municipal agreement with the Town

impression that we had come to an agreement to float a bond to resolve these pending issues
before they became larger dilemmas.

publicly stated that he expects the city to purchase and install the tote system this year. If that is
the case, why was this not included in the Mayor’s budget proposal for the coming fiscal year?
How does the Mayor expect to fund these major projects? Has the Mayor deliberately misled
this council as well as the general public with his intentions? If not, how can he explain this
omission in his budget proposal?

we are requesting the Mayor’s presence to answer these questions.

3. Eviction for Illegal Drug Activity Ordinance
AN ORDINANCE ENACTING CHAPTER 160 OF THE CODE OF THE CITY OF
LACKAWANNA ENTITLED EVICTION FOR ILLEGAL DRUG ACTIVITY
BE IT ENACTED by the City Council of the City of Lackawanna, New York as follows:
Chapter 160. EVICTION FOR ILLEGAL DRUG ACTIVITY.
160.1 DEFINITIONS.
As used in this division, the following terms shall have the meanings indicated.
Premises: means a building or structure, or any part thereof.
Owner: means the landlord of the premises, including his/her agent.
Tenant: in addition to its ordinary meaning, shall include an occupant of one or more rooms in a
rooming house or a resident, not including a transient occupant, of one or more rooms
in a hotel who has been in possession for 30 consecutive days or longer.
Illegal Drug Activity: means use or possession of a controlled substance or marihuana
[marijuana], as defined by the New York State Penal Law.
Criminal Conviction: means the entry of a plea of guilty to or a verdict for one or more counts
set forth in an accusatory instrument.
160.2: EVICTION FOR USE OR OCCUPATION OF PREMISES FOR ILLEGAL DRUG
ACTIVITY.
A special proceeding to evict a tenant from leased premises may be maintained upon the
ground that the premises, or any part thereof, have been used occupied for the purpose of illegal
activity.
160.3: PROCEDURES.
The procedures applicable to summary proceedings to recover possession of real property
under State Law shall be applicable top any proceeding brought under this chapter.
160.4: SERVICE OF NOTICE, COMMENCEMENT OF EVICTION PROCEEDINGS.
A. The following persons, corporations and agencies may serve personally upon the
owner or landlord, or upon his agent, of demised real property used or occupied, in whole or in
part, for the use or possession of illegal drugs, a written notice requiring the owner or landlord to
make an application for the removal of the person(s) so using or occupying the premises:
1. Any domestic corporation organized for the suppression of vice, subject to, or which submits
visitation by the New York State Department of Social Service or the Erie County
Department of Social Services, and possess a certification from such department of such fact
and of such conformity with regulations of the department.
2. Any duly authorized enforcement agency of New York State, or a subdivision thereof, and/or
the City of Lackawanna, under a duty to enforce the provisions of the Penal Law or of any
State or Local Law, ordinance, code, rule or regulation relating to buildings.

September 3, 2013

95

B. If the owner or landlord, or his agent, does not make such application within five days
thereafter, or having made it, does not in good faith diligently prosecute it, the person,
corporation or enforcement agency giving notice may bring a proceeding under this chapter for
such removal as though the petitioner were the owner or the landlord of the premises, and shall
have precedence over any similar proceeding thereafter brought by such owner or landlord or to
the one theretofore brought by him and not prosecuted diligently and in good faith. Proof of all
the ill repute of the demised premises, or of the occupants thereof, or of those resorting thereto,
shall constitute presumptive evidence of the unlawful use of the demised premises required to be
started in the petition for removal. Both the person(s) in possession of the property and the
owner or landlord shall be made respondents in the proceeding.
160.5: MULTIPLE CONVICTIONS CONSTITUTE PRESUMPTIVE EVIDENCE.
Two or more convictions of any person or persons had, within a period of one year for
any of the offenses defined in Article 220 or 221 of the Penal Law, arising out of conduct
engaged in the same real property consisting of a dwelling as that term is defined in Subdivision
4 of the New York State Multiple Dwelling Law, shall be presumptive evidence of conduct
constituting use and occupancy of the premises for the purpose of using and possessing illegal
drugs and of the tenant knowledge thereof.
160.6: CIVIL PENALTIES.
A court granting a petition pursuant to this section may, in addition to any other order
provided by law, make an order imposing and requiring the payment by the respondent of a civil
penalty not exceeding five-thousand dollars ($5,000.00) to the City of Lackawanna, in which
subject premises is located, and the payment of reasonable attorneys’ fees and the cost of the
proceeding to the petitioner. In any case, multiple respondents shall be jointly and severally
liable for any payment so ordered, and the amounts of such payments constitute a lien upon the
subject real property.
THIS ORDINANCE SHALL TAKE EFFECT IMMEDIATELY
Moved by Pirowski seconded by Iafallo to adopt ordinance enacting Chapter 160 of the
Municipal Code regarding Illegal Drug Activity
Yeas: Noman, Iafallo, Jerge, Lewis, Pirowski
Carried 5-0

Newborn Screening
25. To: City Council
Here is a letter I sent to Senators Schumer, Gillibrand and Rep. HigginsAs your constituent, a member of the Lackawanna City Council, and someone who values the
health of our nation's children, I am writing to request that you cosponsor and support the
passage of the Newborn Screening Saves Lives Reauthorization Act.
Over the past fifty years, practically every one of the 4 million babies born each year in the U.S.
has undergone newborn screening. Throughout this time, the number of diseases included in this
well-established public health system has grown exponentially.
For these diseases, early detection through newborn screening is crucial to preventing
irreversible consequences such as mental and physical disabilities, even death. Newborn
screening allows for prompt diagnosis and treatment of diseases that would not otherwise be
recognized at birth, giving our nation's children the best chance for a healthy life that they all
deserve.
Since the passage of the Newborn Screening Saves Lives Act in 2008, there has been significant
progress in establishing national newborn screening standards and ensuring that all states have
the capacity for a comprehensive screening program -- many states have more than tripled the
number of diseases screened for at birth.
Although progress has been made, there is more work to be done. The Newborn Screening
Saves Lives Reauthorization Act will continue this important work and extend federal assistance
to help states expand and improve their newborn screening programs, saving more of our
children from needlessly suffering from a treatable disease.
I very much appreciate your consideration.
For more information about Newborn Screening, please visit www.huntershope.org

Resolution
WHEREAS, the former Friendship House, located at 264 Ridge Road, was condemned by the
City of Lackawanna Office of Code Enforcement division in 2007, and
furthermore is unsightly and not occupied, and
WHEREAS, this building has been deemed a public nuisance; and has been neglected,
abandoned, and looted, creating an unsafe condition for the residents of the City
of Lackawanna, and
WHEREAS, the cost estimate of refurbishing the building, from a 2008-2009 study states that
the cost would be between $5,119,000 and $6,704,000. Since this study, the
building has experienced typical Buffalo area winters with no heat and further
looting of any valuable metals, thereby adding more value to the cost of
refurbishing, and
WHEREAS, this abandoned blight is sitting on 5 acres of developable property. With all the
movement on the old Steel Plant property, this property is receiving numerous
calls of interest looking for business.
NOW THEREFORE, BE IT
RESOLVED, the Lackawanna City Council hereby officially declares the former Friendship
House, located at 264 Ridge Road to be an area of spot blight, and be it further
RESOLVED, that all necessary steps be taken to alleviate said area of spot blight, including
submission of a grant application to the Erie County Block Grant Consortium for
the demolition of the Friendship House and the restoration of the immediate
environs.
RESOLVED, that this resolution shall take effect immediately.
