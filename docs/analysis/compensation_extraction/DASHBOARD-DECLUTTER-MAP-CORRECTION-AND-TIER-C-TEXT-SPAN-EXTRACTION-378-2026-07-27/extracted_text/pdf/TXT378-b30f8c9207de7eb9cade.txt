CITY OF EASLEY, SOUTH CAROLINA

April 8, 2019
The Honorable Larry D. Bagwell
Members of City Council
City of Easley, South Carolina
Dear Mayor Bagwell and Members of City Council:
I am pleased to present to the citizens of Easley, members of City Council, City employees, and other
interested readers the 2019-20 operating budget for the City of Easley, South Carolina.
The projected revenues for all funds are $17,843,284 including a General Fund budget of $14,505,784. This
represents an overall budgetary increase of 2.5% and a General Fund increase of 4.4% over the adopted
2018-19 operating budget, respectively. A summary of all funds outlining the changes is included at the end
of the Executive Summary.
The City of Easley completed a Strategic Plan in 2018 and it is serving as guide the future growth and direction
of the city. The 2019-20 Budget addresses three (3) Themes that council have identified during this process:
• Quality Services
• Quality Places
• Quality Community
The FY 18-19 budget period was the start to a transformative period for the citizens, businesses, and
organizations of Easley. The City of Easley completed the funding of three (3) Bond issuances and have
either implemented projects from these bonds or in the design process. The projects funded through these
bonds will continue through the FY 19-20 budget.
Tax Increment Finance Bond
The City of Easley was able to partner with Pickens County and the School District of Pickens County to
extend the Downtown Tax Increment Finance District for ten (10) additional years. The bond tied to the
extension, and fund balance from the expiring bond payments, provided the City with approximately $1.8
million to invest in the Downtown. Several projects have been completed to date. These include
improvements to the public parking lots, replacement of downtown trees, placement of benches and trash
receptacles, and stage covering for the amphitheater. We also recently closed on the silos on Folger Street
and are working on their redevelopment. There are several additional projects that will be completed during
FY 19-20. These include the railroad fencing, completion of City Green/Doodle trailhead, renovations for
Westend Hall, and new wayfinding signage.
General Obligation Bond
The City of Easley has experienced growth of almost 40% over the last 25 years and is anticipating an
additional 10% growth over the next 5 years. This has added demand to service delivery and created
challenges to delivering quality services. Fire Station #1 and the Public Works facilities have both aged,
maxed out space requirements, and are located in areas that provide challenges to service delivery. Council
has approved DP3 Architects as the architects and J Davis Construction as the Construction Manager at Risk
on the project. The schedule shows construction starting in May 2019 and completion in late Spring 2020.
The City issued $5.5 million in General Obligation Bonds in FY 18-19 for the construction of a new Operations
and Maintenance Facility and Renovation/Addition to Fire Station #1. The Operations and Maintenance
Facility will be located on property we own next to J.B. “Red” Owens Park. It will house Public Works and
Parks and Recreation. This facility will provide ample space of our current operations and provide room for

growth over the next few decades. The City leveraged 3 mills of the tax rate for the bond issuance. These 3
mills equate to over $210,000 in additional revenue and covers our GO Bond payment.
One additional result of a new Operations and Maintenance Facility is that over 12 acres of land in downtown
will become available for development. The City has issued a Request for Proposals for the redevelopment

Hospitality Bond
The City of Easley completed a Parks and Recreation Master Plan in 2017 and is now looking at the
implementation of the plan’s recommendations. The top priorities that were identified in the plan were to
update/improve the current facilities, increase the amount of passive recreation throughout the City, and
improve the maintenance facilities.
Projects like the Brushy Creek Greenway and Nalley Brown Nature Preserve are in progress to create
additional passive recreation. The parks and recreation maintenance will also be part of the new Operations
and Maintenance Facility being designed. Renovations to the current parks are being designed and should
begin shortly. All of these projects will continue into the FY 19-20 budget. Currently, there will be over
$1,000,000 of funds left from the Bond, and Council and Staff will discuss future projects for these funds.
There are indeed exciting projects underway and opportunities on the horizon, but we are also tasked with
providing quality services day to day. This requires us to address the major aspects of our operations Employees and Equipment.
Employees
Employees are the backbone of the City and help to dictate the quality of services provided to our residents.
One of the goals in this budget is to ensure that the City can compete for the highest quality of candidates,
and then continue to keep them. To this end, the 2019-20 budget includes:
• A 2% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for all full and part-time employees effective July 1, 2019.
All pay bands will be increased to reflect.
• Continues with the State Health Plan to keep the City’s and employees cost lower than in previous
years.
• Absorbing the proposed 1% increase to the employer’s contribution portion of the State Retirement
System.
• Continues our Police and Fire pay bands to help to ensure that we keep the highest trained and
tenured employee at the City.
There were two topics that need to be discussed in the future:
• A City-wide longevity policy and corresponding one-time bonus or pay increase to recognize those
that dedicate their career to the City.
• An education reimbursement policy to help employees that seek to further their education by
covering a portion of their costs.
It is recommended that we implement an Education Reimbursement Program for all City employees.
Employee compensation for completion of a degree program would also be part of this program but would
only be for those that are not covered by a pay scale. The goal is to have this drafted and presented to
Council by the end of 2019 for review and input. The longevity compensation is planned to be drafted as well
and them be implemented over the next 2-3 budget years if Council approves. The longevity program would
only be for none pay scale employees. These programs would allow all employees to have chances to move
within their pay scales.

Health Insurance / Wellness
In January 1, 2017 the City moved to the State Health Insurance Plan through SC PEBA. This has improved
the benefits offered to employees and provided an even larger savings to the City in the long run. The City is
looking at a .05% increase in January 2020. This is a negligible increase and pales in comparison to the 2030% increases we were looking at before changing.
The budget recommends continuing funding the gym membership reimbursement initiative for full-time
employees wishing to participate. The plan states that any full-time employee that visits a participating gym
(as approved by the wellness committee) 36 times a quarter (January – March, April – June, July – September,
and October – December) will receive up to $75 per quarter depending on the gym they chose. $7,500 is
budgeted for this initiative.
The City is continuing to see the benefit of an on-site Nurse Practitioner 8 hours a week. This partnership with
Greenville Hospital System has provided the basis for our wellness screenings and counseling. Employees
have someone on site to visit for minor ailments, and this has saved numerous lost hours of productivity. They
also provide flu shots and annual screenings for employees.
New Positions
The FY 19-20 budget requests included request for 15 additional Full-time Employees:
• Public Works – 4 Full-time Employees
• Fire Department – 4 Full-time Employees
• Police Department – 2 Full-time Employees
• Parks and Recreation – 4 Full-time Employees
• Codes and Enforcement – 1 Full-time Employee
The City does not have the funding to increase staffing for all of these requests. The FY 19-20 Budget does
recommend the addition of 4 Full-time employee positions in 3 departments. It is recommended that the Police
receive 2 Full-time police officer positions to allow them to restructure and handle their increased call volume
during peak times. It is also recommended that Public Works and Parks and Recreation each receive 1
additional Full-time employee to handle the increased demand added to these departments.
There are 2 additional positions that were tough to leave out, but funding could not be found for them. If
funding is available at a later date, it would be recommended to fund the additional Building Inspector positions
and a Fire Training Officer.
Equipment Needs
Police
The Police Department began an Operating Lease program for all of their vehicles in FY 18-19. The lease
program works on a 3-year replacement program to recover the highest available return on the sale of the
vehicles. 17 vehicles were on-boarded in FY 18-19 and 17 additional will be included in FY 19-20 for a total
of 34 to date.
The Police Department are in the process of changing out all body cameras to Axion. This is due to lawsuits
between Axion and our current contactor. The body cameras are being funded through the confiscated drug
fund and state reimbursement funds. The Police Department is also wanting to change in car cameras to
Axion so that the software and storage are the same for evidence retention and sharing. This is about a
$325,000 cost over a 5-year agreement. Funding of $115,000 was requested for 1/3 of the camera systems
but removed to balance the budget.

Fire Department
There is $575,000 in the FY 19-20 budget for the purchase of a new Fire Engine. This will replace a 1989
Custom Spartan Chassis Fire Engine. It is recommended that this be a 10-year lease purchase. There is a
$46,000/year lease purchase that will expire in FY 18-19 that will almost cover the payment amount of this
truck. They also have a $62,000 lease purchase payment expiring in FY 19-20. It was originally included in
the FY 18-19 Budget for purchase, but the delivery schedule for the truck was 15 months. This means that
the truck will be received in FY 19-20 for financing purposes
Due to an increased number of non-fire calls, the Fire Department is also in need of smaller vehicles to better
get staff to these calls. It is recommended that the Chief’s and Fire Marshall’s vehicles be assigned to stations
for calls, and then replaced on our operating lease program through Enterprise.
The Fire also has integral equipment about to expire. The Fire’s self-contained breathing apparatuses
(SCBAs) are coming up for expiration over the next few years. Their request for $325,000 to replace all SCBA
gear was reduced to $100,000, and we will stagger their replacement over 3 fiscal years. They are also
hoping to find grant or exterior funding to assist with this cost.
Public Works/Stormwater
Public Works is in need of frontline trucks to transport staff and equipment to projects sites. It is recommended
in the budget that the Administrative vehicles be pushed down to frontline and that we move their vehicles to
our operating lease program. This would include the Public Works Director, Assistant Director, and
Stormwater Director.
The budget also recommends the purchase of a pothole and crack seal machine. This will allow us to better
maintain our roads and extend their life. We will also begin taking maintenance of our roads back from the
County and need to better handle potholes and other minor maintenance.
Public Works also requested $113,500 to purchase a used boom truck that can be used for multiple projects
around the City. This piece of equipment was not funded in the budget.
Parks and Recreation
The Parks and Recreation budget reflects the same as Public Works and Fire related to vehicles. It is
recommended that 2 administrative level vehicles move to the Operating Lease program and their current
vehicles get pushed to front line vehicles.
Park and Facility improvements have been approved as part of the Hospitality Tax Funds. These have been
reviewed by Council after recommendation from the Parks and Recreation Committee.
Other item of interest
The budget includes:
• $135,000 for special events throughout the year including Part of Pendleton, Trunk or Treat, Fourth of
July Festivities, and others.
• $15,000 to replace aging Christmas Decorations in the Downtown
• $36,575 for tourism related activities and events throughout the community
• $130,000 for Stormwater system improvements throughout the community
• $30,000 to continue the demolition of derelict properties throughout the City.
I would like to take this opportunity to personally thank all the department leaders and their staffs for their
assistance in preparing this budget, and for the job they do to serve the citizens on Easley. I feel that this
budget provides the City an opportunity to set a positive path for the future while not over extending our
finances. It also provides better options to beatify, enhance, and restore pride in the community.

value of a property to determine the assessed value. Owner-occupied residences are assessed at 4%,
commercial properties at 6%, motor vehicles at 6%, personal property at 10%, and industrial and utilities at
10.5%. The Property tax rate for FY 19-20 is proposed to remain at 68 mills. The table below shows the tax

Local Option Sales Tax (LOST) is received monthly via a statement from the South Carolina Department of
Revenue for the 1% Local Option Sales Tax collected. 100% of the LOST revenues are used to defray
property taxes. The proposed property tax millage increase for FY 19-20 will result in a small increase to
property tax bills due to the increase in the discount provided by Local Option Sales Tax (LOST) decreasing.
The LOST multiplier decreased from .1826 to .1706 for FY 19-20. This reduces the amount of discount a
property owner receives. The LOST income is expected to account for 19% of the General Fund budget in
FY 19-20.

Licenses, permits and fees represent the City’s revenue source comprising approximately 23% of all General
Fund revenues. The most significant source is the business license tax, which a levy is based upon each
$1,000 of gross receipts, sales, or premiums of business conducted within the corporate limits of the City. The
projection of $3,347,500 in business license receipts (excluding franchise fees) is a 8% increase from FY 1819. This is based on an increase in Business License revenues and Insurance tax collected by the MASC
coming in higher than expected in FY 17-18. The Insurance collection for FY 17-18 was over $250,000 higher
than budgeted, but it was not known until the last few days of the Fiscal Year after we had adopted our FY 1819 budget. Business Licenses exceeded budget by close to $150,000 in the same period. Both of these items
are reflective of the increase in construction in the City and businesses that have open in the last few years.
Franchise fees comprise about 18% of all General Fund revenues. Revenues in this category include
franchise fees for electric, gas, and cable television. The rate structure is based upon a certain percentage of
gross receipts in each case, and the basis of each forecast is trend analysis along with current market
conditions. Projected revenues from all franchise fees for FY 19-20 are $2,603,500 and represents this
revenue source increasing about 2% from FY 18-19. The primary reason for the increase is revenue from
Easley Combined.

Intergovernmental revenue includes grants and allocations from county, state, and federal governments.
State-shared revenue is generally distributed on a pro-rata basis per population or other set formula. The Aid
to Subdivision revenue is distributed quarterly from the Local Government Fund and funded by a transfer of
4.5% of state general fund revenues. Counties receive 83.28% and municipalities 16.72% of the distribution.
The revenue forecast from Aid to Subdivisions is expected to be $472,500 for 2019-20. This reflects the city
receiving over $150,000 less than what is mandated by statute. The budget for the State has not been
completed, but this budget assumes that the State will fund at the House of Representatives budget level.
The State is in the process of setting new guidelines that will use this year as a new baseline for funding and
allow it to increase/decrease based on the State budget. The State does not plan to address the underfunding
from the past decade and will leave Easley underfunded by close to $2,000,000 during this time.
Other Revenues make up the remainder of the General Fund revenues. They consist of transfers from other
city funds. The City transfers a formula transfer from Accommodations Tax and $130,000 from Stormwater
to cover the cost of the Stormwater Engineer and support staff for this service.
The City is also budgeting for $575,000 in financing to cover the cost of a new fire engine that was approved
and ordered in FY 18-19.
There is also a Fines revenue source that is made up almost entirely of Court Fine and Fees. The number
used for Court Fines has been changed to reflect the City’s actual revenue. The State of South Carolina uses
the city’s court revenues to suggest that we are profiting from operating a municipal court. The City does not
get to keep all of these fees, as almost 60% of the revenue is earmarked to the State of South Carolina. We
are budgeted to make $400,000 after adjusting for what we send to the State, and the Court expenses are
budgeted at $366,000.

Funding Highlights
General
The City of Easley has seen growth in our Revenue sources over the last few years, and the trend appears to
be continuing in FY 18-19 and FY 19-20. The increase in the budget for FY 19-20 recognizes this growth and
puts it to use in addressing positions and capital needed to accommodate the growth and resulting demand.
Personnel Changes
This budget includes four (4) new positions throughout 3 departments in the City. There are 2 new police
officers, 1 Public Works Maintenance Worker, and 1 Parks and Recreation Maintenance Worker included in
the budget for FY 19-20. Even though there are 4 new positions in the budget there were requests for 15 total
positions.

Departmental Highlights
•

The General Government Budget includes $565,967 in funding for annual debt and lease purchase
payments. This is an increase of $215,000 from FY 18-19. This reflects the GO Bond that was
approved in FY 18-19 and that the City leveraged 3 mills of our tax revenue. The General Government
also has $140,000 in IT services as we continue to upgrade our systems and connect all city facilities
with fiber, phones, and security systems. The total cost for retiree insurance is also located under this
section to identify the true cost of out Post-Employment Benefits in the budget.

•

The Finance Department has $20,000 funded for our annual external audit.

•

The Court budget reflects the decision to move from 2 Full-time Judge positions to 2 Part-time Judges.
Some of the savings from this change will be used to fund a Part-time administrative position to assist
the Clerk of Court. The budget also assumes that the $25,000 for indigent defense that was an
unfunded mandate from the State in FY 15-16 will continue in FY 19-20.

•

The Fire Department budget includes $575,000 for the lease/purchase of a new Fire engine to replace
a 1988 Engine. They also have $25,000 in Equipment to purchase a new battery powered extrication
system (jaws of life). They also have $100,000 for the purchase of new SCBA gear.

•

In the Streets Division, $450,000 is funded for utilities such as street and stop lights. This has
decreased over the last few years as Easley Combined Utilities has continued to replace old halogen
lights with more energy efficient LED fixtures, but has level off the past couple of years. There is one
(1) Full-time position funded in this budget as well. There is also $14,000 budget for street
maintenance equipment

•

The Sanitation Department did not have any major capital requests in this year’s budget. They show
a decreased cost for vehicle maintenance that reflects the upgrade in major equipment that has
occurred the past few years.

•

In the Police Department, the budget continues with the Operating Lease for their fleet. We will move
to 34 total vehicles under the lease program during FY 19-20.

Special Revenue Funds
Special revenue funds are used to account for the proceeds of specific revenue sources that are restricted by
law or administrative action to expenditures for specific purposes. Annual budgets are established for the
following special revenue funds: Community Development Administration, Hospitality Tax, Accommodations
Tax, Local Accommodations Tax, Victim Rights, Law Enforcement Fund, Fire Fund and Recreation Fund.
Community Development Administration
The Community Development Fund is used to record receipts and expenditures of Community Development
Block Grant funds. $12,500 in revenue is budgeted for 2019-20 and $38,000 in expenses are budgeted. The
largest part of expenses is $24,000 to pay for a BS&A software module to allow the billing and payments for
our CDA loans to be handled within out enterprise software versus a spreadsheet. $25,500 of Fund Balance
is recommended to offset the overage in funding.
Hospitality Tax
Through an Ordinance passed in 2000, the City enacted the levy of one percent (1%) local hospitality tax on
prepared meals and beverages in the City. In 2005 through an amendment the City increased the levy to a
maximum two percent (2%). The proceeds are to be used for tourist related improvements and facilities. The
collection and use of the tax is governed by Article 7, “Local Hospitality Tax,” of Chapter 1, Title 6 of the South
Carolina Code of Laws.
The 2019-20 budget estimates revenue of $2,060,000 and includes $984,701 in eligible Parks and Recreation
expenditures to assist in funding maintenance, operations, and upkeep of all of our Parks and Recreation
Facilities throughout the City. These funds also include the costs associated with putting on the City’s Athletics
and Recreational events. In addition, $962,983 is budgeted for debt service on the 2010 Bagwell Gym and
new 2018 Bond. The 2018 Bond is an interest only payment and the 2010 Bond will expire in FY 19-20. After
this year the bonds will level off around 740,000 for the remainder of the 2018 Bond. The 2018 Bond will be
expended during FY 19-20 to complete the construction of the Operations and Maintenance Facility and on
improvements to City parks as approved by Council.
There is also $135,000 budgeted for the community events and programming.
Accommodations Tax
The Accommodations Tax Fund is used to record the proceeds and allocations of a 2% tax imposed on all
accommodations by the State. Revenue collected from the tax is divided among localities based on the
amount of revenue generated within each jurisdiction.
The proceeds must be spent for tourism promotion and related expenditures. Revenues of $80,000 are
estimated for 2019-20. Following transfers of $27,750 to the General Fund and $15,675 to the Easley
Chamber of Commerce, the remaining balance is estimated to be $36,575. Of this discretionary amount, the
Accommodations Tax Advisory Committee will make a recommendation to the City Council for the allocation
of these funds. This recommendation usually comes to Council in November in the form of a Budget
Amendment.
Local Accommodations Tax
The Accommodation Fee represents a 1.5% levy that is collected from accommodation facilities located within
the City’s jurisdiction. This tax is very similar to the State Accommodations Tax and is levied on every person
engaged in the business of furnishing accommodations for transients within the jurisdiction of the City. The

The City is budgeting $75,000 in revenue and $75,000 in expenditures to help advertise, promote, and market

Law Enforcement Fund
The Law Enforcement Fund is separated into two portions, a restricted holding account and an unrestricted
account. Funds in the restricted holding account are monies that the City has secured through arrests and
must hold until the case in question is disposed. If the defendant is found to be innocent or not guilty the
monies in question will be returned. If the defendant is found guilty the monies may be transferred to the
unrestricted account and can be used by the Police department for law enforcement activities with approval
from City Council. Monies in the Law Enforcement Fund must be used for Police related activities.
The 2019-20 budget includes $200,000 in funding to assist in purchases to support the drug enforcement of
the Police Department. The funds can potentially be used for vehicles, body cameras, equipment, or other
items to support operations. This funding reflects $190,000 coming from fund balance to cover the cost of
equipment.

Enterprise Funds
Enterprise funds are used to account for operations that are financed and operated in a manner similar to
private business enterprises. The intent is that the costs of providing goods or services to the general public
and other local governments on a continuing basis will be financed or recovered primarily through user
charges. Annual budgets are established for Stormwater Management.
Stormwater Management
Stormwater fees are levied to support the stormwater management program. The fee is based on impervious
surface areas of residential and commercial property. The flat rate structure is proposed to be $28 per year
for both developed residential and for equivalent residential units of developed commercial/industrial property.
The 2019-20 revenue estimate from these fees is $410,000.
Budgeted expenditures for 19-20 are $410,000, with $130,000 budgeted to be transferred to the General
Fund. These funds include $75,000 to cover the cost of the city’s Stormwater Engineer position that is under
the Street Department. $55,000 goes to offset the staff and equipment cost to work on the stormwater projects
and issues throughout the year. There is also $135,000 funded for drainage improvements and projects,
$2,100 in permit fees, and $5,000 for public education.

Debt Service Funds
Debt Service Funds are established to account for the accumulation of resources for, and the payment of,
general long-term debt principal and interest payments. Under state law, municipalities can only issue two
forms of long-term debt: general obligation bonds and revenue bonds. The City of Easley has used tax
increment bonds as a means of financing capital improvements, and these debt instruments are also classified
as a form of revenue bonds.

MAYOR AND COUNCIL
Under the Mayor-Council form of government, Section 5-9-10 et seq., of the South Carolina Code, the
City Council is the governing body of the City of Easley. Its membership includes the Mayor, elected at
large, and six Council members elected to staggered four-year terms. The Mayor serves as the presiding
officer at City Council meetings. The Mayor and members of City Council represent the City in a variety
of functions; they hold positions and powers as provided by City code, they exercise political leadership
to develop consensus and form coalitions on issues of community interest. Legislative policy direction is
provided to the City Administrator who is responsible for the operations of City government. Open City
Council meetings are held on the second Monday of each month.

ACCOMMODATIONS TAX
Special Revenue Fund
By a 1984 Act of the General Assembly, a two percent (2%) tax is imposed on all accommodations in the
State of South Carolina. These monies are collected by the State and distributed to counties and
municipalities. The proceeds must be spent for tourism promotion and tourism related expenditures.
Projects are reviewed by an Accommodations Tax Advisory Committee.
State law provides that (a) the first $25,000 must be allocated to the City’s General Fund for general
purpose use; (b) five percent (5%) of the balance must also be allocated to the City’s General Fund; (c)
thirty percent (30%) of the balance must be allocated for the purpose of advertising and promotion of
tourism; and (d) the remaining balance must be used for tourism related expenditures.

STORMWATER MANAGEMENT
Enterprise Fund
In 1991, under the Storm Water Management and Sediment Reduction Act, authority was granted to
local governments to establish a stormwater utility to fund such activities as watershed master planning,
facility retrofitting and facility maintenance. In 2004 Easley City Council adopted a permanent fee, with
the rate structure based on impervious surface areas of individual parcels. A stormwater management
fund was established in FY 2003-04 to record the receipts and expenditures related to the stormwater
management program. The fund is classified as an Enterprise Fund to comply with GASB 34 reporting
standards.
Stormwater Administration and Planning is responsible for developing watershed master plans,
coordinating improvements to the City’s watershed basins, and complying with state/federal mandates
related to stormwater management and water quality design.

TAX INCREMENT FUND
Debt Service Fund
The Tax Increment Fund was established in 2003 to account for the retirement of principal and interest
on tax increment revenue bonds issued by the City for various improvements in the downtown
redevelopment area. The bonds are repaid from the incremental taxes produced by the incremental
increase in assessed property values within the tax increment district. In 2004, a $640,000 tax increment
improvement bond was issued to finance improvements in the downtown redevelopment area. In 2006,
a $1,050,000 another tax increment improvement bond was issued to finance additional improvements.
In 2010, a $1,203,000 tax increment improvement bond was issued to defease the 2004 and 2006 issues
respectively. This bonding expired in FY 17-18.
During FY 17-18 the City worked with Pickens County and the School District of Pickens County to extend
the TIF for ten (10) additional years. The payments will run from FY 18-19 through FY 28-29.

APPENDIX – B
Glossary
Accrual Accounting - A basis of accounting in which revenues are recognized when earned and
expenses when incurred.
Activity - The smallest unit of budgetary accountability which defines specific and distinguishable lines
of work performed by a department or division for the purpose of accomplishing a particular service.
Appropriation - An authorization made by City Council to incur obligations against and to make
expenditures of governmental resources. Appropriations are usually made for fixed amounts and are
typically granted for a one year period, except in the instance of capital projects where they are granted
for the duration of the project.
Assessed Valuation - A valuation set upon real estate or other property by the County Assessor as a
basis for levying taxes.
Assessment Ratio - A percentage which is multiplied by the appraised market value of a property to
determine the assessed value.
Budget - A plan of financial operation embodying an estimate of proposed expenditures for a given period
of time and the proposed means of financing them.
Capital Improvement Program (CIP) - A schedule of capital expenditures to be incurred over a fiveyear period to carry out the City's program of public improvements.
Capital Budget - The first year of the CIP as approved by City Council.
Capital Improvement Project - An item whose construction or other acquisition represents a physical
improvement to the community and adds to the total physical worth of the City. The improvement should
have a useful life of not less than 15 years, and normally will exceed $200,000 in total cost.
Capital Outlay - In the operating budget, refers to any item which has an expected useful life of greater
than 3 years and an estimated unit cost of $500 or more.
CBD - Central Business District.
CDA - Community Development Administration.
CDBG - Community Development Block Grant.
CPI - Continuous Process Improvement.
Debt Service - Payment of interest and principal on an obligation resulting from the issuance of bonds.
DHEC - Department of Health and Environmental Control.
