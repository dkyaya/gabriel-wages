PROFILE OF THE CITY

One hundred and three years ago, a group of wise and visionary pioneers from the United States and
Mexico first named the area “Diaz”, in honor of Mexico’s president at the time, Porfirio Diaz. On July 4,
1904, the first passenger train arrived in town. With the advent of irrigation from the Rio Grande River,
this once arid wilderness developed into an agricultural empire, rich in a wide variety of fruits,
vegetables, cotton and grain.
The City’s first school, post office and town-site development occurred in 1907. It was during that time
that the City’s name was changed to San Benito, in honor of Benjamin Hicks, a benevolent businessman
who was highly regarded by the people of the community. In 1911 San Benito was incorporated.
Today the City’s incorporated area includes more than sixteen miles of territory and a population of over
25,000.
San Benito is located at the tip of Texas i n t he center of Cameron County. The City is located on
Interstate Highway 77 (Interstate 69E), about 10 miles south of Harlingen, 20 miles north of Brownsville,
and 40 miles east of McAllen in the Rio Grande Valley. The City enjoys a strategic location in Cameron
County via Interstate 69E that runs north and south through the City and provides a direct connection
between Harlingen, Brownsville, the international trade bridge at Los Indios and access to South Padre
Island.
San Benito is known as the Resaca City, thanks to the picturesque waterway which meanders through
this rich historic community.

The City operates under a Home Rule Charter, which was adopted on July 30, 1920. The government
body has a commission-manager type of government composed of the City manager, mayor, and four
commissioners elected for three-year terms.
The City Manager is the chief administrator and executive officer for the City and has full responsibility
for carrying out Commission policies and administering City operations, including hiring department
Directors and all other City employees.

City service departments provide a full range of services including police and fire protection, building
inspections, animal control services, parks and recreation services, library services, public works
services and general administrative services (administration, human resources, planning, municipal court
and finance). In addition, the City of San Benito also provides water, wastewater services, and solid
waste collection.
The City’s estimated population at 2013 was 25,250. The City’s is part of a larger metropolitan area
population of approximately ninety thousand.

Local Economy
The City’s economy is positively impacted by the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), the
growth of manufacturing plants and the shipping industry of fruits and vegetables via the international
bridge. The maquiladora (twin manufacturing plant) industry operates on the concept of manufacturing
performed in Mexico with support facilities on the U.S. side. San Benito is just minutes from the Free
Trade International Bridge at Los Indios, which it partially owns. The bridge is one of the most modern
ports of entry of South Texas, and provides a quick route to the Mexican border cities of Matamoros,
Reynosa and Valle Hermoso, as well as the interior industrial city of Monterrey.
San Benito’s economy is supported by two major retail developments Wal-Mart and HEB.
In addition to the San Benito Independent School District, Idea Public Schools is offered to the citizens
of San Benito and surrounding cities including Harlingen. Both the San Benito Independent School
District and the IDEA Public Schools have expanded and improved their structures in order to
accommodate growth.

Economic Outlook
The economic outlook for the City of San Benito continues to be favorable. Southwest Key Programs has
established two facilities within the City. The organization employs approximately 395 individuals
responsible for the care and education of unaccompanied minors, from foreign countries, primarily
Mexico. These jobs are incremental and provide additional stimulus to the City’s economy in the form of
sales taxes (primary) and property taxes for those choosing to locate within the City (secondary).
Business construction permits within the City increased from 2013 levels of $11.23 million to $12.27
million during 2014. Local builders continue to build new homes within the City.

Fiscal 2014-2015 Annual Operating Budget

I am pleased to present the following proposed budget for the 2014-2015 fiscal year. In accordance with
City Charter and policy, the budget is a balanced budget. This year’s budget presents a plan in financial
terms to accomplish the objective established for the upcoming year.
The proposed budget is based on conservative growth and modest economic recovery. Every attempt has
been made to anticipate and meet the demands for services of the community we serve while utilizing the
available resources as effectively as possible. This budget will become the basis by which City staff will
manage the funds provided and will become a management tool for oversight of each department’s
activities.
The General, Utility, Community Development Block Grant, Debt Service and Economic Development
funds are appropriated by ordinance. The Hotel / Motel Tax Fund, Tax Increment Reinvestment Zone
Fund, UTHSCA – Wellness Grant and USDC – EDA Museum grant funds are presented within this
document but are not appropriated by ordinance.
The General Fund’s 2014-2015 budget allocates $11,348,360 toward the provision of services to the
Citizens of San Benito. This operating budget is $243,638 or 2.1% less than the fiscal 2013-2014
operating budget. The budget provides additional core service funding for Police, Fire, Code and Stormwater, Public Grounds, Parks and Recreation and the Public Library. The budget includes a merit increase
for non-exempt employee of up to $.40 per hour. Health care insurance increases of approximately seven
percent (7%) are included within the General Fund’s budget. No amount of this increase has been passed
through to the employees. There were no amounts budgeted for capital outlay for the upcoming fiscal
period.
The City’s Proprietary Fund operating budgets are balanced; expenditures are equal to revenues. The
Water production and Wastewater funds reflect decreases from the fiscal 2013-2014 operating budgets of
$19,562 and $214,123, respectively. These decrease are a result of projections of less water consumption
during fiscal 2014-2015. Health care insurance increases of approximately seven percent (7%) are also
included within these Funds’ budgets. The Solid Waste Collection fund’s fiscal 2014-2015 operating
budget is greater than the fiscal 2013-2014. Increases in this fund are the result of scheduled contractual
consumer price index adjustments charged by the service provider. These increases are passed through to
the customers.
The proposed budget anticipates modest growth within the City during fiscal 2015. The following
strategic principles are reflected within the proposed budget document.

Utility Funds
The City’s Utility funds include Water Production, Wastewater and Solid Waste Collection. The Water
Production and Wastewater funds account for the operations of the City’s water and sewer utilities which
includes the production and distribution of drinking water to the collection and treatment of wastewater.
These funds primary revenue source is service fees that are collected monthly from customers.

The Solid Waste Collection fund operates on a contractual, privatized bases and consists of revenues
billed to customers for services rendered by a third party. Allied Waste / Republic Services serves as the
City’s current provider for Solid Waste collection. The billing and customer service functions are
performed by City staff. Collection services are performed by Allied Waste personnel.
The Rio Grande River is the source of the City’s water supply. Water is diverted from the river into a
Resaca. From the Resaca the water is purchased from the Cameron County Irrigation District #2 and is
treated and distributed by the City to customers throughout the system.
The City recognizes the pressures of drought and population growth on the water supplies throughout the
region and the State. The City of San Benito is impacted by these pressures as well. The City continues to
explore alternate sources of this resource including the installation of wells and desalinization.

COMPREHENSIVE PLAN

The City entered into an agreement for the development of a Comprehensive Plan (the Plan) in mid fiscal
2014. The project is underway at this time. The Comprehensive plan will set forth a vision for the City’s
future land use, physical and economic development and help to prioritize capital projects.
The Plan will act as a long range policy document which will be adopted by the City Commission, once
completed. The Plan will establish action items for future growth and development which will include
infrastructure, public works projects, land use designations, service boundaries, park and recreational
areas and streets.
The Plan is expected to be completed before July of fiscal 2015.

THE BUDGET PROCESS

Budgeting is an essential element of sound financial management. Budgeting is especially important for
the City of San Benito. This year’s budget serves four purposes:
1. To set policy – the budget represents the policy by which specific funding decisions will be made.
Beginning in May, City management, the City Commission and department directors review the
activities necessary to provide programs and services to the citizens.
2. To act as a control over revenues and expenditures – the budget acts to ensure the efficient and
effective delivery of services to the citizen’s during the course of the year. The budget establishes
performance guidelines and benchmarks that are used to monitor the City’s and each
department’s progress in the delivery of services.
3. To serve as a financial planning tool – the budget acts as a guide for determining the amounts of
resource inflows needed for anticipated programs and activities (outflows). The budget also
contemplates unanticipated events and provides a process for periodic adjustments.
4. To communicate policy – the budget describes the activities and related services that will be
provided to the citizens in a manner that is both understandable and sufficiently detailed to reflect
the City’s commitment and responsibility to its constituency.
The City’s budget is mandated by State law and the budget process is outlined in the City’s charter.
Annual budgets are adopted on a basis consistent with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles for all
governmental funds except for capital project funds which adopt project – length budgets. The General,
Debt Service, Community Development Block Grant, Water Production, Wastewater and Solid Waste
funds use formal budgetary integration as a management control device during the year.
The legal level of budgetary responsibility is the department level. Any revisions that alter individual
expenditure categories or department totals within a fund must be approved by the City Commission.
The City’s fiscal year begins on October 1 and ends on September 30 as established by the City Charter.
The Charter requires the City manager to submit a proposed budget and an accompanying budget
message to the City Commission each year.
The planning and efforts made to present a balanced budget for review and approval begin months earlier
than the October 1 budget adoption deadline. In April, the City manager and Finance staff began planning
for next fiscal period’s budget cycle. A budget calendar is distributed to the department directors. The
budget calendar outlines the target dates for the completion of each step in the budget process.
City Commission members hold a retreat to discuss and prioritize goals for the coming year. In May, the
department directors receive budget packets from the Finance Department. These packets include
information about the department’s current spending and a comparison of what was budgeted in the prior
year.
While the department directors are preparing their budget requests, the Finance department works with
the City manager to develop the revenue projections for the next fiscal period. Revenue projections are
based on current and anticipated economic trends.

Budget workshops are held with the department directors, City manager and Finance to discuss the
department’s needs and “wish list” for the upcoming year. The City manager reviews each request and
adjusts the budget as necessary. The budget must be balanced; revenues must equal expenditures as
required by the charter.
Once the budget is balanced, the City manager presents the budget to the City Commission for review.
Department directors are available for questions from the Commission. A series of workshops are held;
usually between July and September, for the City Commission to evaluate the proposed budget in relation
to their goals and objectives. The budget workshops are open to the public and the notices are posted in
accordance with the State law.
The City Charter requires public hearings be held before the budget is adopted. These hearings provide an
opportunity for the citizens to express their ideas and opinions about the budget to their elected officials.
Once the public hearings have been conducted, the City Commission votes on the adoption of the budget.
The budget is approved and an ordinance is signed by the mayor which outlines the appropriations for
funds that have legally adopted budgets. The Commission adopts the tax rate ordinance during the same
meeting as the budget ordinance.

GOVERNMENTAL FUNDS
Most of the City’s basic services are reported in governmental funds, which focus on how money flows
into and out of those funds and the balances left at year-end that are available for spending. These funds
are reported using an accounting method called modified accrual accounting, which measures cash and all
other financial assets that can readily be converted to cash. The governmental fund type budget statements
within this document provide a detailed short-term view of the City’s general government operations and
the basic services to be provided. Governmental fund information helps you determine whether there are
more or fewer financial resources that can be spent in the near future to finance the City’s programs. We
describe the resources that can be spent in the near future to finance the City’s programs. The City’s
Governmental activities, normally supported by taxes and intergovernmental revenues, are reported
separately from its business-type activities, which primarily rely on fees and charges.

a) total governmental fund assets,

The General Fund is the primary operating fund for the City. It accounts for all of the financial resources
of the general government, except those required to be accounted for in another fund. It includes most tax
revenues and such services as public safety, inspections, planning, and administration.

only.

Hotel/Motel Tax Fund is used to account for the 7% occupancy tax revenues received from hotels

Capital Project Funds are used to account for the acquisition and construction of major capital assets
other than those financed by proprietary funds and trust funds.
PROPRIETARY FUNDS
The proprietary funds are used to account for the operations of Water production, Wastewater and Solid
Waste funds. These funds are financed and operated in a manner similar to private businesses where the
goods and services provided are financed through user fees and charges. The budgets for these funds are
also prepared using the modified accrual basis of accounting and the current financial resources
measurement focus. The budget measures the change in net position (assets less liabilities).
The Water Production fund includes four activities (departments) established to account for its operations.
These activities include administrative services, water production, the water distribution system and the
utility customer service.
The Wastewater fund accounts for the three activities necessary to provide sewer collection and treatment
services to the residents of the City. These activities include administration services, wastewater system
operations and collections.
The Solid Waste Collection fund operates on a contractual, privatized bases and consists of revenues
billed to customers for services rendered by a third party. Allied Waste / Republic Services serves as the
City’s current provider for Solid Waste collection. The billing and customer service functions are
performed by City staff. Collection services are performed by Allied Waste personnel.
SAN BENITO ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION (SBEDC)
The SBEDC is a component unit of the City of San Benito. It operates with its own independent budget
and has a board of directors consisting of seven members. The fund is a Texas 4B Economic
Development Corporation that collects one-half cent sales tax. The primary purpose of the fund is to
promote economic development within the City and provide funding for projects that promote economic
activities. The funds fiscal year runs from October 1 to September 30.

Goals and Objectives - Long Term Outlook

City Employees - to serve the public in an atmosphere of courtesy, friendliness and respect, consistently
treating everyone fairly within the policies, rules and regulations of the City of San Benito. To provide the
highest quality municipal services in an effective, creative and fiscally responsible manner.
Fiscal Responsibility - To provide the highest quality municipal services, consistent with the resources
available to us. To allocate such resources fairly to meet the needs of the community as a whole, while
recognizing the needs of various segments within the community.
Economic Development - To recognize that high quality City Services are to a large extent dependent on a
strong business community. To provide and further enhance a strong economic base by encouraging revenue
producing, high quality, "clean" retail, commercial and industrail development that is compatible with our
community atomosphere.
Education - To recognize that a major strength of our community is the educational system. To ensure that
our effors are consistent with maintaining the quality of education provided to our community. To support
the Boards of Education in this critical area.
Quality of Life - To provide a pleasing community atmosphere and a level of maintenance of public streets,
parks, right-of-way and other public facilities that are consistent with the level of maintenance our citizens
provide to their private property. To recognize and promote individual property rights while ensuring that the
rights of other are not infringed upon. To provide quality parks, recreation opportunities, library and other
information services, senior and youth programs and medical services to our citizens. To promote positive
community spirit and pride in the community.
Public Safety - To ensure that residents will be safe in their homes and neighborhoods. To be prepared for
disasters and provide for the protection of life and property in such event.
Public Infrastructure - To protect, maintain an enhance the City's public infrastructure. To anticipate the
long-term needs of the infrastructure and take prudent steps to provide for those needs.
Transportation - To provide quality streets and control systems for the efficient movement of traffic. To
provide for alternative transportation needs of all segments of the community.
Communication - To educate the community on City programs and operations as well as their role in the
governmental process.

Our goals surround continually supporting the research, development, and utilization of new and
existing technological resources. Our objective is to effectively accomplish our goals while operating
within the guidelines of our budget and administration.
Some future goals include:
 Establishing high availability broadband connections, ie, fiber optics, between key municipal

 Increased utilization of cloud based infrastructure to improve resilience and protection of our
resources.
 Continue to research and develop solutions to serve individual department or city wide needs.
 Continue to standardize and deploy industry standards and well supported and documented hardware
and software solutions according to best practices.
 Continue to seek out cost cutting measures and best value options when developing solutions.

resources for meeting areas.
 Continue to improve and maintain security levels to protect technical resources and information.
 Continue to maintain infrastructure, hardware, and software update and replacement cycles to ensure
reliable, secure, and functioning resources are available to our users.
 Continually monitor and evaluate outside progress and developments among other government
bodies and industry leaders in an effort to use their progress to the benefit of our users and citizens.
 Continually strive to resolve user issues quickly, effectively, and completely and allow users access
to our knowledge and experience for their own benefit and training.

STORMWATER DIVISION

Department Description and Mission Statement
The Stormwater division is an awareness program designed to help improve quality in our local
waterways including the Arroyo Colorado and the Lower Laguna Madre.
The mission of the Stormwater Program is to provide guidance for selecting and implementing Best
Management Practices (BMPs) to reduce pollutants in runoff from municipal operations. Assist
municipal staff with incorporating pollution prevention controls into their overall storm water
management program and educate the public in the benefits of clean water techniques that reduce
runoff accumulate pollutants such as oil, gas, and other hydrocarbons, heavy metals, deicers,
pesticides, fine sediment, fertilizers, and bacteria; all of which can impair water quality.

UTILITY ADMINISTRATION - SOLID WASTE COLLECTION

Department Description and Mission Statement
The Business Office is responsible for utility billing and the collection of all payments
received by the City from finance, utility customers, permits, licensing, public facility
rentals, cemetery and the public library.
Utility Billing is a function of the Business office and is responsible for billing, recording,
and collecting charges for water, sewer and sanitation services provided to City of San
Benito residents.
The mission of the Business Office is to provide high quality customer service, ensure
accuracy and efficiency in the collection and assesment of user charges and to do so in a
professional and courteous manner.

DEPARTMENT’S MISSION, STRATEGIC GOALS AND OPERATING BUDGETS

The Community Grants and Programs Department serves as the City’s liaison with funding agencies and
related agencies, providing grant related guidance and support to all City Departments and ensuring
compliance with grant requirements.
The City receives an annual grant from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development for
participation in the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program. These grant funds are used
to improve areas of the community populated by citizens with low to moderate incomes.
Fiscal 2014-2015 program activities include expenditures for public facilities and improvements, public
services and administration.

Within the department there are three funds:
Community Development Block Grant

The Community Grant and Program Department's defined goal is to become a broader-based
department with additional revenue streams to offer new services to the City of San Benito. The
department will continue to administer the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program,

employees that will be responsible for specific duties such as grant writing, grant administration and

HOTEL OCCUPANCY TAX (HOT) FUND

The City of San Benito levies a 7% hotel occupancy tax, which by law must be used to promote tourism

goals and objectives and identify how the funds requested will meet the criteria established over how
these funds can be utilized.

INTERNAL CONTROLS
POLICY

The City will maintain an effective system of internal control to ensure the safeguarding of assets, and to
monitor compliance with policies and procedures established by management.
PROCEDURES

Accounting controls are designed to achieve five basic objectives.
Validation
The examination, by the Finance Director or the Assistant Finance Director, for evidence that a recorded
transaction actually took place, and that it occurred in accordance with the prescribed procedures.
Accuracy
The accuracy of amounts and account classification is achieved by establishing control tasks to check
calculations, extensions, additions, and account classifications.
Completeness
Completeness controls ensure that all transactions are initially recorded on a control document and accepted
for processing one time only.
Maintenance
The objective of maintenance controls is to monitor accounting records after the entry of transactions to
ensure that they continue to reflect accurately the operations of the City.
Physical Security
Adequate physical protection of assets requires that access to assets be limited to authorized personnel.
1. Determination of Validity
A. All transactions will be reviewed by the Finance Director or the Assistant Finance Director.
B. Determination of validity will be made by examination of supporting documentation and check
for appropriate approvals.
2. Control of Documents
A.

All transactions will be entered on a control document.

B. Control documents will be pre-numbered, and physical control will be maintained over
unissued documents.
C.
Control documents include the following:
* Journal Vouchers/Debit Memoranda
* Checks
City of San Benito, Texas – Annual Budget 2014 - 2015

Page 139

CITY OF SAN BENITO – ANNUAL OPERATING BUDGET
* Purchase Requisitions/Purchase Orders
* Travel Authorization/Trip Report
* Receipts

3. Check for Accuracy
A. Documents supporting transactions will be checked for mathematical accuracy. The individual
performing the check will initial or sign the document.
4. Record in Appropriate Journals
A. As transactions are completed, they will be filed promptly in the appropriate filing area,
including a notation or cross-reference indicating the completion of the transactions.
5. Check for Completeness
A. Completeness of the transaction records will be established by reviewing the numerical control
of items (Section 2 above) that have not been matched with supporting documentation, or are
not complete. Review of control documents will be performed randomly.
6. Investigation of Unprocessed Transactions
A. Unprocessed transactions will be investigated periodically by the Accounting Coordinator or a
designee of the Finance Director.
7. Performance of Monthly Reconciliations
A. Reconciliations between subsidiary records and control accounts will be performed monthly to
ensure that postings are correct and adjustments have been properly processed.
B.

All differences will be investigated and resolved on a timely basis.

8. Safeguarding of Accounting Records
A. All assets and the accounting records will be properly safeguarded to prevent theft.
B. Assets will be tagged with sequential numbers, and recorded by the Finance Department.
C. A physical inventory will be performed regularly, and compared to property records.
D.

Access to accounting records will be limited to authorized personnel.

E. Electronic accounting records will be backed up daily to a reliable media. The most recent
backup will be preferably stored off-site.
A. Independent Audits
A. Audits by an independent CPA firm will be performed annually.

FINANCIAL POLICIES

FINANCIAL AND REPORTING SYSTEMS
The City’s accounting and financial reporting systems shall be maintained in accordance with the
standards prescribed by Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAB) and the Governmental
Accounting Standards Board (GASB).
INDEPENDENT AUDIT
The City Commission shall provide for an independent annual audit of all City accounts and may provide
for more frequent audits as it deems necessary. Such audits shall be made by a certified public accountant
or firm of such accountants who no personal interest, direct or indirect, in the fiscal affairs of the City
governments or any of its officers. The City Commission may designate such accountant or firm annually
or for a period not exceeding three years, but the designation for any particular year shall be made no later
than thirty (30) days after the beginning of such fiscal year.
BUDGET
On or before the 15th day of July of each year, the City Manager shall submit to the City Commission a
budget for the ensuing fiscal year and an accompanying message. The City Manager’s message shall
explain the budget both in fiscal terms and in terms of the work programs. It shall outline the proposed
financial policies of the City for the ensuing fiscal year, describe the important features of the budget,
indicate any major changes from the current year in financial policies, expenditures and revenues together
with the reasons for such changes, summarize the City’s debt position and include such other material as
the City Manager deems desirable.
The budget shall provide a complete financial plan of all City funds and activities for the ensuing fiscal
year and, except as required by law or this Charter, shall be in such form as the City Manager deems
desirable or the City Commission may require. The budget shall begin with a clear general summary of its
contents, shall show in detail all estimated income, indicating the proposed property tax levy, and all
proposed expenditures, including debt service, for the ensuing fiscal year, and shall be so arranged as to
show comparative figures for actual and estimated income and expenditures of the preceding fiscal year.
It shall indicate in separate sections:
(1) The proposed goals and objectives and expenditures for current operations during the ensuing
fiscal year, detailed for each fund by organization unit and program, purpose or activity, and the
method of financing such expenditures;
(2) Proposed capital expenditures during the ensuing fiscal year, detailed for each fund by
organization unit when practical, and the proposed method of financing each such capital
expenditure; and
(3) The anticipated income and expense and profit and loss for the ensuing year for each utility or
other enterprise fund operated by the City.
For any fund, the total of proposed expenditures shall not exceed the total of estimated income plus
carried forward fund balance, exclusive of reserves.
The City Commission shall publish in one or more newspapers of general circulation in the city a brief
summary of the budget and a notice stating:
(1) The times and places where copies of the message and budget are available for inspection by
the public, and

(2) The time and place, not less than two weeks after such publication, for a public hearing on the

After the public hearing, the City Commission may adopt the budget with or without amendment. In
amending the budget, it may add or increase programs or amounts and may delete or decrease any
programs or amounts, except expenditures required by law or for debt service or for an estimated cash
deficit, provided that no amendment to the budget shall increase the authorized expenditures to an amount
greater that total estimated income.
The City Commission shall adopt the budget on or before the 30th day of September of each year. If it
fails to adopt the budget by this date, the then existing budget, together with any tax-levying Ordinance
and appropriation Ordinance, shall be deemed adopted for the ensuing fiscal year on a month to month
basis with all items in it prorated accordingly until such time as the City Commission adopts a budget for
the balance of that fiscal year.
To implement the adopted budget, the City Commission shall adopt, prior to the beginning of the ensuing
fiscal year:
(a) An appropriation Ordinance making appropriations by department or major organizational
unit and authorizing a single appropriation for each program or activity;
(b) A tax levy Ordinance authorizing the property tax levy or levies and setting the tax rate or
rates: and
(c) Any other Ordinances required to authorize new revenues or to amend the rates or other
features of existing taxes or other revenue sources.
If during the fiscal year the City Manager certifies that there are available for appropriation revenues in
excess of those estimated in the budget, the City Commission may make supplemental appropriations for
the year up to the amount of such excess.
To meet a public emergency affecting life, health, property or the public peace, the City Commission may
make emergency appropriations. Such appropriations may be made by emergency Ordinance in
accordance with the provisions of §2.13. To the extent that there are no available unappropriated revenues
or a sufficient fund balance to meet such appropriations, the City Commission may by such emergency
Ordinance authorize the issuance of emergency notes, which may be renewed from time to time, but the
emergency notes and renewals of any fiscal year shall be paid not later than the last day of the fiscal year
next succeeding that in which the emergency appropriation was made.
If at any time during the fiscal year it appears probable to the City Manager that the revenues or fund
balances available will be insufficient to finance the expenditures for which appropriations have been
authorized, the Manager shall report to the City Commission without delay, indicating the estimated
amount of the deficit, any remedial action taken by the Manager and recommendations as to any other
steps to be taken. The City Commission shall then take such further action as it deems necessary to
prevent or reduce any deficit and for that purpose it may by Ordinance reduce one or more appropriations.
At any time during the fiscal year the City Commission may by Resolution or Ordinance transfer part or
all of the unencumbered appropriation balance from one department or major organizational unit to other
departments or major organizational units. To meet a public emergency affecting life, health, property or
the public peace only, the City Manager may transfer part or all of any unencumbered appropriation
balances among programs within a department or organizational unit and shall report such transfers to the
City Commission in writing in a timely manner.

INVESTMENT POLICY
The City’s Investment Policy establishes policies for the cash management and investment of funds of the
City of San Benito in accordance with the Public Funds Investment Act, Chapter 2256, Texas
Government Code, (the “Act”), Public Funds Collateral Act, Chapter 2257, Texas Government Code, and
other applicable federal, state and local requirements. The cash management and investment of all funds
shall be governed by the following investment objectives, in order of priority:
Safety – The primary objective of the CITY’s investment activity is the preservation and safety of
principal. Each investment transaction shall seek to first avoid capital losses, whether they arise from
issuer defaults or erosion of market value. Credit risk shall be managed through collateralization of bank
deposits and prohibition of corporate entity risk.
Liquidity – The second objective of the CITY’s investment portfolio is to remain sufficiently liquid to
meet operating requirements. Liquidity shall be achieved by matching investment maturities with
estimated cash flow requirements, maintaining liquid reserves and by investing in securities with active
secondary markets.
Diversification – It is the CITY’s policy to diversify its investment portfolio. Invested funds shall be
diversified to minimize risk or loss resulting from over-concentration of assets in a specific maturity,
specific issuer, or specific class of investment, when appropriate. Cash flow projections shall be utilized
to spread investment maturities, smooth market fluctuations, and reduce reinvestment risk.
Yield – Subordinate to the safety and liquidity objectives, the CITY’s investment activity shall seek to
earn competitive market rates of return commensurate with the appropriate level of risk.

DEBT ISSUANCE POLICY
This policy applies to all long-term debt securities issued by the City and may include general obligation
bonds G.O., certificates of obligation C.O., revenue bonds, capital leases, private placements, and letters
of credit.
General purpose debt and tax-supported debt instruments are recorded in the City’s Long-term Debt
Account Group while current principal and interest requirements and necessary resources to service debt
instruments are recorded in the City’s Debt Service Fund.
Certificates of Obligation are recorded in the fund that generates user fees that are pledged to repay the
debt. For example, Water Certificates of Obligation are recorded in the Water Enterprise Fund,
Wastewater Certificates of Obligation in the Wastewater Enterprise Fund. A Certificate of Obligation
may be allocated between the Water and Wastewater funds based on the stated purpose of the debt
issuance. All certificates of obligation issued by the City shall be approved by ordinance and issued in
accordance with the laws of the State of Texas.
An exception may occur when a self-supported issuance is supported by a dedicated tax such as
Hotel/Motel Tax. In this situation, the certificates will be recorded in the City’s Long Term Debt Account
Group, because they are considered general-purpose debt.
Revenue bonds are recorded in the Enterprise Fund that generates the user fees that represent the
underlying revenue pledge for the debt. For example, Water and Wastewater Revenue Bonds are recorded
as a liability in the Water and/or Wastewater Enterprise Fund(s).
Capital leases for governmental funds are recorded in the Long-Term Debt Account Group. Capital leases
for enterprise funds are recorded in the Enterprise Funds.
Each of the funds referred to above is accounted for in the City of San Benito Comprehensive Annual
Financial Report (“CAFR”).
All bond funds approved by a vote of the citizens of the City of San Benito, Texas will be expended only
for the purposes stated in the bond issue.

FUND BALANCE POLICY

Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to ensure that fund balances are reported in accordance with Generally
Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) as promulgated by the Government Accounting Standards
Board (GASB). GASB No. 54 Fund Balance Reporting and Governmental Fund Type Definitions was
issued to improve the usefulness and comparability of fund balance information. The standard clarifies
the definitions of the different types of funds that a governmental entity may establish for financial
reporting purposes.
Definitions
The standard also requires the classification of fund balance among the following categories:
•

•

•

•

•

Non-spendable Fund Balance – Fund balance reported as “non-spendable” represents fund
balance associated with non-current receivables, inventory or prepaid items. The cash outlay for
these types of items has already been made and therefore the resources represented by this fund
balance category cannot be spent again; hence the term “non-spendable”.
Restricted Fund Balance – Fund balance reported as “restricted” represents amounts that can be
spent only on the specific purposes stipulated by law or by the external providers of those
resources.
Committed Fund Balance – Fund balance reported as “committed” includes amounts that can be
used only for the specific purposes determined by a formal action of our City’s highest level of
decision-making authority, the City Commissioners.
Assigned Fund Balance – Fund balance reported as “assigned” represents amounts intended to be
used for specific purposes, but not meeting the criteria to be reported as committed or restricted
fund balance. In addition, our governing body may grant to an administrator within our
organization the authority to “assign” fund balance.
Unassigned Fund Balance – Fund balance reported as “unassigned” represents the residual
classification of fund balance and includes all spendable amounts not contained within the other
classifications.

Policy
It is the policy of the City that the governing body may commit fund balance for any reason that is consistent
with the definition of Committed Fund Balance contained within GASB-54. Examples of reasons to
commit fund balance would be to display intentions to use portions of fund balance for future capital
projects, stabilization funds, or to earmark special General Fund revenue streams unspent at year-end that
are intended to be used for specific purposes.
After approval by the City Commissioners, the amount reported as Committed Fund Balance cannot be
undone without utilizing the same process required to commit the funds. Therefore, in accordance with
GASB-54, it is the policy of the City that funds can only be removed from the Committed Fund Balance
category after motion and approval by the City Commissioners.
In accordance with GASB-54, funds that are intended to be used for a specific purpose but have not received
the formal approval action at the governing body level may be recorded as Assigned Fund Balance.
City of San Benito, Texas – Annual Budget 2014 - 2015

Likewise, redeploying assigned resources to an alternative use does not require formal action by the
governing body.
GASB-54 states that resources can be assigned by the governing body or by another internal body or person
whom the governing body gives the authority to do so, such as the City Manager or Director of Finance.
Therefore, having considered the requirements to assign fund balance, it is the policy of the City that the
City Manager will have the authority to assign fund balance of this organization based on intentions for
use of fund balance communicated by the governing body.
It is the policy of the City that when expenditures are incurred that would qualify as expenditures of either
Restricted Fund Balance or Unrestricted Fund Balance (Committed, Assigned, or Unassigned), those
expenditures will first be applied to the Restricted Fund Balance category.
Furthermore, it is the policy of the City that when expenditures are incurred that would qualify as a use of
any of the Unrestricted Fund Balance categories (Committed, Assigned, or Unassigned), those
expenditures will be applied in the order of Committed first, then Assigned, and then Unassigned.
It is the policy of this City to maintain at all times an overall Unrestricted Fund Balance: that not
otherwise designated for a specific purpose (Committed Fund Balance, Assigned Fund Balance, and
Unassigned Fund Balance) of not less than two (2) months of regular General Fund operating
expenditures, measured based on the most recently completed fiscal year. If it is determined that the City
is below this minimum established fund balance level, the governing body will be informed of this
condition and take necessary budgetary steps to bring the fund balance level into compliance with this
policy through budgetary actions.

GLOSSARY

Account: A term used to identify an individual asset, liability, expenditure control, revenue control,
encumbrance control, or fund balance.
Accounting System: The total structure of records and procedures which discover, record, classify, and
report information on the financial position and operations of a governmental unit or any of its funds,
balanced account groups, and organizational components. Sometimes referred to as Chart of Accounts
and/or Account Classification System.
Accounts Receivable: Amounts owing on open account from private persons, firms, or corporations for
goods and services furnished by a governmental unit (but not including amounts due from other funds of
the same governmental unit).
Accrual Basis: The basis of accounting under which revenues are recorded when earned and
expenditures are recorded as soon as they result in liabilities for benefits received, notwithstanding that
the receipt of the revenue or the payment of the expenditure may take place, in whole or in part, in
another accounting period.
Accrue: To record revenues when earned and to record expenditures as soon as they result in liabilities
for benefits received, notwithstanding that the receipt of the revenue or payment of the expenditure may
take place, in whole or in part, in another accounting period.
Accrued Expenses: Expenses incurred during the current account period but which are not payable until
a subsequent accounting period.
Accrued Interest Payable: A liability account which represents the amount of interest accrued at the
balance sheet date but which is not due until a later date.
Accrued Revenue: Revenue earned during the current accounting period but which is not collected until
a subsequent accounting period.
Activity: A specific and distinguishable line of work performed by one or more organizational
components of a governmental unit for the purpose of accomplishing a function for which the
governmental unit is responsible. For example, "Code Enforcement" is an activity performed in the
discharge of the "Public Safety" function.
Ad Valorem: In proportion to value. A basis for levy of taxes upon property.
Budget Document: The instrument used by the budget-making authority to present a comprehensive
financial program to the appropriating body. The budget document usually consists of three parts. The
first part contains a message from the budget-making authority, together with a summary of the proposed
expenditures and the means of financing them. The second part consists of schedules supporting the
summary. These schedules show in detail the information as to past years' actual revenues, expenditures,
and other data used in making the estimates. The third part is composed of drafts of the appropriation,
revenue, and borrowing measures necessary to put the budget into effect.
Budgetary Accounts: Those accounts which reflect budgetary operations and condition, such as
estimated revenues, appropriations, and encumbrances, as distinguished from proprietary accounts.
