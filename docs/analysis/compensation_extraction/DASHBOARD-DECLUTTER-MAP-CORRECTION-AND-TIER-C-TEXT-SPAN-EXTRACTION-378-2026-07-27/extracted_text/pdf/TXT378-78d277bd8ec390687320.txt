Request for Purchase Approval – Self Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA)

Summary
The Delta Township Fire Department is in the process of replacing our aging SCOTT Self-Contained Breathing
Apparatus (SCBA). This process began a year ago while we were writing for a FEMA AFG (Assistance to
Firefighter Grant). As part of our due diligence in the grant writing process, we requested price quotes for 2019
NFPA compliant SCBA from three of the major vendors available, and also requested that demo units of each
brand be available for staff to evaluate.
The three major vendors that we requested demo units from were:
•
•
•

SCOTT, (brand currently used)
MSA
Draeger

We requested cost estimates and demo units from these three vendors because of our experience with all of them,
as well as their warranties, familiarity, serviceability, and fire service reputation.
The information received included costs for a complete SCBA, which includes the SCBA frame and two bottles,
RIT (Rapid Intervention Team) bags that include one SCBA frame, bottle, and mask for use with trapped
firefighters, and individual pricing for accessories such as spectacle kits; adapters; soft goods; shipping costs and
staff training costs.
SCBA are an essential part of firefighter personal protection equipment when operating in environments that are
Immediate Dangerous to Life and Health (IDLH). These environments include all fires; hazardous materials
incidents; low oxygen level areas, confined spaces, and fire investigations.
Background
The Delta Township Fire Department submitted a grant request for Fiscal Year (FY) 2020 Assistance to
Firefighters Grants grant number EMW-2020-FG-11637, for the replacement of Self-Contained Breathing
Apparatus (SCBA) that ranges from eleven (11) years of age to twenty-nine (29) years of age.

Retention and Recruitment Memorandum of Understanding

In 2017, the Township Board supported adding six new suppression fire department positions to react to the
increasing number of EMS runs throughout the Township and Looking Glass Regional Fire Authority.
These positions were added using a SAFER grant. Since that time, we have had challenges remaining at full
staffing. In December, we had three departures which left us with eight open positions.
This has been a problem across fire departments, EMS agencies, and in general all public safety including
law enforcement (see graphic). There is currently a statewide and national paramedic shortage. Of the
employees we have hired, we have lost some to regional departments, some to other careers such as nursing,
I.T., mechanic, private EMS, moving closer to their home, and to retirements.
The Township has generally insisted on hiring firefighters only with paramedic certifications (FF/EMT-P).
We have had basic EMT (FF/EMT-B) who have been part-time employees in the past. In addition, we have
often had paramedics filling part-time roles who have moved into full-time roles when there are vacancies.
We have not been able to attract any part-time help for several years.
Over the last few months we have increased our marketing of job postings for full-time paramedics,
streamlined the hiring process, eliminated the residency requirement to draw from further out in the state,
and have offered double overtime in order to keep per day staffing levels up. We hired a part-time
paramedic who is being sponsored through the fire academy. We continue to strengthen our relationship
with Lansing Community College and have recruited through other colleges and paramedic programs.
There are just not a lot of paramedics in the state to choose from. Our continuing shortages lead to
paramedic burnout, stresses mutual aid, and impacts our service delivery under the Looking Glass Regional
Fire Authority partnership.
Forward and Better Workgroup
In December 2021, we began a workgroup consisting of myself, both Chiefs, the Union president and vice
president, and several representatives from the department to brainstorm solutions for staffing and called it
the “Forward and Better” workgroup. We needed to think boldly and move quickly to address the existing
problem, but also find solutions that will work moving forward as we will have as many as six to nine
retirements over the next three years as these firefighters reach 25 years of service and 50 years of age. In
addition, we recognized that a model of every department trying to poach from other departments is not a
sustainable model for service delivery. This workgroup met weekly, analyzed information and sought

Current Staff Retention:

not able to work from home or participate in the worksharing program as many of our other employees

the shortage of FF/EMT-P, we need to bolster our staffing with FF/EMT-B. There are many organizations
that run staffing models with both. We are fortunate to have several employees who have experience in
those models prior to coming to Delta.
The Township has had the ability to hire EMT-B but typically has only hired part-time in the recent past.
This MOU recognizes the organizational shift to hiring FF/EMT-B and also Paramedics without Firefighter
1 and 2 certifications with the goal of sponsoring them through a paramedic program or fire academy. We
cannot predict the future, but the ultimate goal is to grow from within with new hires eventually obtaining
full FF/EMT-P status. We will have certain clawbacks and commitments for new hires in exchange for
sponsoring through licensure programs. Paramedic programs range from $5,000 to $10,000 per student.
There will also be some costs for overtime backfill and clinical hours. A fire academy for individuals
already licensed as paramedics is less costly. The total estimated cost will vary depending on how many
individuals hired and where they are at in their programs.
We will continue to recruit FF/EMT-P as our ultimate goal, however in order to maintain staffing levels we
must expand our positions.

time of ratification.
5. Probationary employees employed at the time of ratification will receive the onetime bonus upon successfully completing probation.
6. The bonus will be included in the next possible payroll after a signed MOU
(expected to be on February 25th or March 11).
7. The one-time $4,000 bonus will not be considered compensation for the purposes
of MERS retirement.
EMS recruitment – FF/EMT-B
8. Full-time FF/EMT-B will be hired at the minimum level (see payscale attachment
1) as bargaining unit members.
9. Full-time FF/EMT-B receiving tuition reimbursement for paramedic certification
from the Township will be given 36 months to obtain a paramedic license.

10. A Full-time FF/EMT-B receiving tuition reimbursement for paramedic
certification may be given additional time upon evaluation by the Chief or the
Township of up to a year.
11. The Township will pay tuition for a township approved paramedic program. The
Township will pay an equal amount to a program that a new hire may already be
participating in and is approved by the Township.
12. FF/EMT-B receiving paid tuition must obtain a paramedic license or may be
subject to tuition repayment.
13. Upon obtaining a paramedic license, the FF/EMT-P must stay with Delta
Township Fire for at least 3 years or may be subject to repayment of tuition.
14. A FF/EMT-B is not eligible for tuition reimbursement under 57.2 until obtaining
paramedic licensure.
Paramedic Only
15. A Paramedic without Firefighter 1&2 certification will be hired at the minimum
level and work a 40 hour week (See payscale attachment 1) as bargaining unit
members.
16. The Township will fund the Firefighter 1&2 academy.
17. A Paramedic receiving tuition support for firefighter 1 &2 certification must obtain
Firefighter 1 & 2 certification within 12 months.
18. A Paramedic receiving tuition support for firefighter 1&2 may be given additional
time up to a year to complete Firefighter 1&2 certification upon evaluation by the
Chief or the Township up to a year.
19. A Paramedic is not eligible for scheduled shift or administrative overtime unless
emergency conditions exist or is held over due to a EMS or Fire response until
obtaining Firefighter 1 and 2 certification.
20. Paramedic must stay with Delta Township Fire for at least 1 year or may have to
reimburse the Township for the fire academy.
21. A Paramedic is not eligible for tuition reimbursement under 57.2 until obtaining
Firefighter 1 and 2 certification.
22. A Paramedic is not eligible for Educational Incentives under article 58.1 until
obtaining Firefighter 1 and 2 certification.
23. A Paramedic is not eligible for Food allowance in Article 40.1 until obtaining fulltime firefighter status.
24. A Paramedic is not eligible for holiday pay in Article 38.1-38.4 until obtaining fulltime firefighter status.
Paramedic Retention
25. In order to recognize a differential between FF/EMT-P and FF/EMT-B and
Paramedic only – The Township will provide an increased paramedic incentive (at
or around November 15th) to replace 58.1b to read:
“b. Any employee that is a rank of FF/EMT-P and above and maintains their
Paramedic license shall receive $2,500 annually.”
26. In order to recognize the burden on those serving in an FTO capacity, the Township
will pay assigned FTO’s an increased rate of $2.00/hour under Article 65.2:5

“65.2:5. The assigned F.T.O.’s will be paid an hourly premium of
$2.00/hour. The premium will be a flat rate added to the base rate. Unit
members on overtime will be eligible for the premium, however the
premium will not be calculated based on overtime rates.
