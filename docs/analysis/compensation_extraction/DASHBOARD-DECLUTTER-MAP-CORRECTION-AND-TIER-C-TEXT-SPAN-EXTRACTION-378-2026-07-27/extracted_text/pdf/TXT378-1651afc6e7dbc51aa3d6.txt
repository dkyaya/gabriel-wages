Executive Summary

The Executive Summary has been prepared to provide an overview of the 2018-2019 budget, its
challenges and achievements over the past years, as well as its priorities based on community input. The
budget document is served as a policy document, a financial plan, an operations guide, and a
communication device to the public. We hope that it is being used to its fullest extent benefiting all
interested readers learning about our community.
Community Goals and Priorities
During the past six years, the City has been able to maintain a fund balance above the minimum policy
requirement (90-days working capital) and ended each year with a surplus. Recovering from less than
45 days fund balance was toughest especially in 2013 and 2014. A few vacant positions and salaries were
frozen during those two years. Citywide expenditure was trimmed to absolute needs. Finally, the goal
of reaching 90 days fund balance was achieved at end of fiscal year 2014. In 2015, the City conducted a
community study to identify current issues. During budget 2016, Council and staff began our commitment
to the following goals delineated in the 2015-2022 Strategic Economic and Community Development Plan:
1. Infrastructure improvement plan
2. Blight reduction
3. Interstate 45 plan
4. Communication plan
5. Community center plan
6. Grocery store plan
7. Tax base expansion strategy
8. Economic development plan
9. Compensation improvement plan
10. Youth recreation program development
11. La Marque Lemonade Day plan
During the past three budget years, the City accomplished projects that align with the established longterm goals including:
Infrastructure improvement:
• Completion of Phase I of the Wastewater Treatment Plant upgrade.
• Replacement of aged Badger water meters to new smart AMI meters with more accurate
reads of water usage.
• Council approval of $2.5 million to improve our sanitary sewer overflow (SSO) citywide.
• Construction of a new public safety building.
• Development and utilization of an Infrastructure Improvement Plan is ongoing

Blight reduction:

Executive Summary
The City continues to move forward with the same established direction identified in the Strategic
Economic and Community Development Plan. As part of Budget 2019, and assuming no unanticipated
decrease in revenues during the year, Council approved eight new Police vehicles, one new vehicle for
City Marshal, one vehicle for Emergency Management, a 1-ton truck for Public Works, GIS map services,
playground equipment, park restroom upgrades, and citywide drainage projects.
Overview of City Funds
The City has 38 funds with total budgeted revenues of $21,245,862 in fiscal year 2018-2019. Of the 38
funds, General Fund is the largest fund with total budgeted revenues of $11,084,117. Second largest
fund is Utility Fund with budgeted revenues of $4,859,679. Eighteen out of the 38 funds are Special
Revenue Funds which are restricted to specified expenditure. In addition, the City has Capital Projects
Funds which capture major projects and a Debt Service Fund which captures all the tax-supported debt
payments. The Economic Development Corporation Fund receives one-half cent of sales tax through the
Texas State Comptroller’s office. The City also has trust funds, where it serves as a trust agent. These
funds include the Library Memorial Fund and the Drug Seizure pending final Court decision Fund.
General Fund
Major Revenue Sources
Sales Tax
Sales tax is the largest revenue source in the General Fund. Sales tax accounts for 36% of General Fund
total revenues. City’s sales tax rate is 8.25 cents collected through the state. The city receives 2 cents
and the state keeps 6.25 cents. Of the 2 cents the city receives, .5 cent is allocated to Economic
Development Fund with restricted purposes. 1.5 cent is allocated to General Fund where .5 cent is for
property tax relief and the remaining 1 cent is for general operations of the city. Sales tax revenue
comes from sales transactions within the city limits. Sales tax revenue has been increasing steadily the
past few years. The City budgeted conservatively for fiscal year 2019 with a slight 1% decline due to
possible construction project on IH 45 by TxDOT. In fiscal year 2016, the City received windfall revenues
from a special audit. For fiscal year 2018, sales tax revenue is projected to increase by 6%.
Property Tax
Property tax is the second-largest revenue source in General Fund. Ad valorem tax accounts for 33% of
total General Fund revenues. There are two components to the ad valorem tax rate per $100 of taxable
assessed valuation: (1) Maintenance and Operations; and (2) Debt Service. The General Fund captures
the Maintenance and Operations component of the tax rate. The Debt Service Fund captures the Debt
Service component of the tax rate. Property tax values are set by the Galveston County Appraisal District
each year. The City adopts a tax rate, which is based on taxable values and needed expenditure, to
carry out city operations as well as paying off debts during the fiscal year. Property tax revenues have
been growing steadily over the years except for 2016. During August 2017, the City experienced
Hurricane Harvey with minimal damage and very little impact on value depreciation. In fiscal year 2017,
tax revenue increased by 9%. Due to new subdivisions and growth, fiscal year 2018 revenue is projected
to increase by 15%. In August 2018, the city issued new debts for capital acquisition and capital projects,
additional funds are needed for debt service allocation. To maintain the same tax rate, additional Debt
Service allocation caused Maintenance and Operation portion to decrease. Revenues generated from
new values on the roll is projected to partially offset the lower Maintenance and Operation rate in
General Fund. Total General Fund Current Tax revenue for fiscal year 2019 is budgeted at slightly less
than 2018 assuming a 98% collection rate.

Executive Summary
Property tax rate remained at .490764 cents since 2015 after a rollback from .514360. Tax benefits the
city currently provides include: Homestead exemption, Disabled Veterans exemption of $10,000, Over
65 exemption of $20,000, and Over 65 Freeze which means during the lifetime of a qualified taxpayer,
he/she or his/her surviving spouse will never pay more than the frozen tax amount starting from the year
he/she becomes qualified for this freeze.
Franchise
Franchise is third-largest revenue source in General Fund. It accounts for 8% of total General Fund
revenues. Franchise derives from authority given to city government to grant permission for the use of
public right-of-way for purposes referred to as “utilities” such as gas, electricity, cable, and
communications. In fiscal year 2017, franchise revenues increased by 5% due to growth. Fiscal year 2018
is projected to increase by 3%. Fiscal year 2019 budget reflects a conservative 1% increase pending
negotiations on cable franchise agreement.

Licenses and Permits Revenues
Licenses and Permits are user fees to compensate for services provided to end users. This category of
revenues account for 7% of total General Fund revenues. In fiscal year 2017, revenues increased by 22%.
In addition, the city received a one-time pipeline permit fee of $264,015. In fiscal year 2018, revenues
are projected to increase by 37% mainly due to new subdivision growth. Building permits reflect a huge
increase from $308,364 to $569,076 (85%). Another area showing signs of growth is Plan Review Fees
with a projected increase from $44,136 to $57,224 (30%). Budget 2019 reflects a conservative projection
assuming growth rate returns to average level.
Fine/Forfeitures
Fine and forfeiture revenues come from violations in the community. This category also accounts for 7%
of total General Fund revenues. Fiscal year 2017 shows a slight decline of 1%. Fiscal year 2018 is
projected to increase by 14% mainly due to a combination of (1) having an active City Marshal on
warrants; (2) code and police officers active on citations. Budget 2019 reflects a conservative average
level based on historical trends.
Fire/EMS
Fire/Ambulance is also a form of user fees to partially compensate for services provided. Ambulance
revenue is heavily impacted by heath care legislation changes as well as industry trends in health
insurance and Medicare. The fluctuations follow changes that pass at any point in time. Fiscal year 2016
showed a huge decline, however, fiscal year 2017 revenue resumed with an increase of 10%. Fiscal year
2018 projected revenue shows a decline of 25%. Budget 2019 assumes an average level based on historical
trends.

Executive Summary
Expenditures
The City’s biggest expenditure function is Police followed by Fire/EMS, General Government, Other
Requirements (legal fees, liability insurance, etc.), Public Works, Court, and Library.
Police
City’s certified police officers, except for the Chief of Police, follow Civil Service laws and are under
Collective Bargaining agreement with the City. Each budget year, personnel related expenditure
increases based on terms and conditions agreed from negotiations. To ensure Police maintains an
efficient fleet, each year the City has been budgeting for batched vehicles replacement. This concept
relieves placing too much burden on any one budget year for capital outlay. Over the years, it has been
a challenge to recruit and retain telecommunicators. Council has approved different pay incentives as
well as rate increases, but the challenge remains.
Fire/EMS
Fire/EMS personnel, except of the Fire Chief and the Administrative Assistant, function under terms of
Collective Bargaining agreement with the City. During fiscal year 2018, the city increased nine additional
full-time Fire/EMS staff due to our new public safety building. In addition, supplies, insurance, training,
equipment, etc. increased with anticipation to occupy the new facility. Budget 2019, the City is
increasing nine additional part-time staff upon completion of the facility. To relieve heavy burden on
any budget year, the City allocates around $40,000 for SCBA to ensure the department continues to
function efficiently.
Other governmental functions
During August 2018, Council approved to purchase three buildings of which one is to house our Public
Works department staff, the others are planned to be used as community center for different community
events including some youth activities. In addition, Council also approved to renovate other municipal
facilities to enhance space utilization. During fiscal year 2019, the City plans to participate with the
Galveston County streets project program to improve certain city streets under poor condition. More
detailed information will be available once the County releases additional information to the City.
Utility Fund
Due to aged infrastructures over number of years, unaccounted water usage has been a severe issue
causing large sum of revenue loss each year. In 2016, the City hired an independent consultant to analyze
and recommend a solution. The study result reflected 60% nonbillable water loss. Part of the
recommendation was to replace the aged water meters with AMI smart meters to read more accurately
so we can be more accountable for true usage. In addition to meters replacement, Council allocated
some funds to repair waterlines and to minimize leakages citywide.
Since 2010, the City’s water and sewer rates remained the same. The city’s budget for purchased water
from Gulf Coast Water Authority (GCWA) continues to increase each year due to infrastructure cost.
Upon completion of the AMI meters project, it is anticipated that some of the unaccounted lost revenue
will be captured to offset some of the increased water expenditure, however, La Marque’s Wastewater
Treatment plant requires additional upgrades to comply to state requirements. The City plans to
continue seeking grants to help with this project.

Executive Summary

One of the biggest challenges experienced by our Utilities department has been recruiting licensed and
experienced personnel to operate and maintain the water, sewer, and wastewater system to meet state
guidelines. For budget year 2019, assuming no unanticipated decrease in revenues or increase in
expenses, Council approved five new positions and one new position in Utility Billing department during
the year.
Conclusion
Every year, the City recognizes the challenge balancing community demands with limited resources while
maintaining compliance to Federal and State laws as well as City Charter and City policies. Staff and
Council have been very conservative to ensure all residents continue to receive quality services
throughout the year at minimum cost.

History

La Marque, also known as Highlands and as Buttermilk Station, is an incorporated
residential community on Interstate Highway 45, State Highway 3, and Farm Roads
519, 1765, 1764 and 2004, some twelve miles northwest of Galveston in northwestern
Galveston County. The community was originally known as Highlands, probably for its
location near Highland Creek, and was renamed in the 1890s when residents learned
of another mainland community of the same name. Madam St. Ambrose, postmistress,
chose the new name, which in French means "the mark."
The community’s post office operated from 1887 until the 1930s. During the Civil
War, the town was known as Buttermilk Station after the soldiers’ practice of
purchasing buttermilk there on the trip between Galveston and Houston. In 1867, the
town had 6 families and its residents raised cattle or rice. The local population rose
from 100 in 1890 to 175 in 1896, when the community had a Baptist church and
several fruit growers. A school with fourteen students existed before 1895, when
Amos Stewart gave land for a larger facility. By 1909, two teachers served an
enrollment of fifty-five students, and in 1913, further construction began.

By 1914, the community had been reached by
four railroads: the International and Great
Northern; the Galveston, Houston and
Henderson; the Missouri, Kansas and Texas; and
the Interurban. At that time, La Marque had
both a railroad station and general store
located in a private home. The town's
population reached 500 in 1914 and 1,500 by
1952, when it had ninety businesses. As it grew
together with nearby Texas City, La Marque
served as a residential community for
employees at nearby Union Carbide plant and
other plants in the La Marque-Texas City area,
as well as the Galveston Island Medical Center.

The Budget Process
The City of La Marque’s Home Rule Charter provides
for the submission of the budget to the City Council by
the City Manager by August 1st. The City’s Fiscal Year
runs from October 1 to September 30.

The following information provides a brief overview of
the steps taken to prepare the City’s annual budget.
Please keep in mind that this is a simplified version of
a complex process that is a combined effort of
members of each of the City’s departments and
requires an extensive period of time to complete. Both
revenues and expenditures are subject to change until
the meeting when the City Council officially adopts
the budget. Likewise, the budget calendar serves as
an approximate timeline and the events may not
always occur at the exact time indicated.

The annual budget is prepared under the direction of
the City Manager. Each department Director
formulates a base budget for each of the departments
included in their division. Base budgets include costs
related to all existing general services, contractual
services and other expenses. Directors also prepare
supplemental requests that provide a separate set of
expenditures required to provide a new service,
increase staffing levels, or purchase new equipment.
Once base and supplemental requests are prepared,
the directors submit their budgets to the Finance
department. The Finance Director calculates
personnel related budgets.

The Finance department compiles the information submitted, along with the revenue estimates, for review by the
City Manager. Meetings are then held with the department directors to review and revise. The proposed budget
document is prepared and then submitted to the City Council by August 1st. Shortly thereafter, a budget workshop
is held for the Staff and City Council to review and discuss the proposed budget. Tax rate calculations and all of
the related notice publications and public hearings, if necessary, occur throughout August and into September. A
Public Hearing on the Budget is held in early September and revisions continue. The final budget is reviewed by
the City Council and adopted in late September.

Legal Debt Margin

Article XI, Section 5 of the Texas Constitution applicable to cities of more than 5,000 population
limits ad valorem tax rates to $2.50 per $100 assessed valuation for cities operating under a
Home Rule Charter.
The legal debt margin amount is important in that it represents the net amount of external
financing resources that is available to the City through the issuance of debt whose repayment
is guaranteed through a pledge of a full faith and credit of the City’s ad valorem taxes.

Financial Policies

Introduction
The Financial Policies are a result of the desire of the City Council and staff to establish a basic framework for the fiscal
management of the City. The policies encompass requirements of the City Charter and the Texas Local Government
Code. The policies provide a format to evaluate the City’s operations and the authoritative procedures by which the City
conducts its financial affairs.
Fiscal Year
The City operates on a fiscal year beginning on October 1 and ending on September 30 of the succeeding year. The
fiscal year will also be established as the accounting and budget year in compliance with the City’s charter.
Budget Administration
The City uses a program/project based budget approach to operating expenditures. Each year every activity, program,
and project is evaluated as new; and costs are analyzed on a line by line basis.
The Annual Budget includes appropriations for all City operating funds. Capital projects funds are presented separately in
a dedicated section. Appropriations for the annual operating budget lapse at the end of each fiscal year. Appropriations
for capital projects carry over until projects completion.
Budget appropriation shall NOT exempt buyer departments from following procurement statutes and Council adopted
Purchasing policy requirements.
Under the City’s budgetary process, outstanding encumbrances at the end of the fiscal year are reported as reservations
of fund balances; they do not constitute expenditures or liabilities.
Control and Accountability – Each Director, appointed by the City Manager, will be responsible for the administration of
his/her departmental budget. This includes accomplishing the Goals and Objectives adopted as part of the budget and
monitoring each department budget for compliance with spending limitations. Directors may transfer funds within the
operations and maintenance or capital line items other than personnel related line items category within a departmental
budget with additional approval of City Manager and Finance Director. Only City Manager and Finance Director can
transfer Personnel line items within each fund. All agenda items impacting City budget including those requiring budget
amendments must be approved by City Manager and verified by Finance Director before submission to City Clerk for
Council packets.

Basis of Accounting and Budgeting

Adopted Annual Budget

The City of La Marque’s annual budgets shall be prepared and adopted on a basis consistent with generally accepted
accounting principles (GAAP) as promulgated by the Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB) for all
governmental and proprietary funds.
Accordingly, all governmental fund budgets are presented using the current resource measurement focus and modified
accrual basis of accounting. Under this method of accounting, revenue and other governmental fund resources are
recognized in the accounting period in which they become susceptible to accrual – that is, when they become both
“measurable and available” to finance current operating expenditures for the fiscal period.
Property tax revenue recognition, “available” means property tax revenue is recognized currently if levied before the fiscal
year end and collected within 60 days after the fiscal year end (considered the availability period).
Sales taxes, franchise fees, hotel occupancy taxes, permit fees, fines and forfeitures, charges for services, and
miscellaneous revenue are recorded as revenue when cash is received.
Revenues
For every annual budget, the City will levy two property tax rates: maintenance & operations and debt service. The debt
service levy shall be sufficient for meeting all principal and interest payments associated with the City’s outstanding taxsupported debt for that budget year. The debt service levy and related expenditures shall be accounted for in the General
Debt Service fund. The maintenance & operations levy shall be accounting for in the General Fund.
Revenues are budgeted conservatively using an objective approach to analyze historical data and inherent trends.
Adjustments are made to account for known events and projected economic activity within the city and surrounding areas.
Revenue from “one-time” or limited duration sources will not be used to pay for recurring expenditures within the City’s
budget.
Restricted revenue shall only be used for the purposes legally permissible and in a fiscally responsible manner.
On an annual basis, the City will set fees and rates at levels to recover total direct and indirect operating costs, including
capital outlay and debt service.
The City will follow an aggressive policy of collecting all revenues by reviewing its receivables annually and implementing
collection procedures to obtain all revenues due to the City in a timely manner.
Expenditures/Expenses
The City will budget, account, and report detailed expenditures in the following categories: Personnel, Supplies,
Maintenance, Services, Debt Service, Capital Outlay, Transfers, and Other Financing Uses.
The City will constantly strive to improve the level of service for its citizens without an increased level of cost. The City will
also seek to reduce the cost of the current level of services provided through innovative programs and initiatives.
Personnel expenditures will reflect the minimum staffing necessary to maintain the established quality and scope of city
services. The City will maintain a market-competitive compensation and benefit package to attract and retain quality
employees.
The City will provide for adequate maintenance of capital assets and for their timely replacement. Each division shall
project future capital requirements for a minimum of five years in order to accommodate the acquisition of capital outlay
while maintaining a consistent level of expenditures for each budget year.
Cash Management/Investment Policies
Investments and cash management will be the responsibility of the Director of Finance.
City funds will be managed in accordance with the prudent person standard with an emphasis on safety of principal,
liquidity, and yield, in that order.
Investments of the City will be made in accordance with the City’s adopted Investment Policy.

The City Council is the government’s highest level of decision-making authority and the formal action that is

expenditures in all City funds. Due to the volatile nature of a majority of its revenues, it is not deemed excessive
for the City to maintain fund balance in the General Fund at levels greater than 33% of the budgeted
operational expenditures. The purpose of this unassigned balance is to alleviate significant unanticipated
budget shortfalls and to ensure the orderly provisions of services to citizens. Should unassigned fund balance
fall below the goal or has a deficiency, the City will seek to reduce expenditure prior to increasing revenues to
replenish fund balance within a reasonable time frame.
The City will try to avoid using fund balances for recurring operational expenditures. To the extent that the
unassigned fund balance exceeds the minimum target, the City may draw upon the fund balance to provide
cash financing for capital projects or other one-time purchases.

ACCOUNT - A term used to identify an individual asset, liability, expenditure control, revenue control,
encumbrance control or fund balance.
ACCRUAL ACCOUNTING - The method of accounting under which revenues are recorded when they are

AD VALOREM TAXES - Real estate and personal property taxes. Ad Valorem is defined by Webster's

at a specified rate.

obligation promissory notes that are backed by approved, irrevocable future tax levies for debt service.
BOND ISSUE - Bonds sold.
BUDGET - A plan of financial operation embodying an estimate of proposed expenditures for a given

BUDGET ORDINANCE - The official enactment by the City Council establishing the legal authority for

TRANSFER FROM GENERAL FUND - Transfer of funds from the City's General Fund.
TRANSFER FROM HOTEL/MOTEL FUND - Transfer of funds from the City's Hotel/Motel Fund.
TRANSFER FROM GCWA FUND - A transfer from the Gulf Coast Water Authority to the General Fund.
This amount represents cost recovery for services rendered the fund by other City operations.
TRANSFER FROM UTILITY FUND - A transfer from the Utility Fund to the General Fund. This amount
represents cost recovery for services rendered the fund by other City operations as well as a payment
in lieu of ad valorem taxes.
WARRANT FEES - Proceeds from fees that may be imposed for the issuance and service of arrest
warrants issued out of municipal court.
WATER METERS - Proceeds from the installation of water meters.
WATER RECONNECT FEES - Proceeds from a charge to utility customers to have their water
reconnected after service has been temporarily disconnected for non-payment of services rendered.
WATER TAPS - Proceeds from a user fee charged to utility customers who desire to be connected to
our water system at a location that did not previously have service.
WRECKER PERMITS - Proceeds from individuals desiring a permit to operate a wrecker service in the
City.
