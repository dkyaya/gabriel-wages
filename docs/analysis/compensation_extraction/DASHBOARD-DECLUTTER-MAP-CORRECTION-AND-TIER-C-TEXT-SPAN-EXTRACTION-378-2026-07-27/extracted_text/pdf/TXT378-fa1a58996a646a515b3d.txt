FINAL ORDER
Lower Swatara Township (Township) filed timely exceptions and a
supporting brief with the Pennsylvania Labor Relations Board (Board) on
June 25, 2018, from a Nisi Order of Certification (NOC) issued on June
4, 2018.1 The NOC certified Teamsters Local Union No. 776, affiliated
with the International Brotherhood Of Teamsters (Teamsters), as the
exclusive representative of “[a]ll full-time and regular part-time
police officers including but not limited to detectives, sergeants,
school resource officers and patrolmen; and excluding the Chief of
Police and other managerial employes[,]” for purposes of collective
bargaining under Act 111 of 1968, as read in pari materia, with the
Pennsylvania Labor Relations Act (PLRA).2 The Teamsters filed a response
to the exceptions on July 13, 2018.
The Teamsters filed a Petition for Representation with the Board
pursuant to the PLRA and Act 111 on January 30, 2018, seeking an
investigation into a question of representation of all full-time and
regular part-time police officers employed by the Township.3 On February
12, 2018, the Secretary of the Board issued an Order and Notice of
Hearing (ONH).
Based on stipulations of fact before the Hearing Examiner, the
Township argued that the petitioned-for police officers would function
as security guards for the Township within the meaning of Section
604(3) of the Public Employe Relations Act (PERA), and therefore the
police officers could not be represented by the Teamsters. Section 604
of PERA provides, in relevant part, as follows:

Following the submission of post-hearing briefs, the Hearing
Examiner issued an Order Directing Submission of Eligibility List
(ODSEL) on April 26, 2018. In the ODSEL, the Hearing Examiner held
that, based on the holdings in Union Township, 25 PPER ¶ 25198 (Final
Order, 1994) and Commonwealth of Pennsylvania v. PLRB and FOP, 441 A.2d
470 (Pa. Cmwlth. 1982), aff’d in relevant part, 502 Pa. 7, 463 A.2d 409
(1983), police officers under Act 111 are not security guards under
PERA, and thus may be represented by an employe organization that
represents non-security guard employes. See 43 P.S. §1101.604(3).
On exceptions, the Township raises the same arguments presented
to the Hearing Examiner that under Section 604(3) of PERA, the
Teamsters cannot represent the police officers for purposes of Act 111,
because the police officers function as security guards for the
Township, and the Teamsters represent non-security guard employes of
the Township. The Board has thoroughly reviewed the April 26, 2018
ODSEL and the analysis therein, and finds no error. The Finding of
Facts of the Hearing Examiner are supported by substantial evidence,
and the Board adopts the Hearing Examiner’s discussion and reliance on
Union Township and Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.4 The Hearing Examiner’s
Conclusions of Law on the issues are not in error. Hence, the
Township’s exceptions thereto are without merit.
After a thorough review of the exceptions and all matters of
record, the Board herein adopts and incorporates the discussion and
analysis of the Hearing Examiner set forth in the ODSEL. The Findings
