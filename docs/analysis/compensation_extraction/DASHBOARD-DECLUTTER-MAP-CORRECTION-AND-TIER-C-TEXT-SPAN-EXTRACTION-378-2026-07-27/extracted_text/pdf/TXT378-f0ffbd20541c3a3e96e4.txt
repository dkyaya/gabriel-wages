RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS
The Administrative Code, Section § 5-209 defines public participation at Council meetings.
1.
Citizens who wish to make public comment must pre-register following the instructions in
Agenda #3 Public Comment on this agenda.
2.
Citizens attending Council meetings are expected to conduct themselves in a responsible and
respectful manner that does not disrupt the meeting.
3.
Those wishing to have conversations should do so in the hall outside Council Chambers in a
low speaking voice.
4.
Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the agenda.
Comment must be directed to Council as a body and not to any individual Council member
or public or elected official in attendance. Clapping, calling out, and/or cheering when a
speaker finishes his comments is not permitted.
5.
Citizens may not approach the Council tables at any time during the meeting.
6.
Any person making threats of any type, personally offensive or impertinent remarks or any
person becoming unruly while addressing Council may be called to order by the Presiding
Officer and may be barred from speaking, removed from Council Chambers and/or cited.

7.

Failure to abide by these regulations could result in your removal from Council Chambers
and/or a citation. These regulations are meant to avoid disruptions at the meeting and they
are not meant to interfere with public participation.

Sheila Perez, no address provided, stated that when she registered she thought the Parking Stall
program was an agenda topic. Council President Reed stated that the Parking Stall topic is not
included on tonight’s agenda and as there is no topic listed where Ms. Perez registered to speak, she
will be provided with three (3) minutes to comment.
Ms. Perez expressed the belief that District 5 residents disagree with the parking stall initiative and
believe that this initiative will not resolve the parking stress. She stated that State law prohibits
parking fines where a remedy, such as a garage parking, is not available. She noted that Reading’s
fines and fees are significantly higher than other Home Rule cities.
Evelyn Morrison, no address provided, expressed her point of view on Council’s inability to make
an appointment to the vacant District 5 seat. She described her conversations with various City
officials about the need to correct the 2023 General Fund and Capital budgets through ordinances.
She stated that Bill 24 has a number of entries and one of them corrects an $11M error. She
questioned how that large amount was not identified during the budget review process. She noted
that these budgets were not balanced when they were adopted. She thanked the Auditor and
Controller for identifying these errors and working to correct them.
Tyler Simmons, of Penn Street, stated that his sneaker business “The Plug” started in the 200 block
of Penn Street and recently relocated to a larger venue in the 500 block of Penn Street. He explained
that he believed that moving to a larger store in the heart of the commercial core would be beneficial
but he quickly learned about the drawbacks created by the lack of parking, the homeless population
and the City’s lack of initiative to improve the commercial core. He expressed the belief that
although the mayor has interest in driving business to the downtown, the Parking Authority is
driving business and customers away. He expressed the belief that there is too much talk and too
little innovation. He noted the importance small businesses have on the economy and he expressed
the belief that the City is not supporting businesses by being satisfied with an environment that is
stuck in the 1990s.
The auditor described her discussion about Bill 24 with Ms. Morrison.
The finance director stated that the errors in the General Fund and Capital budgets were identified a
week after the budgets were adopted.

The finance director stated that a draft plan of Downtown initiatives will be reviewed with Council in
late March, after a Downtown Coordinator is hired.

8

Councilor Ventura noted her recognition of the problems experienced by small businesses and the need
to provide support to businesses, especially those in the downtown.

The Council President suggested that the City conduct a downtown summit to provide all stakeholders
with the ability to identify potential solutions for the downtown. She noted that the City-owned
properties at 5th and Penn that have been vacant for over 10 years are a major part of the problem. She
stressed the need for the administration to address the reuse of these building to restore the vibrancy of
the downtown. She added that Council has not been updated on the RFP to sell the properties for quite
some time. She noted the need for the administration to address these vacant properties and obtain
public input about the restoration of the downtown including an innovative solution to the parking
issues. She asked the Council Solicitor and City Solicitor to compare our parking regulations for the
downtown against those in Lancaster and Allentown, as those downtowns are thriving. That research
can help drive a solution.
Councilor Ventura suggested considering a 20 minute grace period or a one (1) hour parking limit.
Councilor Miller noted that Mr. Simmons’ business is in District 6 and he invited him to meet to discuss
the downtown. He stated that several downtown business owners have been in contact with him about
their viewpoints for downtown solutions.
Councilor Goodman-Hinnershitz noted her appreciation for input on downtown solutions, noting that
she has worked in the downtown since she began her career. She stressed the need to obtain input from
all those who own businesses and work for downtown employers. She noted the need to identify
solutions that will inspire downtown workers to leave their offices during their lunch periods.
Councilor Butler thanked Mr. Simmons for stepping forward to speak at tonight’s meeting.

instead meets only when the need arises. She reported on the Board’s activities since their 2020 report
to Council:
• 2 dismissed complaints by the Investigative Officer in 2022 due to lack of jurisdiction
• the Board issued five advisory opinions:
o 2020-2 – found that the City may issue a cash prize to the winner of the City’s logo contest
o 2021-1 – found that there is no conflict of interest for a sitting Council member whose
business is located within the DID to vote on the re-establishment of DID
o 2021-2 – found that members of the community who work for organizations that compete
with the City may serve as members of a BAC but that the member must be aware of
potential conflicts of interest
o 2022-1 – found that City officials and employees may not receive Royals tickets due to the
relationship between the City and the Convention Center Authority

16

The Council Solicitor noted that the topic can be considered at the COW and any nominations can be
considered at the regular meeting that follows.

o 2022-2 – found that there is no prohibition against the hiring of a sitting Council member’s
immediate family as long as no Council action is required as part of the hiring process. The
Board noted the potential for conflicts of interest.
Ms. Eggert stated that the Board recommended an amendment to the Ethics Code requiring the
filing of the annual local Statement of Financial Interest form and added questions regarding the use
of City vehicles, City credit cards, the ownership or management of businesses that do business
with the City, and donations made to City election campaigns. The local form is distributed with
the PA Ethics Commission’s annual Financial Interest statement. Both forms are to be completed
and returned to the City Clerk’s office by May 1.

Ms. Eggert noted that the Board has adopted a policy limiting the scope of investigations to the
information contained in the complaint and is currently drafting policies to begin enforcement of
required campaign finance reports and enforcing the non-filing of the Supplemental Statement of
Financial Interest. She thanked its Solicitor, Ed Stock; Investigative Officer, Jim Smith; and staff
liaison, Shelly Smith for their dedication and professionalism.
In response to a question from Councilor Goodman-Hinnershitz, Ms. Eggert stated that the best way
to assure that employees and officials are properly trained on ethics is to have training sessions. She
recalled the training session done by the last administration which was a joint project with the
Greater Reading Chamber Alliance.
Council President Reed thanked Ms. Eggert and the Board for their work.

[Added 7-24-2017 by Ord. No. 73-2017; Amended 5-9-2022 by Ord. No. 43-2022]
A. No person shall park or leave an unattended vehicle on private property without the consent of the
owner or person in control or possession of the property except in case of emergency or disablement of the
vehicle, in which case the operator shall arrange for the removal of the vehicle as soon as possible.

the public about the restriction. For purposes of this section, “private parking lot” shall mean a parking lot
open to the public or used for parking without charge; or a parking lot used for parking with charge.

hours of darkness so as to be readily visible to an ordinarily observant driver.

is prohibited unless restrictions are posted in accordance with this subsection.

have removed an unattended vehicle by a privately operated towing business if (i) the vehicle in violation of
this section is reported to the City of Reading Police Department by the property owner, or person in control
or possession of the property; (ii) a citation is issued to the vehicle by the City of Reading Police Department;
and (iii) the property owner provides the City of Reading Police Department with a valid contract permitting
the privately operated towing business to conduct business on the private property or, in lieu thereof, the
private property owner has, prior thereto, provided the City of Reading Police Department with documentation
indicating the private property that the privately operated towing business has authority to conduct business at
along with a copy of the fully signed contract between the private property owner and the privately operated
towing business. Any towing company contracted to remove vehicles from private property shall not charge
more than $100 for towing, $50 for "dropping" the vehicle on site with no tow and no more than $10 a day for

Each employee in this unit may use a total of three (3) sick leave days per year for

Employees shall be allowed twelve (12) sick leave days per year which shall be
accumulated at the rate of one (1) day per working month. However, no sick leave shall be
accumulated unless an employee works at least half (1/2) the days during the month, when an

supervisor not less than one-half (1/2) hour before the scheduled starting time for that days’ work.

will be able to return to work. Human Resources may also require that an employee returning from
sick leave supply a physician's certificate that the employee is sufficiently recovered from the
illness which caused the absence to return to work.

sanctions to control sick leave use. The City will meet and discuss the creation of said policy with
the Union, however, the final policy is at the discretion of the City with consideration for the

ARTICLE XXI - GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE
Section 1.
Any grievance or dispute which may arise concerning the application, meaning or
interpretation of this Agreement shall be settled in the following manner:
Step 1: The Union shall present in writing the grievance to the grievant's immediate
supervisor and a copy to the Human Resources Office within fifteen (15) calendar
days of its occurrence or knowledge thereof. The supervisor shall respond in fifteen
(15) calendar days.
Step 2: If the grievance remains unsettled, the Union within fifteen (15) calendar
days of the receipt of the supervisors answer, shall in writing appeal the grievance
to the Human Resources Office, who shall have fifteen (15) calendar days to
respond.
Step 3: If the grievance remains unsettled the Union within fifteen (15) calendar
days of the receipt of the Human Resources Director's answer, shall appeal the
grievance to the Mayor or their designee. The Mayor or their designee shall have
fifteen (15) calendar days to respond.
Step 4: The Union shall have the right to demand arbitration through the American
Arbitration Association within forty-five (45) calendar days of the date of the
receipt of the decision of the Mayor or their designee but not thereafter. If
arbitration is demanded it shall be as provided by statute with the American
Arbitration Association as arbitrating agency. The cost of arbitration will be shared
