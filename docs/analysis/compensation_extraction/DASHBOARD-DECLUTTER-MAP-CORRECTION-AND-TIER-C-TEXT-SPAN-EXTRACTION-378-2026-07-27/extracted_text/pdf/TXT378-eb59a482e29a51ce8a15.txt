Dear Mayor Bagwell and Members of City Council:
I am pleased to present to the citizens of Easley, members of City Council, City employees, and other
interested readers the 2018-19 operating budget for the City of Easley, South Carolina.
The projected revenues for all funds is $17,351,750 including a General Fund budget of $13,783,750. This
represents an overall budgetary increase of 3.4% and a General Fund increase of 2.0% over the adopted
2017-18 operating budget, respectively. However, the proposed FY 18-19 General Fund budget is 2% less
that the FY 17-18 budget when you remove revenues from Lease Purchases in both years. This is due to the
budget showing $550,000 budgeted for a new fire engine. A summary of all funds outlining the changes is
included at the end of the Executive Summary.
The City of Easley is in the process of creating a Strategic Plan to guide the future growth and direction of
the city. The 2018-19 Budget addresses three (3) Themes that council have identified during this process:
• Quality Services
• Quality Places
• Quality Community
The FY 18-19 budget period has the potential to be a start to a transformative period for the citizens,
businesses, and organizations of Easley. There are three (3) primary funding sources that will provide the
funding to address the priorities of the community and offer the city opportunities to partner with public
and private partners.
Tax Increment Finance Bond
The City of Easley was able to partner with Pickens County and the School District of Pickens County to
extend the Downtown Tax Increment Finance District for ten (10) additional years. Additional areas of
potential development were added to expand the district and allow for greater impact. The bond tied to
the extension, and fund balance from the expiring bond payments, will provide the City with approximately
$1.8 million to invest in the Downtown during FY 18-19. Council and Staff are working to prioritize projects,
establish scope, and design the projects currently, but the majority of work will take place during the FY
18-19 fiscal year. This Bond will be tied to the TIF District, and will be paid in full during last fiscal year of
the TIF District, FY 2028-29.

General Obligation Bond
The City of Easley has experienced growth of almost 40% over the last 25 years. This has added demand to
service delivery and created challenges to delivering quality services. Fire Station #1 and the Public Works
facilities have both aged, maxed out space requirements, and are located in areas that provide challenges
to service delivery. The City is currently exploring the idea of relocating these facilities.
Fire Station #1 would be moved from Highway 8 where the current facility is maxed out on space and
lifespan of the building, and where traffic growth on Hwy 8 has made simply entering and exiting the
property a challenge. The Public Works campus would be relocated from Downtown where it is out of place
in the business district, aged out of current facilities, and would open up space for development.
It is proposed to issue $5.5 million in General Obligation Bonds in FY 18-19 for the construction of these
new facilities. The City has reduced our debt payments from $830,000 in FY 15-16 to an estimated payment
of $275,000 in FY 18-19. This provides the flexibility in the General Fund to take on these payment, and
still keep our debt payments below the FY 15-16 level. It is estimated that we would peak at $612,000 in
FY 19-20, and then level off between $500,000 - $550,000 in the five (5) years following. The City would
be required to leverage 3 mills of the tax rate for the bond issuance. The FY 18-19 budget recommends
increasing the millage from 65 to 68 mills to address this requirement.
Hospitality Bond
The City of Easley completed a Parks and Recreation Master Plan in 2017 and is now looking at the
implementation of the plan’s recommendations. The top priorities that were identified in the plan were
to update/improve the current facilities, look at the adding rectangle multi-use fields, and to increase the
amount of passive recreation throughout the City.
Council and Staff are currently working to create a priority list for updating and improving the current park
facilities. Options on how to include additional open space, free play spaces, and additional rectangle
fields are also being explored. Staff is also working on the Brushy Creek Greenway and Nalley Brown Nature
Preserve to compliment the Doodle Trail and park system while adding additional passive trail systems
throughout the City.
The Hospitality Fund has seen a continued growth from $1.5 million in FY 13-14 to being on pace to top $2
million in FY 17-18. This growth has allowed bonds to be issued from the construction of the Doodle Trail
and Downtown connection. The Bond for the Bagwell Gym is also expiring and will reduce our bond
payments by over $500,000 per year. Between the growth in revenue and expiring debt, the City has the
opportunity to issue bonds the first part of 2019 to implement the Master Plan and address projects that
may arise.

There are indeed exciting opportunities on the horizon, but we are also tasked with providing quality
services day to day. This requires us to address the major aspects of our operations - Employees and

Employees
Employees are the backbone of the City and help to dictate the quality of services provided to the
residences. One of the goals in this budget is to ensure that the City can compete for the highest quality
of candidates, and then continue to keep them. To this end, the 2018-19 budget includes:
• A 1% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for all full and part-time employees effective July 1,
2018-19. All pay bands will be increased to reflect.
• Continues with the State Health Plan to keep the City’s and employees cost lower than in
previous years.
• Absorbing the proposed 1% increase to the employer’s contribution portion of the State
Retirement System.
• Incorporates a new pay scale for our firefighters to ensure competitive salaries and reflect
increases tied to long term training opportunities. This will help to ensure that we keep the
highest trained and tenured employee at the City.
There were two topics that arose during budget meetings with Department Heads and committee meetings
with Councilmembers that are not addressed in this budget, but need to be discussed in the future:
• A City-wide longevity policy and corresponding one-time bonus or pay increase to recognize
those that dedicate their career to the City.
• An education reimbursement policy to help employees that seek to further their education by
covering a portion of their costs.
Both of these items would need to be discussed with Council, drafted, and then adopted as part of the
Personnel Manual.
Health Insurance / Wellness
In January 1, 2017 the City moved to the State Health Insurance Plan through SC PEBA. This has improved
the benefits offered to employees and provided an even larger savings to the City in the long run. The City
is looking at a .04% increase in January 2019. This is a negligible increase and pales in comparison to the
20-30% increases we were looking at before changing.

The budget recommends continuing funding the gym membership reimbursement initiative for full-time
employees wishing to participate. The plan states that any full-time employee that visits a participating
gym (as approved by the wellness committee) 36 times a quarter (January – March, April – June, July –
September, and October – December) will receive up to $75 per quarter depending on the gym they chose.
$7,500 is budgeted for this initiative.
The City is continuing to see the benefit of an on-site Nurse Practitioner 8 hours a week. This partnership
with Greenville Hospital System has provided the basis for our wellness screenings and counseling.
Employees have someone on site to visit for minor ailments, and this has saved numerous lost hours of
productivity. They also provide flu shots and annual screenings for employees.
New Positions
During FY 17-18, City Council approved the creation of a Planning and Project Manager position. This
position was filled in January 2018 and is reflected in the FY 18-19 budget.
The FY 18-19 budget also calls for the creation of one (1) position in the Cemetery/Grounds Maintenance
department. This is to create a grounds maintenance and beautification position. This will be paid for
through the $60,000 that we were paying a contractor to maintain a few medians across the city.
Equipment Needs
Police

In a typical year, the city tries to replace five police
cars. As general practice police cars should be replaced
more frequently than your average vehicle. Due to the
nature of the job police cruisers are driven much more
aggressively than your typical vehicle which lends
itself to more wear and tear on the car. This activity
necessitates their replacement more frequently.
The
current replacement of five (5) cars per year puts the
entire fleet at a ten (10) year replacement cycle.
The Police Chief and I have been researching options to
shorten this cycle and have found an option that we
support. We are proposing a capital lease option through
Enterprise Fleet Management.
This option would see
every vehicle replaced after 3 years of service and
provide an estimated savings of $175,000 during the
first three (3) years. The estimated cost through the
first six (6) years of the lease is $55,000 higher than
if we budgeted at the current level during the same time
period. This also assumes that we will get the lowest
estimated amounts for the vehicles that we sale.
We
would anticipate that the $55,000 would be the highest
amount we would see.

The FY 18-19 budget reflects the reorganizing of the Finance and City Council Departments. The City

• $10,000 to replace aging Christmas Decorations in the Downtown
• $36,575 for tourism related activities and events throughout the community
• $113,000 for Stormwater system improvements throughout the community
Demolitions
The past few years, the city has funded the demolition of sub-standard structures within the city. It was a
point of focus by City Council to try and reverse address this concern. Thus, $50,000 was included in the
FY 2014-15, 15-16, and 16-17 budgets to begin taking care of the backlog. Due to the progress that has
been made, this budget decreased the amount to $30,000 in FY 17-18. The FY 18-19 budget holds this
amount at $30,000. This will allow the City to continue to address this issue.

Property taxes comprise about 19% of all General Fund revenues. The tax levy on a piece of property is
determined by market value, assessment ratio, and millage rate. Market value is determined by the Pickens
County Tax Assessor's Office using a variety of factors such as size, condition, location, and recent selling
prices of comparable properties. Assessment ratio is a percentage which is multiplied by the appraised
market value of a property to determine the assessed value. Owner-occupied residences are assessed at
4%, commercial properties at 6%, motor vehicles at 6%, personal property at 10%, and industrial and utilities
at 10.5%. The Property tax rate for FY 18-19 is proposed to increase from 65 mills to 68 mills. The 3-millage
increase will be pledged for the GO Bond payment for construction of the new Public Works facility and
Fire Station 1/Fire Administrative Office. The table below shows the tax calculations for the 2015-2017 tax
years and shows the estimated tax rate for 2018. This 2018 number is based on the City’s tax credit
adjusting based on the past 5-year average change. This would provide an estimated $3.50 increase in a
$150,000 home. The City will not know out LOST discount rate until after the budget is adopted.

Revenue for the 1% Local Option Sales Tax collected. 100% of the LOST revenues are used to defray property

tax bills due to the increase in the discount provided by Local Option Sales Tax (LOST) not quite canceling
the millage increase. The rebound in sales has led to the increased LOST discount, lower property tax
revenues, and higher Hospitality Tax collections. The LOST income is expected to account for 19% of the
General Fund budget in FY 18-19.

Licenses, permits and fees represent the City’s revenue source comprising approximately 22% of all
General Fund revenues. The most significant source is the business license tax, which a levy is based upon
each $1,000 of gross receipts, sales, or premiums of business conducted within the corporate limits of the
City. The projection of $3,092,500 in business license receipts (excluding franchise fees) is a slight increase
from the 2017-18 forecast of $2,978,500. This is based on an increase in Business License revenues and
Insurance tax collected by the MASC.
Franchise fees comprise about 19% of all General Fund revenues. Revenues in this category include
franchise fees for electric, gas, and cable television. The rate structure is based upon a certain percentage
of gross receipts in each case, and the basis of each forecast is trend analysis along with current market
conditions. Projected revenues from all franchise fees for FY 18-19 are $2,543,500 and represents this
revenue source decreasing $22,000 from the 2017-18 figure of $2,565,500. The primary reason for the
decrease is revenue from Fort Hill Natural Gas. This is based on the mild winter weather that we have seen
the past two years.
Intergovernmental revenue includes grants and allocations from county, state, and federal governments.
State-shared revenue is generally distributed on a pro-rata basis per population or other set formula. The
Aid to Subdivision revenue is distributed quarterly from the Local Government Fund and funded by a transfer
of 4.5% of state general fund revenues. Counties receive 83.28% and municipalities 16.72% of the
distribution. The revenue forecast from Aid to Subdivisions is expected to be $435,000 for 2018-19. This
reflects the city receiving over $166,000 less than what is mandated by statute. The budget for the State
has not been completed, but this budget assumes that the State will continue to not fully fund the Local
Government Fund. Currently our short fall is more than $1,000,000 over the past 6 years.

Special Revenue Funds
Special revenue funds are used to account for the proceeds of specific revenue sources that are restricted
by law or administrative action to expenditures for specific purposes. Annual budgets are established for
the following special revenue funds: Community Development Administration, Hospitality Tax,
Accommodations Tax, Local Accommodations Tax, Victim Rights, Law Enforcement Fund, Fire Fund and
Recreation Fund.
Community Development Administration
The Community Development Fund is used to record receipts and expenditures of Community Development
Block Grant funds. $14,000 in revenue is budgeted for 2018-19 and $26,500 in expenses are budgeted. The
largest part of expenses is $23,000 to pay the remainder of the Main Street Challenges that have been
awarded.
Hospitality Tax
Through an Ordinance passed in 2000, the City enacted the levy of one percent (1%) local hospitality tax on
prepared meals and beverages in the City. In 2005 through an amendment the City increased the levy to a
maximum two percent (2%). The proceeds are to be used for tourist related improvements and facilities.
The collection and use of the tax is governed by Article 7, “Local Hospitality Tax,” of Chapter 1, Title 6 of
the South Carolina Code of Laws.
The 2018-19 budget estimates revenue of $1,900,000 and includes $1,134,619 in eligible Parks and
Recreation expenditures to assist in funding maintenance and upkeep at the J.B. “Red” Owens Recreation
Complex and Larry D. Bagwell gymnasium. These funds also include the costs associated with putting on
the City’s Athletics and Recreational events. In addition, $741,881 is budgeted for debt service on the 2010
Bagwell Gym, 2014 Doodle Trail, and 2016 Doodle Trail bonds.
The 2018-19 Hospitality tax budget also includes $95,000 for facilities repairs and improvement, plus an
additional $132,500 for Special Events.
Accommodations Tax
The Accommodations Tax Fund is used to record the proceeds and allocations of a 2% tax imposed on all
accommodations by the State. Revenue collected from the tax is divided among localities based on the
amount of revenue generated within each jurisdiction.
The proceeds must be spent for tourism promotion and related expenditures. Revenues of $80,000 are
estimated for 2018-19. Following transfers of $27,750 to the General Fund and $15,675 to the Easley
Chamber of Commerce, the remaining balance is estimated to be $36,575. Of this discretionary amount,
the Accommodations Tax Advisory Committee will make a recommendation to the City Council for the
allocation of these funds. This recommendation usually comes to Council in November in the form of a
Budget Amendment.

Local Accommodations Tax
The Accommodation Fee represents a 1.5% levy that is collected from accommodation facilities located
within the City’s jurisdiction. This tax is very similar to the State Accommodations Tax and is levied on
every person engaged in the business of furnishing accommodations for transients within the jurisdiction of
the City. The uses of the money are very similar to the State Accommodations Tax and Hospitality Taxes
in that the monies can only be used for tourism related purposes.
The City is budgeting $75,000 in revenue and $75,000 in expenditures to help advertise, promote, and
market the City with focus on the Recreation Department and City Events that bring people into the City.
The goal is to attract tournaments, promote our programs, and increase use of our facilities.
Law Enforcement Fund
The Law Enforcement Fund is separated into two portions, a restricted holding account and an unrestricted
account. Funds in the restricted holding account are monies that the City has secured through arrests and
must hold until the case in question is disposed. If the defendant is found to be innocent or not guilty the
monies in question will be returned. If the defendant is found guilty the monies may be transferred to the
unrestricted account and can be used by the Police department for law enforcement activities with approval
from City Council. Monies in the Law Enforcement Fund must be used for Police related activities.
The 2018-19 budget includes $200,000 in funding to assist in purchases to support the drug enforcement of
the Police Department. The funds can potentially be used for vehicles, cameras, equipment, or other items
to support operations.

Enterprise Funds
Enterprise funds are used to account for operations that are financed and operated in a manner similar to
private business enterprises. The intent is that the costs of providing goods or services to the general
public and other local governments on a continuing basis will be financed or recovered primarily through
user charges. Annual budgets are established for Stormwater Management.
Stormwater Management
Stormwater fees are levied to support the stormwater management program. The fee is based on
impervious surface areas of residential and commercial property. The flat rate structure is proposed to be
$28 per year for both developed residential and for equivalent residential units of developed
commercial/industrial property. The 2018-19 revenue estimate is $650,000, with $425,000 coming from
fees and $225,000 coming from lease purchase financing.
Budgeted expenditures for 18-19 are $600,000, with $150,000 budgeted to be transferred to the General
Fund. These funds include $75,000 to cover the cost of the city’s Stormwater Engineer position that is
under the Street Department. $80,000 goes to offset the staff and equipment cost to work on the
stormwater projects and issues throughout the year. There is also $113,000 funded for drainage
improvements and projects, $2,500 in permit fees, and $5,000 for public education.
The Stormwater Fund has been repaying the General Fund back over $1,000,000 that was used on the Oak
Creek Stormwater Project. This is expected to be paid down to under $100,000 after FY 17-18 and is
recommended to be forgiven. This will allow the Stormwater Fund to begin making needed repairs to the
system.

Debt Service Funds
Debt Service Funds are established to account for the accumulation of resources for, and the payment of,
general long-term debt principal and interest payments. Under state law, municipalities can only issue two
forms of long-term debt: general obligation bonds and revenue bonds. The City of Easley has used tax
increment bonds as a means of financing capital improvements, and these debt instruments are also
classified as a form of revenue bonds. The debt service payments associated with the Certificates of
Participation, Series 2006, are accounted for in the Hospitality Tax Special Revenue fund.

ACCOMMODATIONS TAX
Special Revenue Fund
By a 1984 Act of the General Assembly, a two percent (2%) tax is imposed on all accommodations in
the State of South Carolina. These monies are collected by the State and distributed to counties and
municipalities. The proceeds must be spent for tourism promotion and tourism related expenditures.
Projects are reviewed by an Accommodations Tax Advisory Committee.
State law provides that (a) the first $25,000 must be allocated to the City’s General Fund for general
purpose use; (b) five percent (5%) of the balance must also be allocated to the City’s General Fund;
(c) thirty percent (30%) of the balance must be allocated for the purpose of advertising and promotion
of tourism; and (d) the remaining balance must be used for tourism related expenditures.

STORMWATER MANAGEMENT
Enterprise Fund
In 1991, under the Storm Water Management and Sediment Reduction Act, authority was granted to
local governments to establish a stormwater utility to fund such activities as watershed master
planning, facility retrofitting and facility maintenance. In 2004 Easley City Council adopted a
permanent fee, with the rate structure based on impervious surface areas of individual parcels. A
stormwater management fund was established in FY 2003-04 to record the receipts and expenditures
related to the stormwater management program. The fund is classified as an Enterprise Fund to comply
with GASB 34 reporting standards.
Stormwater Administration and Planning is responsible for developing watershed master plans,
coordinating improvements to the City’s watershed basins, and complying with state/federal mandates
related to stormwater management and water quality design.

TAX INCREMENT FUND
Debt Service Fund
The Tax Increment Fund was established in 2003 to account for the retirement of principal and interest
on tax increment revenue bonds issued by the City for various improvements in the downtown
redevelopment area. The bonds are repaid from the incremental taxes produced by the incremental
increase in assessed property values within the tax increment district. In 2004, a $640,000 tax
increment improvement bond was issued to finance improvements in the downtown redevelopment
area. In 2006, a $1,050,000 another tax increment improvement bond was issued to finance additional
improvements. In 2010, a $1,203,000 tax increment improvement bond was issued to defease the 2004
and 2006 issues respectively. This bonding expires in FY 17-18.
During FY 17-18 the City worked with Pickens County and the School District of Pickens County to
extend the TIF for ten (10) additional years. The City will be issuing between $1.5 and $1.6 million in
debt tied to the TIF extension. The payments will run from FY 18-19 through FY 28-29.

APPENDIX – C
Glossary
Accrual Accounting - A basis of accounting in which revenues are recognized when earned and expenses when
incurred.
Activity - The smallest unit of budgetary accountability which defines specific and distinguishable lines of
work performed by a department or division for the purpose of accomplishing a particular service.
Appropriation - An authorization made by City Council to incur obligations against and to make expenditures
of governmental resources. Appropriations are usually made for fixed amounts and are typically granted for
a one year period, except in the instance of capital projects where they are granted for the duration of the
project.
Assessed Valuation - A valuation set upon real estate or other property by the County Assessor as a basis for
levying taxes.
Assessment Ratio - A percentage which is multiplied by the appraised market value of a property to determine
the assessed value.
Budget - A plan of financial operation embodying an estimate of proposed expenditures for a given period of
time and the proposed means of financing them.
Capital Improvement Program (CIP) - A schedule of capital expenditures to be incurred over a five-year
period to carry out the City's program of public improvements.
Capital Budget - The first year of the CIP as approved by City Council.
Capital Improvement Project - An item whose construction or other acquisition represents a physical
improvement to the community and adds to the total physical worth of the City. The improvement should
have a useful life of not less than 15 years, and normally will exceed $200,000 in total cost.
Capital Outlay - In the operating budget, refers to any item which has an expected useful life of greater than
3 years and an estimated unit cost of $500 or more.
CBD - Central Business District.
CDA - Community Development Administration.
CDBG - Community Development Block Grant.
CPI - Continuous Process Improvement.
Debt Service - Payment of interest and principal on an obligation resulting from the issuance of bonds.
DHEC - Department of Health and Environmental Control.
DOT - Department of Transportation.
ECU – Easley Combined Utilities.
