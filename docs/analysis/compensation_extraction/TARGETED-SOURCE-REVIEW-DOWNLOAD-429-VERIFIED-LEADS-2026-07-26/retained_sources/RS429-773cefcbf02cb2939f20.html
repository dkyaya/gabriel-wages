Bristol Twp. Plans to Hire More Police Officers

August 29, 2014

·

By

Tom Sofield & Jeff Bohen

More police officers will be on the streets of Bristol Township in the coming months.

Bristol Township Manager Bill McCauley confirmed to LevittownNow.com that the community plans to hire new officers and recall two. With the recalled officers and new hiring, the township police force will jump from 58 to 61.

Advertisements


The extra officers are already included in the budget, McCauley said. He added that the two recalled officers have to be brought back before the township can hire through the civil service commission.

While township officials hope to have the new cops on the streets in the coming months, McCauley said the timing depends on if the new hires would have to still complete the police academy.

Township police have seen their numbers decrease over the past several years. When an officer left, they were not always replaced.

Advertisements


Earlier this year, Acting Chief John Godzieba called two officers back from federal task forces and put them back on the streets to supplement patrol units. In July, the two-officer narcotics unit was disbanded and those officers were placed back on patrol.

McCauley said at a recent township council meeting that police are working more overtime to cover shifts. Despite staffing, crime was down in the past few years and response times were below national averages.

Advertisements


“Even though we have fewer officers, those officers are working more hours,” he said at last week’s meeting.

McCauley said the township has several more officers than it did when it hit a low of 54 sworn personnel in 1990s.

Detective Tim Fuhrman, who is head of the police department’s union, outlined staffing arraignments and what he says they mean for residents.

An “ideal” number of Officers would be 75 to 80. That way patrol squads, criminal investigation divisions, and specialty units could be fully staffed, with enough manpower to join federal task forces which sometimes brings government funding to the police department.

An “adequate” number of Officers would be 67 to 73. Staffing levels would meet minimum goals, but proactive patrols and investigations could begin.

If only resident safety and reactionary responses are the desires of the Township, then a minimum of 64 Officers would be needed. This number of 64 Officers was a long standing minimum, but the Township fought hard in Act 111 Interest Arbitration to have this provision removed and were victorious, enabling them to lower Police staffing to dangerous levels.

Advertisements


In response to residents comments before council about raising police staff levels to 78, McCauley said having a police force that big would be “financially devastating” for the township.

Bristol Township police have faced staffing problems at periods through the past 50 years, many times funding was blamed for short staffing.

Advertisements



Currently, Bristol Township has about $81 million in unfunded liabilities. The township manager told LevittownNow.com in July that since the department won its contract arbitration case with the township,which included across the board raises and no health insurance contributions, the township didn’t have the money to hire more cops. McCauley has blamed much of the unfunded debt on employee costs.

Recently, the township lost a $189,000 a year contract with with the county housing authority to provide fixed police protection of the Venice Ashby public housing development.

Advertisements


The township council last week approved to put aside $50,000 dollars to install police cameras in Winder Village and other sections. The cameras are one way, according to officials, the township can help add extra eyes on the street. The township also has $50,000 for the project, which is expected to be completed in the next year.

McCauley said the acting chief and other police officials would never leave citizens in the town unsafe.
