# Internal causal-mechanism claim package

This is a PI-facing internal evidence package, not a polished report. It states bounded institutional findings assertively while keeping causal estimation and national wage-gap estimation out of bounds.

## Executive claim map

- **CLAIM-A: Formal bargaining, arbitration, and factfinding** — Safety compensation is exposed to recurring formal negotiation and impasse-resolution institutions that can lift wage floors, preserve leverage, and lock increases into agreements or awards. *(strength: moderate; fit: supports)*
- **CLAIM-B: Step schedules and seniority progression** — Step, seniority, rank, and COLA structures embed recurring wage growth rather than requiring a wholly discretionary raise each year. *(strength: strong; fit: supports)*
- **CLAIM-C: Non-base compensation pressure** — Safety compensation pressure extends beyond base pay because overtime, holidays, longevity, premiums, stipends, and allowances are structured compensation channels. *(strength: strong; fit: supports)*
- **CLAIM-D: Market, recruitment, and retention pressure** — Recruitment, retention, and comparator pressure provides an explicit institutional justification for raising schedules or adding targeted premiums, with public-safety staffing making the channel especially salient in many records. *(strength: strong; fit: supports)*
- **CLAIM-E: Retroactivity and implementation** — Retroactive effective dates and implementation provisions turn bargaining delay into payable increases and back pay instead of eliminating scheduled growth. *(strength: strong; fit: supports)*
- **CLAIM-F: Ordinance, budget, and pay-plan formalization** — Council action, ordinances, budgets, pay plans, and classification systems translate compensation proposals into funded policy, while also imposing fiscal limits. *(strength: moderate; fit: partially_supports)*
- **CLAIM-G: Non-safety mechanisms as counterweights** — Non-safety units also receive bargaining, steps, COLAs, and pay-plan mechanisms; the defensible distinction is a reinforcing safety pressure bundle, not a claim that non-safety compensation lacks institutional growth channels. *(strength: moderate; fit: complicates)*
- **CLAIM-H: Mechanism interpretation versus wage-gap estimation** — The corpus supports the direction and operation of a safety wage-pressure story more strongly than it supports a clean global wage-gap or causal-effect estimate. *(strength: strong; fit: supports)*

## Safety-wage-growth assessment

The corpus supports a bounded causal-mechanism interpretation: safety compensation appears especially exposed to a reinforcing bundle of bargaining, impasse resolution, step progression, non-base compensation, recruitment/retention pressure, comparability, and retroactive implementation. These institutions can raise floors, schedule growth, protect add-ons, strengthen leverage, and make delayed increases payable. Non-safety units also receive several of these mechanisms, so the evidence supports the direction of the safety wage-pressure story more strongly than a universal safety-only mechanism or a clean global wage-gap estimate.

## What this package does not claim

- A national safety wage-gap estimate: Matched city-cycle breadth and harmonized pay basis are insufficient.
- A national prevalence estimate for any mechanism: Evidence-unit counts are not population-weighted denominators.
- A causal effect estimate of a mechanism: No counterfactual identification design or treatment-effect analysis was run.
- A regression-based claim: No regression was run or authorized.
- A precise claim that safety wages grow X percent faster: Global wage-gap readiness fails.
- The mechanism caused an observed wage difference: Documentary mechanism interpretation is not causal estimation.

## Good-as-gold upgrade path

The decisive upgrade is a repeated city × bargaining-cycle panel with matched safety/non-safety units, comparable roles, harmonized base and non-base compensation, audited implementation dates, and a credible identification design.
