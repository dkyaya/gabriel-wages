I.

INTRODUCTION

This matter concerns a Fact-finding proceeding between Perry Township (Stark County),
Ohio (hereinafter “Township”) and the Fraternal Order of Police, Ohio Labor Council, Inc.
(hereinafter “Union”). The State Employment Relations Board (SERB) duly appointed the

undersigned as Fact-finder on April 24, 2025, to conduct a Fact-finding hearing concerning a new
Collective Bargaining Agreement for all sworn police officers having the classification of
Sergeant. The parties did execute and filed with SERB an Extension/Waiver Agreement, extending
the period to hold the fact-finding hearing to May 30, 2025, as provided under Ohio Administrative
Code Rule 4117-9-05(G). A Fact-finding hearing was scheduled and took place on May 30, 2025.
The Parties were successful in reaching tentative agreements on all provisions of the new
collective bargaining agreement, with the exception of Article 32, Section 32.1, Compensation.
The Fact-finder, after a brief period of mediation, which did not change the position of the parties,
commenced a hearing on the issue.
The Fact-finding proceeding was conducted pursuant to the Ohio Collective Bargaining
Law as well as the rules and regulations of the SERB, as amended. During the Fact-finding
proceeding, this Fact-finder provided the parties with the opportunity to present arguments and
evidence in support of their respective positions on the issues remaining for this Fact-finder’s
consideration. The Parties waived the taking of a transcript.
In making the recommendations in this Report, consideration was given to all reliable
evidence presented relevant to the outstanding issues before me and consideration was given to
the following criteria listed in Rule 4117-9-05 (K) of the State Employment Relations Board:
(1)

Past collectively bargained agreements, if any, between the parties;

(2)

those issues related to other public and private employees doing comparable work, giving
consideration to factors peculiar to the area and classification involved;

(3)

administer the issues proposed, and the effect of the adjustments on the normal standard of
public service;

(4)

II.

BACKGROUND

General
Perry Township is one of seventeen townships of Stark County, Ohio. It is an urban
township; the 2020 census found 28,389 people in the township. The Township is governed by a
three-member board of trustees. The Township provides road and bridge maintenance, cemetery
maintenance, police protection, fire protection, and emergency medical services. Funding sources
for the Township, like other Townships in Ohio, relies on property taxes levied on real estate
property. Often, Townships need to collect revenue beyond the minimum millage permitted under
the Ohio Constitution to maintain operations. In such an event, a levy must be placed before the
voters for a vote. While Perry Township has been successful in raising revenues through Police
and Fire levies, the Township has not been successful in seeking revenues for Road Maintenance.
Since the last Police and Fire levies passed by thin margins, the Township seeks to be prudent in
expenditures made.
This Bargaining-unit consists of three (3) members, all having the classification of
Sergeant. Historically, the bargaining-unit has consisted of four (4) members. The Township
asserted that the Police Chief was currently interviewing officers to bring the unit back up to four
(4) members.

3

The most recent three-year term Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) among the
parties expired on December 31, 2024., has been extended by agreement of the parties, and is
currently in effect.
The Township and the Union engaged in bargaining sessions on November 12, 2024,
December Dec 2, 2024, December 9, 2024, and February 14, 2025. During these negotiation
sessions, the Parties reached tentative agreements on all issues, except Compensation. This issue
of wages was submitted to the Fact-finder for consideration at the Fact-finding hearing.
The parties are in agreement that Perry Township is financially sound and well-managed.
While it has the financial ability to increase the Sergeants’ wages under the proposals presented,
the primary question in this case is whether it is appropriate under the facts presented to maintain
the current wage differential of fifteen percent (15%) above the highest paid Patrol Officer, or to
increase the differential to twenty percent (20%), as proposed by the Union.

1.

Article 32, Section 32.1 - Compensation

The Position of the Union
The Union proposes to create a new wage step, “Senior Sergeant,” for a sergeant with one
or more years’ service. A sergeant with less than one year of experience would remain at the
current “Sergeant Step,” and receive a rank differential of the current fifteen percent (15%) above
the highest paid Patrol Officer. A sergeant at the “Senior Sergeant Step,” would be compensated
4

at a rank differential of twenty percent (20%) above the highest paid Patrol Officer.
The Union argues that the increase for Sergeants with more than one-year experience in
that position is necessary to fairly compensate the Sergeants commensurate with the increases
given to the Patrol Officers whom they supervise. In order to understand the reasoning behind the
Sergeant’s proposal, it is insightful to understand the successful negotiations that resulted in a new
collective bargaining agreement for the Patrol Officers’ Unit.
During this negotiation cycle, the Township and the Patrol Officers’ bargaining-unit agreed
to significant annual wage increases of six percent (6%) effective January 1, 2025, four percent
(4%) effective at the start of 2026, and four percent (4%) effective at the start of 2027. In order to
address recruitment and retention issues, the Township also agreed to lessen the number of wage
steps from seven (7) steps to six (6) steps, and decreased the number of step years necessary to
reach the top wage from fifteen (15) years to five (5) years. These measures, taken to expedite the
advancement through the wage step schedule, greatly benefited the vast majority of the Patrol
Officers who have less than fifteen years’ service with the Township. These junior officers will
experience financially impactful percentage wage increases at both the start of each new year and
mid-year on the occurrence of each of their individual anniversary date as they progress to the next
higher step on the new, now-shortened, wage schedule. The decrease in the steps enabled the Patrol
Officers to top out on the wage scale ten (10) years earlier than under the prior contract. As a result,
these junior officers will experience anywhere from 16.8% - 36.6% wage increase over the three
years of the Agreement, depending on the current wage step they are situate.
The four (4) most senior patrol officers who were at the top of the pay scale upon
ratification of the Agreement can expect percentage base wage increases of about 14.65% over the
life of the CBA. To help offset the financial wage increase disparity between the most senior and
5

the more junior officers, the Parties agreed to raise the officer-in-charge (OIC) rate of pay from a
flat $2.00/hour to the actual Sergeant rate of pay for all hours worked as OIC. Typically, the most
senior officers assigned to a patrol shift serves as OIC. The 2025 increase in the OIC rate of pay
combined with the new 2025 hourly rate, actually works out to be about a nine percent (9%)
increase in the senior patrol officers’ hourly rate of pay for every hour worked as an OIC in 2025.
The substantial 2025 increase of the Patrol Officers combined with the base hourly wage rate and
OIC rate increase persuaded the Patrol Officer bargaining-unit to accept the terms of their new
Agreement. The result of the Patrol increases placed the Officers in a ranking of third place
compared to comparable wages earned in comparable jurisdictions presented by the Union. The
Township apparently agreed to the wage package to aid in hiring and retaining Patrol Officers,
which in the long run saving the Township money by attracting quality Patrol Officers and
decreasing the possibility of contiguous Townships and Counties hiring Perry Townships wellpaid and trained Patrol Officers.
The Union argues that none of these new contract measures for the Patrol Officers, (1)
decreasing the number of wage steps, (2) decreasing the number of step years or (3) increasing the
OIC hourly rate, benefited the Sergeant bargaining-unit. It reasons that it is not fair to ignore these
factors and fail to consider a comparable increase in wages for the Sergeants. The simple solution
to similarly improve the compensation for members of the Sergeant bargaining-unit relative to the
other internally comparable co-workers is to create a new wage step, “Senior Sergeant” (sergeant
with one or more years’ service) that pays the Sergeants at that step a rank differential of twenty
percent (20%). This conclusion is based upon an analysis of both internal and external parity
among other Sergeants performing similar work.

(INTERNAL PARITY)
1. HEALTH CARE INSURANCE. Perry Township offers medical, dental, vision and
prescription healthcare insurance to its’ employees and township officials. Insurance plans,
benefit levels and costs are reviewed annually during the fall months by the health care
committee (HCC), which is comprised of representative members from each bargaining-unit,
non-union employees, and township officials. The HCC then provides a non-binding
recommendation of an insurance renewal plan to the Board of Trustees (“the Board”).
a.

The Board is responsible for selecting the healthcare insurance provider and benefit
levels. Effective January 1, 2025, the Board approved the HCC recommendation to
move to a Health Savings Account (HSA)- compliant Preferred Provider Plan
(Anthem - PPO) with higher deductibles and lower monthly premium costs than
the now former (Aultcare - PPO) insurance plan. The new Anthem plan has much
higher deductible amounts and maximum out-of-pocket costs, and thus the Board
approved funding an HSA up to the 2025 annual in-network maximum-out-ofpocket costs of $3,675 for the single plan and $7,350 for the family plan.

b.

The Board approved a Township funded HSA for all employees and officials to
offset the employees and officials’ higher premium cost share. The employee
monthly premium percentage cost share has stood consistently at eight percent (8%)
of the total monthly premium costs since at least the 2012 Agreement. Effective
January 1, 2025, the HCC recommended that all township employees and officials
pay increased monthly premium costs. All township employees and officials using
the Township provided insurance plan shall pay ten percent (10%) in 2025; in 2026,
7

pay fifteen percent (15%), and in 2027, pay twenty percent (20%) of the total
monthly premium.
c.

The HCC meetings were held separately from contract negotiations between the
Township and its various bargaining-units during the fall/winter of 2024. The two
police bargaining-units negotiated separately with the Patrol Officer bargainingunit agreeing to a successor Agreement in January 2025. Ultimately, the health care
insurance terms negotiated between the Township and the Patrol Officers were
adopted by the other employee groups, including the Sergeants’ bargaining-unit.

8

The wage increases for the Sergeants will jump them to a third position ranking, which is
the same level to which the Patrol Officers’ rate increases advanced them, and this Unit deserves
to be treated the same. The cost to the Township per Sergeant is negligible. The cost difference
between the Union’s proposal (remain at 15%) and the Township’s proposal (increase to 20%) is
only $13,809 for the first year of the Agreement ($4,603 per officer, with roll-up included),
$19,148 for the second year ($4,787 per officer x4, with roll-up included), and $19,912 for the
third year of the Agreement ($4,978 per officer x4), for a total of $52,869 for the life of the new
Agreement. That is not a significant amount of money to compensate the Sergeants for their extra
responsibilities and provide them with a comparable wage as paid in comparable jurisdictions.
For all of these reasons, the Union’s wage proposals should be recommended.
The Position of the Township
The Township proposes current contract language which provides for a 15% rank

differential.
The bargaining-unit employees’ competence and worthiness are not at issue.

The

Township very much appreciates the service of these public employees to the Township and its
citizens. And the Township has already agreed to matters which would improve employment with
the Township by agreeing to an additional holiday, an increase in uniform allowance, an increase
in funeral leave, and improved insurance benefits with the Township providing the funding toward
each employee’s HSA.
However, the Township also has a responsibility to the citizens to be fiscally responsible,
and a responsibility to be fair to other Perry Township employees. Any increase in rank differential
for this bargaining-unit, which would provide greater increases than other bargaining-units, is
simply not warranted. The effective percentage increase given to the Patrol Officers is 14.9% over
the three-year term. 2 That was justified for both recruiting and retention purposes. The increase
requested by the Union, however, would result in a 19.5% increase for the Sergeants over the
three-year term of this contract. Under the Township’s proposal, the increase to the Sergeants
would be 14.9%, or an amount of increase equal to the Patrol Officer increases. This additional
4.6% is not justified.
While the Townships ability to finance an increase in wages for its police department is
not in dispute, the Township Trustees need to be prudent in the expenditure of the revenues they
receive from the levies voted on by the residents. Past tax levies were passed by slim margins, and
there is no guarantee that further levies to fund large increases would be approved by the voters.

In addition to the above external comparables, the State Employment Relations Board
(SERB) 2024 Annual Wage Settlement Report reflects that the Sergeant’s wage increases
statewide averaged 3.86% for 2024; the Columbus region wage increases were above the reported
Local (Akron/Canton), Statewide, Township, and Police average wage increases over the last three
years, as well as over a six-year comparison.
The Township acknowledges that internally, the Police Officers and the Sergeants did
receive a lesser increase than the Fire and AFSCME Bargaining-units, but their wages were
significantly less than those of the Police to begin with. The Union argued that a records clerk in
the AFSCME received a 20% increase, but those classifications were paid significantly less than
either a Patrol Officer or a Sergeant. Wages of lower paid employees are often raised at a higher
percentage to bring them to a minimal market rate. As an example, raising a $17.00 per hour
employee wage 20% results is a far different cost than raising a $41.00 per hour employee at the
17

same percentage rate. In this case, the Sergeants are at the proper market rate.
The Township can responsibly implement wage increases reflected in the fifteen percent
(15%) wage differential in each of the next three years. The Township recognizes that maintaining
the current fifteen percent (15%) differential will not cause the Sergeants to be the highest paid
amongst the Union’s hand-picked comparable jurisdictions, but they will remain being paid above
the average wage rate, and wage differential of comparable jurisdictions.
Discussion, Findings and Recommendation
The Township is currently in excellent financial condition as measured by its healthy
General Fund carryover balance. There is little argument that the Township can absorb increased
salaries for its police and recognizes that healthy increases are needed due to the need to recruit
and retain Police Officers and likewise to advance the salaries of the Sergeants resulting from their
rank differential, but it believes it must be prudent in the amount of any wage increases it agrees
to give the Police Officers and Sergeants over a three-year period because of limited revenues
generated from current levies.. It believes that the current rank differential, keeping in mind the
increases given to the top Police Officer wage step, in light of comparable wages paid to Sergeants
in external police departments, is fair and reasonable.
The Union believes that the Sergeants wage differential should be increased for several
reasons: (1) it is unfair for the Patrol Officers, primarily due to a decrease in wage steps and
increased OIC hourly rates, to receive an overall higher wage increase than that provided by the
current rank differential; (2) the Sergeants supported the increase in employee contributions for
insurance premiums (offset with better coverage and a funded HSA), but the level of increase in
wages for the Patrol Officers helped them pay for the increases while the Sergeants did not get an
increase in rank differential to cover these increases; (3) the Sergeants have more responsibilities
18

than other comparable contiguous jurisdictions because they must also perform the same duties
that are performed in other jurisdictions by higher ranking officers (Lieutenants, Captains) and
they, on average, supervise more subordinates than those jurisdictions; (4) a 20% rank differential
is needed to place this bargaining-unit on par with other comparable jurisdictions; and (5) an
increase in rank differential would move the Sergeants to a third position among comparable
jurisdictions, which is comparable to the level to which the Patrol Officers were moved as a result
of their wage increases.
The first observation is that the members of the Sergeants’ Bargaining-unit, compared to
comparable jurisdictions selected by both the Union and Township, are paid an above average rank
differential and above average wages. That reflects a positive approval and commitment of the
Community through levies, the commitment and appreciation of the Township Trustees for a wellpaid police department, and a recognition of the need for high-quality law enforcement. The issue
for the Fact-finder is to determine what is an appropriate wage rank differential for this unit in
light of the interest and welfare of the public, the ability of the Township to finance and administer
the issues proposed, and the effect of the adjustments on the normal standard of public service.
The Fact-finder recognizes the emotional side of the Union’s argument that Sergeants are
either disappointed or frustrated that a number of the police officers that they supervise will receive
a higher percentage wage increase than they will under the current rank differential of 15% due to
a compression of the steps to advance to the next level of compensation and the increase in the
OIC hourly wage, but the practicality of raising those wages of Patrol Officers was necessary for
recruiting and retaining the officers. Notwithstanding the increases received by the Patrol Officers’
Unit, the Sergeants factually did receive a wage of 15% more than the highest paid Patrol Officer
due to their rank differential. As such, while it is understandable why the Sergeants advanced this
19

argument, it is not a basis for increasing the rank differential percentage when considering the
interest of the public welfare. They are paid more for their responsibilities than their subordinates,
even though some will advance in wages quicker because of the reduction in the number of years
to advance to the next wage step level.
The argument that the Patrol Officers received more wages to offset employee
contributions to health care premiums may be the effective result of the overall wage increases
received by some of the Patrol Officers, but, as argued above, the Sergeants nonetheless received
an increase due to the police officer top step level increases. The fact that some Patrol Officers
may have effectively received a higher wage increase due to the factors previously cited, an
argument that Sergeants should therefore have a similar increase is not persuasive.
Likewise, the argument that an increase in rank differential based solely upon a showing
that a 20% differential is needed to place this bargaining-unit at a higher ranking than other
comparable jurisdictions is not supported by the evidence. Evidence indicates that the City of
Canton may have a 20% rank differential, but Canton has more than twice the population and more
than six times the number of police officers. Massillon may have a 17% rank differential, but it
has a larger population and more patrol officers to supervise. Jackson has a 19.9% rank differential,
but it is not only a much wealthier township, it has a higher population and more police officers to
supervise. While balancing these statistics with other jurisdictions’ comparables may give support
for a higher differential for this unit, it does not support an increase to a 20% rank differential.
The argument that an increase in rank differential would move the Sergeants to a third
position among comparable jurisdictions, which is comparable to the level to which the Patrol
Officers were moved as a result of their wage increases is a cogent argument, but there must be a
public interest basis for the Township to expend more money for Sergeants that is necessary to
20

maintain and attract senior police officers. It was apparently demonstrated by the Patrol Officers
that their step wage increases needed to be compressed to attract and retain those bargaining-unit
members. That did result in an elevation of their position among comparable jurisdictions. There
is no rational basis to then increase the Sergeants differential for this reason alone.
I find that while considering these factors as a whole and considering the evidence of the
supervisory duties of these Sergeants compared to Sergeants in other comparable jurisdictions,
there is a justification for a rank differential increase due to the duties and responsibilities of the
members of this bargaining-unit. The current number of Patrol Officers supervised by these
Sergeants is similar to that of other jurisdictions, but considering the number of Patrol Officers
management deems necessary to meet the public needs in the Township, more Patrol Officers are
needed. While it is anticipated that another Sergeant will be hired in the near future, even with four
Sergeants, those Sergeants will be supervising more Patrol Officers and taking on more
responsibilities without higher ranking officers (Lieutenants and Captains). Because of the
demonstrated current level of duties and responsibilities of the Sergeants, and those anticipated
over the term of this new Agreement, the Fact-finder believes that a 2% increase in the rank
differential to 17% is justified and in the public interest. The Fact-finder arrives at this percentage
based upon the demographics of comparable townships and a couple of comparable cities provided
by the parties. Additionally, the evidence supports a finding that the Township can afford the
increase. Increasing the rank differential to increase the wages of an individual Sergeant is
beneficial to the Township for it will involve far less cost than hiring higher supervisory officers
or several additional Sergeants.

21

RECOMMENDATION
It is recommended that the Parties create a new wage step, “Senior Sergeant,”
for a sergeant with one or more years’ service. A sergeant with less than one
year of experience would remain at the current “Sergeant Step,” and receive
a rank differential of the current fifteen percent (15%) above the highest paid
Patrol Officer. A sergeant at the “Senior Sergeant Step,” would be
compensated at a rank differential of seventeen percent (17%) above the
highest paid Patrol Officer.
CONCLUSION
In conclusion, this Fact-finder hereby submits the above-referenced recommendations on
the outstanding issues presented to him for his consideration. Further, the Fact-finder incorporates
all tentative agreements previously reached by the parties and recommends that they be included
in the Parties’ Final Agreement.
