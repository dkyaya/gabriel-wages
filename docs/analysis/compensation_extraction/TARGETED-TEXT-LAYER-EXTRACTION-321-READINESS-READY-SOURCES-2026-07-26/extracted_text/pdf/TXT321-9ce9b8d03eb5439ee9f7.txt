II. Proposed Approach to Compensation Study
A. Study Scope and Objectives
The City of Spring Hill has 300 FTE employees assigned to approximately 100 job classifications,
each assigned to one of 34 pay grades (of which 21 are for public safety). The study will
encompass all jobs and employees.
As indicated in the RFP, the present study is intended to be an update of the City’s current wage and
salary plan. The primary objectives for the study based on the RFP, subject to confirmation and
modification by City management, include the following:
•

Ensure that the City’s pay practices are competitive in order to attract and retain the required
talent to provide quality services to citizens (this includes ensuring that all employees are
assigned to the appropriate job titles and that individual employee compensation accurately
reflects the nature of the work being performed).

•

Ensure internal equity of compensation across functional areas, especially for the same or
similar job classifications, and that pay is administered in a fair and consistent manner.

•

Assess the competitiveness of the City’s employee benefits package and work with the City
to determine how this should be considered in updating the pay plan.

Burris, Thompson & Associates project consultants will seek opportunities to involve the HR
Director and department heads in the definition of job classifications, review of market data,
consideration of different pay plan designs (if applicable), review of proposed pay structure, and
development of a pay administration policy. Periodic progress reviews are identified in the
tentative proposed work plan (see section IV below).

B. Fact Finding
1. City Management Direction
Burris, Thompson & Associates will attend an initial meeting with the City Administrator, HR
Director, and other designated management staff members to discuss pay plan objectives, Study
parameters, and to develop/confirm the City’s pay strategy and how it supports the operational
objectives of the City’s various departments. The pay strategy would include definition of the
competitive labor market for the City, desired positioning relative to that market, extent to
which the current pay plan design may be modified based on City input, affordability, and other
related issues. These pay plan objectives and parameters would guide completion of the market
data analysis and development of Study recommendations.

2. Department Head Interviews
Interviews with department heads will serve two purposes. First, the project consultant would
ask each department head to review the organization of work and the various positions in their
areas (discussion is usually driven by review of departmental organization chart). We would

3. Review Pay Plan Documentation
Burris, Thompson & Associates will review current City job classifications and pay ranges, pay

C. Job Analysis
1. Review Job Descriptions
Burris, Thompson & Associates will review all City job descriptions and organization charts to
facilitate valid benchmarking to the salary survey data. We primarily compare job functions and
qualifications – not just job titles. We will identify job descriptions that seem out of date and
suggest some edits but complete overhaul of job descriptions is not within the scope of the
Study. We will also evaluate and recommend FLSA status for all jobs.

2. Employee Job Analysis Questionnaires
To supplement job descriptions, collection of job content information from employees may be
desirable for some or all positions. To accomplish this, Burris, Thompson & Associates will
develop a job content questionnaire for employees to complete (See Appendix II for an outline
of a sample job analysis questionnaire – actual questionnaire is seven pages, including “white
space” for responses).
As deemed to be necessary to supplement existing job descriptions, the City would distribute the
questionnaire to employees representing some or all job classifications for them to complete.
Upon completion by employees, supervisors would review and approve/supplement
questionnaires. All questionnaires would then be forwarded to Burris, Thompson & Associates.
Burris, Thompson & Associates will review all completed questionnaires.

3. Job Analysis Interviews
As indicated above, Burris, Thompson & Associates will conduct job analysis interviews with
all department heads in which they would describe all jobs in their office or department. If
needed to supplement these discussions and the employee completed questionnaires, we can
interview selected individual employees to ensure understanding of job responsibilities and
required competencies and qualifications. In the interests of timeliness of Study completion,
controlling project costs, and minimizing disruption of City operations, we anticipate that these
job incumbent interviews will be limited to only those deemed to be critically necessary.

D. Review Employee Job Title Assignments
Based on review of employee job questionnaires, department head discussions, and existing job
descriptions, Burris, Thompson & Associates will review the job titles of all employees. We will
recommend assignment of employees to different job titles that match the employees’ job duties and
responsibilities where appropriate. Work on other similar engagements suggests that there will
likely be only a few recommended changes.

E. Identify Market Data Sources
Burris, Thompson & Associates will compile comparative salary and benefits data from available
published sources representing local government employers and general business and industry.
Sources that may be used include:
• Burris, Thompson & Associates’ 2022 Public Sector Salary Survey – with City
management, we will review the set of public sector benchmark employers used in the
pay study we completed for the City in 2020. This same set of benchmarks could be used
for the current Study, though some additions or deletions may be appropriate. We just
published the results of our 2022 survey – 77 cities and counties participated in the
survey, likely including most if not all that Spring Hill would choose to be included in the
comparison group for the present study. We can solicit data from any that did not.
• Economic Research Institute Salary Assessor, an extensive computer database updated
quarterly that contains data for more than 4,000 job titles, filtered by industry and
location. This database would be used for data representing general business and industry
in the Maury/Williamson county area.
• US Bureau of Labor Statistics - Area Occupational Employment and Wage Estimates.
This database would be used for data representing general business and industry in the
Middle Tennessee area.
• KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation Employer Health Benefits 2022 Annual Survey).
• Other data sources that might be provided by the City.

F. Compile Market Salary Data
For each City job we will then compile data from the survey or surveys that contain a job that
matches an understanding of the job gained from the incumbent completed job questionnaire,
department head discussion, and the City’s job description. The benchmark salary survey numbers
used (i.e., market average, 60th percentile, etc.) will be based on the City’s desired competitive
position in the labor market that was defined by City management. We will work with City
management to determine the appropriate salary planning date for the study – the date to which all
data will be projected to create the ‘snapshot’ of the market. Typically, this is the beginning of the
fiscal year, July 1, 2023, in this case, but the City could choose a more aggressive date.
Since cities compete for talent with general employers as well as other public sector employers, we
usually recommend identifying a public sector data point and a general employer data point for

each job where appropriate. The average of these two benchmark salaries will represent the market
rate for the job. (If desired by the City, the public sector data could be weighted more heavily than
the general employer data.)
The market rate is for the job and is usually representative of salaries of employees in their jobs 5
to 7 years. Recent hires are typically paid slightly less than the market rates for their jobs while
most employers allow salaries to exceed job market rates for those employees with many years of
service, up to a limit that is usually 15% to 20% above the market rate.
For department head and certain senior level professional jobs, salary is often related to city
population. Population can be a proxy for the degree of complexity of these jobs. Burris,
Thompson & Associates will complete a regression analysis of the relationship between the salary
data from the city government benchmarks and city population for those jobs. We can then plug in
the City of Spring Hill’s population in the resulting regression formula to determine a ‘predicted’
market salary rate (median, 60th percentile or other) based on population. This predicted market rate
might be a more valid representation of the market value of the City of Spring Hill job than the
overall survey sample average.

G. City of Spring Hill Benefits Market Comparison
Burris, Thompson & Associates will review the City’s health insurance, vacation, sick leave,
disability, and retirement benefit offerings and compare to public sector benchmark employer
practices. This high level review is intended to determine how the City’s benefits programs might
impact the City’s positioning of wages and salaries. However, design and implementation of
benefit plan revisions is beyond the scope of the present study. Burris, Thompson & Associates
may identify benefit plan design features that the City can pursue with its brokers, insurance
carriers, plan administrators, and financial services providers.

H. City of Spring Hill Salaries Market Comparison
1. Structure Market Index
Burris, Thompson & Associates will calculate a Structure Market Index for each City of Spring
Hill job. The Structure Market Index is the ratio of the current Pay Range Midpoint to the
Market Rate for the job.
Structure Index = Range Midpoint ÷ Job Market Rate
Individual and aggregate Structure Index numbers will allow comparison of current City of
Spring Hill pay ranges to market practice.

2. Salary Market Index
Burris, Thompson & Associates will calculate a Salary Market Index for each City of Spring
Hill employee. The Salary Market Index is the ratio of the employee’s current salary to the
Market Rate for the employee’s job.
Salary Index = Employee Salary ÷ Job Market Rate

I. Job Classification and Pay Structure
1. Confirm Jobs are Assigned to Appropriate Pay Grades
Burris Thompson & Associates will review the Structure Market Index calculated for each job
and identify those jobs for which the index is low (typically 92% or so), indicating the current
pay range is significantly below market. We will review these ‘out of line’ jobs as well as those
that City staff perceive to be misaligned and may recommend changes in pay grade assignments.
Note that in most cases these discussions of revised job pay grade classifications are tied to
discussion and clarification of the job duties and qualifications in the job descriptions that result
in changing how jobs are matched to the market data. Job descriptions may need to be further
revised to more appropriately reflect how City of Spring Hill management views the jobs. In
this manner, the Study addresses internal equity.

2. Review Job FLSA Status
Burris, Thompson & Associates will review the FLSA status of all jobs based on the federal
guidelines and recommend changes as appropriate.

3. Revise Pay Structure(s)
Burris, Thompson & Associates will then review the design of the City’s pay structures to
identify possible modifications that could enhance competitiveness and/or simplify pay
administration. City officials and management must determine what is acceptable and we can
revise the pay structure(s) if needed.
We will work with the City to determine if a single pay structure for all jobs may be desirable or
if separate pay structures for Fire and Police, usually tied to professional development, would be
more appropriate.
Finally, we will recommend an adjustment to the pay ranges based on the market data to ensure
competitiveness.

employee salaries that would be appropriate with implementation of the revised pay structure(s).
Typically, these salary adjustments would be comprised of the following:
• Adjustments to pay range minimums if employees’ salaries fall below the proposed pay
ranges for their jobs.

2. Pay Adjustments to Address Pay Compression
To address pay compression and internal equity we will help the City improve the linkage
between actual employee pay rates and job (and City) tenure. This will mitigate pay
compression between employees with differing lengths of service in their positions. A few
additional adjustments may be needed to ensure reasonable “daylight” between pay rates of
supervisors and their subordinates.
To accomplish this Burris, Thompson & Associates will analyze how closely current City of
Spring Hill employee pay rates are correlated with time in job. We will then create a “formula”
or scheme for adjusting pay based on job tenure. An example that could be modified to fit the
City is as shown below.

For each employee, if his/her pay rate is not at or above the “Target” percent of range midpoint,
pay would be adjusted to that rate. This “formula” or scheme could be applied before or after
any City-wide general increase. It could incorporate fewer or more tenure categories and the
targets can be set as aggressively as the City wishes or can afford. Total City tenure can be
considered as well – often as an add on. For example, an extra 2% for 10 years, 4% for 15
years, and 5% for 20 or more years might be appropriate.
Once the methodology has been determined, Burris, Thompson & Associates will then apply it
across all employees to determine the amount of salary adjustment that would be needed for
each employee (many will not receive an adjustment as they are already above their “target” pay
rates).
This suggested tenure based adjustment for each employee should be reviewed with the
department head to ensure that it is appropriate given other considerations not considered by the
“formula.” Some additional pay adjustments may be needed to mitigate pay compression
between supervisors and their subordinates.

3. Pay Adjustment Cost Projections
Burris, Thompson & Associates would project the total cost (including impact on FICA and
retirement plan funding) of recommended employee salary adjustments. The affordability of
this total cost would be reviewed with City management. The amounts and/or timing of the
implementation of pay adjustments can be revised accordingly if needed.

K. Preliminary Review with Management
We will review preliminary recommendations (any recommended reassignment of employees to
different job titles, job pay grade re-classifications, the proposed 2023/2024 pay structure, and
recommended employee salary adjustments) with the HR Director and other senior management as
appropriate. To ensure buy-in, once given the go ahead we typically review these preliminary
recommendations with department heads and obtain their feedback. We will channel discussion of
disputed job pay grade assignments to discussion of the jobs themselves – job duties and
qualifications. Revision of any proposed job pay grades will be based on revised understanding of
the nature of the jobs that in turn would lead to revising salary survey job matches in determining
the market rates for jobs.

L. Review Pay Administration Policy
Burris, Thompson & Associates will help the City review existing pay administration policies and
procedures, including how employees progress through pay ranges, and recommend changes based
on feedback from department heads.

M. Develop Implementation Plan
Burris, Thompson & Associates will assist the City in creating a plan for implementing the new pay
plan. The plan would include proposed effective date of pay changes, communication schedule (see
section II N “Employee Communications” below) and pay change procedures as applicable.
The City may be able to fund all proposed pay adjustments at the time the revised compensation
plan is implemented. If potential implementation costs are significant, we normally develop an
alternative for implementing the revised compensation plan over a two-year (or, though rarely, three
year) period for the City’s consideration. This involves our development of interim pay structures.
Burris, Thompson & Associates will train HR staff in the methodology for maintaining the pay
plan, including classification of new jobs and periodic updating of the pay structure based on
changing labor market conditions. Much of this would be documented in the pay policy.
