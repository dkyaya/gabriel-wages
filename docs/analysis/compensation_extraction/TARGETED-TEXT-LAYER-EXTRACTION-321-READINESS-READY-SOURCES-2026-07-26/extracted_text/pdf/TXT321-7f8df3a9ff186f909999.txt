The no-new-revenue tax rate is the tax rate for the 2024 tax year that will raise the same amount of property
tax revenue for City of Humble from the same properties in both the 2023 tax year and the 2024 tax year.
The voter-approval rate is the highest tax rate that City of Humble may adopt without holding an election to
seek voter approval of the rate, unless the de minimis rate for City of Humble exceeds the voter-approval
rate for City of Humble.
The de minimis rate is the rate equal to the sum of the no-new-revenue maintenance and operations rate for
City of Humble, the rate that will raise $500,000, and the current debt rate for City of Humble.
The proposed tax rate is greater than the no-new-revenue tax rate. This means that City of Humble is
proposing to increase property taxes for the 2024 tax year.
A PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSED TAX RATE WILL BE HELD ON September 26, 2024 AT
6:30 pm AT City Hall 114 W. Higgins, Humble, Texas 77338.
The proposed tax rate is greater than the voter-approval tax rate but not greater than the de minimis rate and
does not exceed the rate that allows voters to petition for an election under Section 26.075, Tax Code. If City
of Humble adopts the proposed tax rate, the City of Humble is not required to hold an election so that voters
may accept or reject the proposed tax rate and the qualified voters of the City of Humble may not petition the
City of Humble to require an election to be held to determine whether to reduce the proposed tax rate.

City Manager’s Budget Transmittal Message

Please accept this transmittal message with attached Fiscal Year 2024-2025 (FY25) proposed budget per
state statute and the City of Humble Charter.
For the FY25 budget, City staff remains vigilant and conservative in forecasting proposed revenues,
especially sales tax, which is our largest revenue source. As of the date of this transmittal it appears that
the City will finish Fiscal Year 2023-2024 with near record sales tax collections in excess of $17 million.
However, the macro economic climate is uncertain with a potential slow down on the horizon; accordingly
staff has projected sales tax revenues to be flat for FY25. With regard to the Ad Valorem taxes, this year’s
budget was prepared with an estimated tax rate of $0.261476 based upon $2.42 billion in assessed valuation
with 100% of the rate going towards the operations and maintenance of the City. The rate represents
an effective increase of 8.10%. The rate proposed in this budget is considered the De Minimis Tax Rate,
which is the rate afforded to cities with less than 30,000 in population and does not raise more than
$500,000 in new tax revenue above what the No New Revenue Rate would be. While this is an increase in
the tax rate, it is consistent with the Council and this Administration’s efforts to bring the tax rate to a
sustainable level in order to adequately fund our operations and plan for future growth and improvements –
and remains one of the lowest in Harris County.
While revenues for the General, Special Revenue and Enterprise Funds are generally looking positive, the
conclusion of the Red Light Camera Program will result in a $3.5 million reduction in revenues. However,
we maintain a healthy balance in the Red Light Camera Fund and should be able to continue funding our
traffic enforcement operations from that fund for the next three to four years before fully absorbing those
operations back into the general fund.
The total estimated beginning balances and revenues across all funds is projected to be $126,380,013.
The budgeted expenditures across all funds for FY25, including capital outlay, totals $82,498,756,
which is a 
% decrease from the previous fiscal year. This is largely due to the completion of
several major capital projects including Fire Station Number 2, the Senior Activity Center, S. Bender
Avenue rehab and the reconfiguration of Main and S. Houston. This will leave us with an estimated
surplus of $43,881,257 year-end fund balance across all funds.

Specifically, with regard to the General Fund, expenditures total $50,946,257 of which $12,145,021 is
capital outlay included in the operating expenses. The City will continue to maintain a healthy General
Fund reserve as it is forecast to be $36,467,006 or approximately 71% of annual expenditures inclusive of
capital outlay. While the city maintains such high reserves over the minimum requirements, it may not
be feasible to address all needs in the future as the City continues to grow and reinvest in itself. It is
also important to note that capital projects are separated from the base budgets to adhere funds that
are to be used for maintenance and operation versus expansion and addition. The spend down of the fund
balance only includes one-time expenditures, as the proposed budget for the General Fund shows
$41,871,500 in new revenues vs. $38,801,190 in operations and maintenance expenditures (recurring
expenses), and therefore is fully balanced.
Overall, the FY 25 budget allows the City to continue to provide excellent service to our community, while
continuing to have strong reserve balances that are well above the statutory requirement. This budget
represents our collective commitment to investing in our community with an eye towards the future as it
addresses numerous needs and long-overdue improvements throughout the city. The following items are
highlights of the proposed budget:
•

Pursuant to a position class and compensation study, this budget recommends a 3% cost of
living adjustment (COLA); the reclassification of 66 of the 103 individual job positions in
the City; and a reformat of the City’s current grade and step plan to a 21-step system from
the existing 11-steps. The primary objective is to bring the midpoint of each position’s pay
grade to the 50th percentile of the existing market, which for the most part has been
achieved. Those positions not reclassified will be eligible for performance based step
increases in addition to the COLA. Front line / non-executive sworn positions in the Police,
Fire and Fire Marshal Departments will receive both a grade adjustment as well as step
increases in addition to the COLA. Total payroll equals 45% of the operations and
maintenance expenditures across all funds.

•

Personnel headcount was increased nominally with the addition of four new positions in
the Administration, Police, and Building Maintenance Departments. There are
recommended to be 275 total budgeted positions throughout the City.

•

The proposed budget includes a significant investment in infrastructure including $7.4
million in street projects, $3.9 million in water projects, $12.5 million in
wastewater projects, and $3.4 million in drainage projects.

•

The City continues its efforts to improve our ability to fight crime and reduce the fear of
crime as the proposed budget includes $565,000 for the establishment of a real-time
crime center and new tools, technologies and cameras that will greatly increase our
ability to investigate and deter crimes.

•

$965,000 is budgeted to go towards improving IT capabilities, equipment and data security,
updating our operating systems, and transitioning to new and more robust financial and
court management software packages.

•

Staff has worked intentionally to streamline and simplify the entire budget for
better understanding and transparency by removing cumbersome transfers between
funds and moving certain capital expenditures back into the individual department
budgets from the Capital Projects Fund which is now reserved exclusively for grant, debt
service or other

restricted fund related projects. Staff will continue to work through the upcoming fiscal
year to further this endeavor by creating a new General Mobility Fund ahead of Fiscal Year
2026 in which all METRO General Mobility revenues and eligible operations /
maintenance and capital expenses will be housed in.
The Fiscal Year 2025 Budget continues to adhere to several critical guidelines and policy directives that
the City Council has traditionally demanded. The City remains in a very strong financial position, revenues
are budgeted conservatively, which drives our conservative approach to budgeted expenditures in the face
of growing costs. This year’s budget remains balanced between revenues and recurring expenditures while
providing for critical capital improvements and purchases. This budget also addresses our most valuable
resource that we provide to our residents: our employees. By providing a COLA, enacting the findings of
the recently completed compensation study and providing for merit-based step increases – the delivery of
services to our residents will continue to be at the highest of standards and efficiency.
This Budget represents the culmination of months’ worth of work put in by our department heads and the
Finance Staff led by Christina Collins and Jessica Dyess, which cannot go unrecognized and celebrated. It
is a budget that I believe enables the City to continue its path towards re-imagining itself as we approach
the 100th anniversary of our incorporation and begins to chart a course for our next 100 years.
Respectfully submitted,

Jason Stuebe
City Manager
