Through the Office for Dispute Resolution, the Pennsylvania Department of Education (PDE)
fulfills its statutory mandate to maintain a special education due process system. PDE contracts
with the Central Susquehanna Intermediate Unit to provide fiscal and certain management
support for that office, without becoming involved in substantive operations. The hearing officers
and mediators are free from interference or influence on any matters affecting the outcome of
individual mediations and due process hearings. This includes, without limitation, interference
or influence from any entity, individual, or group, such as parents, advocacy groups, school
districts, intermediate units including CSIU, ODR staff, and PDE. At the same time, those
hearing officers and mediators are provided with administrative support, as well as training
delivered in a manner preserving their impartiality through ODR, which itself is also free of such
interference or influence.
The Central Susquehanna Intermediate Unit will not discriminate in educational programs,
activities or employment practices based on race, color, national origin, gender, disability,
marital status, age, religion, sexual orientation, ancestry, union membership or other legally
protected classifications. Announcement of this policy is in accord with Title VI of the Civil Rights
Act of 1964, Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, Section 504 of the Rehabilitation
Act of 1973, and the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990. Employees and program
participants who have an inquiry or complaint of harassment or discrimination or who need
information about accommodations for people with disabilities, should contact Director of
Human Resources, CSIU, 90 Lawton Lane, Milton, PA, 17847, 570-523-1155.

Introduction
The Office for Dispute Resolution (ODR) provides the resources for parents and local
educational agencies (LEAs) to resolve educational disputes involving children served by the
early intervention system, students with disabilities (or thought to have disabilities) and
students who are gifted (or thought to be gifted). Pennsylvania’s recognition as an “exemplar
state” by the Office for Special Education Programs (OSEP) and the Center for Appropriate
Dispute Resolution in Special Education (CADRE) is an acknowledgement of the array of
quality services offered by ODR. During FY 2015-2016, ODR created a listserv to make ODR
news and hearing officer decisions more readily accessible to stakeholders. Individuals can
sign up for the listserv on the ODR website at http://odr-pa.org/subscribe-to-odr/.

ODR Stakeholder Council
The ODR Stakeholder Council (SC) brings together representatives from a broad array of
stakeholder groups to provide functional input and recommendations on the special education
dispute resolution system. Detailed biographical information on each SC member is available
on the ODR website, http://odr-pa.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/Stakeholder-Council-Bios.pdf

Presentations
ODR employees and hearing officers are speakers throughout the year as part of stakeholder
education. The hearing officers, singly and in conjunction with others, presented at a variety of
conferences, including the National Academy for IDEA Administrative Law Judges and
Impartial Hearing Officers (Seattle University School of Law); various Pennsylvania Bar
Institute seminars; and Lehigh University’s Special Education Law Symposium. The ODR
Director spoke to the PAIU special education administrators regarding ODR services, which
resulted in ODR receiving numerous LEA and IU requests for presentations throughout the
Commonwealth. ODR has reached out to the parent centers to explore their interest in
various ODR presentations.
The ODR Program Manager and CADRE staff presented a national webinar on constructive
individualized and systemic approaches to recurring conflict. The ODR Program Manager
continues to provide training on conflict resolution (Creating Agreement) throughout the
Commonwealth to both professionals and paraprofessionals. Staff members provide
information about ODR services, including IEP Facilitation, upon request.

Hearing Officer Training
The hearing officers engage in rigorous professional development each year, both in-house
and through highly regarded professional associations. They are members of the National
Association of Hearing Officials (NAHO), and participate in the certification process. Hearing
officers regularly attend the NAHO conference and/or the National Academy for IDEA
Administrative Law Judges and Impartial Hearing Officers through Seattle University School
of Law, in addition to numerous trainings conducted within the Commonwealth where they are
frequently asked to also participate as session presenters. In-house training in FY 2016-17
1

included a day-long interactive presentation on compensatory education as a remedy in due
process hearings. The hearing officers routinely attend conferences and presentations related
to a myriad of issues impacting children with disabilities.

ODR Projects
Hearing Officer Settlement Conferences
After a successful pilot period, as of March 2017, Hearing Officer Settlement Conferences
(HOSCs) are now a regularly-offered service in any dispute where a complaint has been filed,
and both parties request a HOSC. ODR maintains a randomized list of HOSC hearing officer
assignments. When parties request a HOSC, the next hearing officer on the HOSC list is
assigned to the HOSC (after confirming that the hearing officer does not have an ongoing
case with the family or the LEA—if so, the next hearing officer on the list will receive the
assignment). A general framework applies—a three-sided conversation between the
settlement hearing officer and the attorneys (or LEA attorney and pro se parent) takes place.
Thereafter, the settlement hearing officer has a one-sided conversation with each party. At
that point, each HOSC moves in its own unique direction—sometimes resolution has been
achieved, sometimes involving further collaborative conversations, sometimes involving
further one-sided conversations. The HOSC can continue until the parties feel it has met its
goals, or their conversations can continue without the settlement hearing officer. Data on the
HOSC service is being gathered as each conference concludes. Thus, reliable quantitative
data on a large sample of HOSCs will soon emerge for further direction. Information about
HOSCs is available on the ODR website at http://odr-pa.org/alternative-disputeresolution/hearing-officer-settlement-conference/.
Virtual Hearings Pilot
After a lengthy two-stage pilot, with the ongoing participation of and input from all key
stakeholders (including invaluable hours of volunteer participants), virtual hearings—and
videoconference technology generally—are available as an ODR service as of June 2017.
Using an internet-based, highly-secure videoconference platform, easily accessed through
any webcam-enabled computer, laptop, tablet, or smartphone, virtual hearings will allow for
participants to utilize videoconferencing to increase efficiencies and access in special
education due process. Whether the hearing unfolds with all participants and the hearing
officer gathering in a physical location and a witness participates virtually, or the participants
gather in a physical location and the hearing officer participates virtually, or everyone
participates virtually, the technology allows special education due process to be much more
nimble in responding to the needs of participants in a world where notions of geographicbased processes and “physical presence” become much more flexible. Virtual hearings are
entirely voluntary, only where all participants see value in utilizing videoconference technology
for conducting the whole hearing, or some particular session(s), or the testimony of a
particular witness.

CADRE Multi-State Project
Pennsylvania, via ODR, is one of three states invited by CADRE to participate in an initiative
designed to enhance productive engagement between the state’s parent centers, and the
state educational agency. During FY 2016-2017, ODR remained active in this initiative with
the Parent Engagement Project Manager facilitating and participating in state partner
meetings and projects associated with the initiative. The initial assignment from CADRE was
for each state cohort to identify gaps in their state’s dispute resolution resources, especially
pertaining to alternative dispute resolution services. State cohorts then self-select project
goals, scope and parameter to address identified needs and access to services. Given the
well-established quantity of resources available in Pennsylvania, initiative participants decided
to develop a digital guide that can be posted on partner and stakeholder websites. The guide
will include descriptions, links, forms, publications, and other relevant resources for families
and schools to access dispute resolution services.
HUNE Project
ODR contracts with Hispanos Unidos para Ninos Excepionales (HUNE) to assist in reaching
traditionally underserved Spanish-speaking families in Pennsylvania. Through this unique
collaboration, ODR, through HUNE, is able to provide culturally-responsive outreach activities
and services. During FY 2016/2017, HUNE established the Spanish Help-Line Plus, a
statewide toll-free service where callers speak with a bilingual information specialist about
special education concerns and community-specific resources. The Spanish Help-Line Plus
Specialist receives training from ODR’s ConsultLine Specialists and at times, can serve as
bilingual liaison to assist Spanish-speaking families and other stakeholders in understanding
and facilitating access to early dispute resolution options such as ConsultLine’s Call
Resolution Process (CRP).

Call Resolution Process and Provision of BSE Complaint Packets
ConsultLine’s Call Resolution Process (CRP) is an effective dispute resolution tool for
compliance-related concerns for IDEA-related issues and certain types of concerns related to
Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973. During CRP, the Specialist acts as a conduit of
information between the parent (as defined in 34 CFR 300.30) and the school; the Specialist
does not serve as an advocate, decision-maker, mediator, or compliance advisor.
5

The CRP format and process includes an email summarizing the parent’s compliance-related
concerns, and to the extent known, the parent’s proposed resolution. The email is prepared by
the Specialist in consultation with the parent so that the message accurately reflects the nature
of the issue from the parent’s perspective. Once the message is drafted and reviewed with the
parent, the parent provides verbal permission to send the message to the LEA administrator
responsible for special education, the BSE division chief, and the regional BSE advisor. Once
the email has been sent, the Specialist’s involvement in CRP has concluded. During FY 20162017, CRP was offered to 260 callers, and provided with the parent’s permission in 130
instances. ConsultLine Specialists also explain and distribute information about the BSE
complaint process. Even when a parent participates in CRP---with the hope that it will resolve
the compliance issue---the Specialist still sends a complaint packet in the event the parent is
not satisfied with the outcome of the CRP.
During FY 2016-2017, 231 state complaint forms and information packets were
distributed to callers.

Appeals to State and Federal Court
During FY 2016-2017, there were 27 appeals to state or federal court; only six of these
appeals pertained to 2016-2017 requests for hearing, with the balance pre-dating the
reporting period. Twenty-six appeals were brought under the Individuals with Disabilities
Education Act (IDEA) and one appeal was brought under Chapter 16. The appellant
chose federal court in 85% of the appeals. Parents accounted for 67% of the total
number of appeals received. As of the close of the reporting period, 16 cases were still
in progress, two cases settled, and three cases were dismissed, voluntary or otherwise.
