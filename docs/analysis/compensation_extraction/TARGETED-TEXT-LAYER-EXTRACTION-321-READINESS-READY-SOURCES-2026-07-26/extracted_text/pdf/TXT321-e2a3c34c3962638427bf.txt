Introduction
Case Background
Felicia Bernardini was selected by the parties to serve as Fact Finder in the above referenced
case and duly appointed by the State Employment Relations Board (SERB) on November 1, 2019 in
compliance with Ohio Revised Code (ORC) Section 4117.14C(3). The case concerns a fact finding
proceeding between the City of Monroe, Ohio (hereafter referred to as the “Employer” or the
“City”) and the Monroe Firefighters IAFF, Local 3824 (hereafter referred to as the “Union” or
“Unit”).
Prior to the hearing, the parties engaged in contract negotiations on multiple dates in
September and October 2019. The parties also worked with a SERB mediator; however, no final
settlement was forthcoming, and an impasse was declared. At fact finding, five articles remained
open with unsettled proposals. The hearing was scheduled for January 16, 2020. Both parties timely
filed the required pre-hearing statements. Upon review of the pre-hearing statements it was apparent
that both parties had changed their remaining proposals from where they had positioned themselves
in bargaining and mediation. Based on the new positions of the parties, a brief period of mediation
was entered into with the Fact Finder. A full settlement was not reached in mediation, although the
Employer agreed to withdraw its proposal regarding health insurance premiums and its proposal to
eliminate holiday flex time. The parties proceeded to hearing with the following four open contract
articles.
Article 23: Holidays
Article 24: Vacation
Article 25: Sick Leave
Article 32: Wages

Employer Proposals and Positions
1. Article 32: Wages
The Employer proposes 2% wage increases in each year of the three-year successor
agreement. Like every public jurisdiction, the City of Monroe has its budgetary limits and financial
concerns; however, the City also recognizes that the current economic conditions are favorable, and
the financial forecast is reasonably positive and stable. Under these circumstances the City has
offered what it believes is a reasonable cost of living increase. The City’s proposal is consistent with
statewide averages charted by SERB in its annual wage settlement report and in its reported Fact
Finding/Conciliation statistics. Looking back at SERB’s statewide settlement data from 2015-2018,
the Monroe Firefighter Unit has consistently received general wage increases higher than statewide
averages and higher than statewide averages for all Firefighter units. Similarly, over the same
timeframe the Monroe Firefighter Unit’s wage increases were higher than average wage increases
awarded by fact finders and conciliators statewide. The Employer’s proposal for three 2% wage
increases keeps pace with, and exceeds, the current trend in inflation (as measured by the
Department of Labor’s CPI-W Midwest data) and the COLA provided by the Social Security
Administration to Social Security beneficiaries. To the extent possible the Employer has attempted
to maintain parity among all employee units. Other than in cases where a fact finder or conciliator
has varied from the Employer’s established pattern of bargaining, the City has proposed and
negotiated hard to maintain the same general wage increase for all City bargaining units. Exceptions
have been made by the Employer only when external comparables show that a market-based
adjustment is warranted.
As for external comparables in relation to the Firefighter unit, the Employer maintains that
the City’s Firefighters are properly compensated and therefore no salary adjustment beyond a basic
general wage increase is warranted. The Employer maintains that the following cities are the proper
comparator group: Bedford, Galion, Heath, Montgomery, University Heights, Wilmington and

The Employer rejects the Union’s proposal for 4% increases across all three years of the

2. Article 23: Holidays
The Employer opposes the Union’s proposal to create “super holiday” language that would
require the City to pay employees required to work on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year’s
Day at a rate of 2.25 times the regular rate of pay. More than being a cost issue for the City this is an
issue of internal parity with other bargaining units. Neither the Police Unit nor the Teamster Unit
receive super holiday call-in pay. Although Police Officers receive a premium for working on
Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year’s Day the premium is 1.5 times the regular rate of pay. This
is the same premium rate that Firefighters are paid when working on these holidays and when called
into work on these holidays for emergency purposes. The Employer seeks to maintain the Holiday
contract article as is, with a minor noncontroversial housekeeping edit to change the semiannual
pay-out of holiday pay from the first “payroll” in June and December to the first “paydate” in June
and December.
3. Article 24: Vacations
The Employer rejects the Union’s proposal to increase the vacation leave benefit and seeks
to maintain current contract language with a minor edit to allow for a Fire Chief “designee” to
authorize leave requests and shift changes. The Union has proposed to increase the vacation accrual
rate by 12 hours for unit members with 1-4 years of service and by 24 hours for unit members with
5 or more years of service. Increasing the accrual rates in this manner would increase the City’s cost
by $19K. In addition to this increase in costs, overtime expenses would increase in association with
staffing these additional vacation absences. The Employer projects that the proposed increase of 24

4. Article 25: Sick Leave
The Employer seeks to maintain current contract language in this article. The Union
proposes to increase the accrual rate by two additional hours per month. Over the course of a year
this proposal provides for an additional two days of sick leave per employee. As with the Union’s
vacation leave proposal, more days off for bargaining unit members results in both direct costs of
the leave benefit and overtime costs associated with staffing for these additional days of absence. In
addition, the Union proposes to increase the retirement payout of sick leave. These proposals are
unsupported by internal or external comparables. These proposals are not cost effective for the
Employer and serve no purpose other than to increase time away from work for bargaining unit
members and decrease efficiency. The Employer rejects these proposals in their entirety.

4. Article 25: Sick Leave
The Union proposes to increase the sick leave accrual by 2 hours per month totaling an
additional 24 hours of sick leave accumulation over a year. In addition, the Union seeks to raise the
retirement/resignation payout of accrued but unused sick leave. The Union would increase the
payout from ¼ to ⅓ of accrued but unused sick leave up to a maximum of 720 hour and increase
the payout from ⅓ to ½ of accrued but unused sick leave in excess of 720 hours. As with the
Union’s proposal regarding Vacation Leave, the Sick Leave proposal is supported by both internal
and external comparables. The difference between the Firefighter’s hours of work in a year (i.e.,
2652) is 27.5% greater than the Police Officer’s hours of work in a year (i.e., 2068). To achieve parity
with Police Officers in the City of Monroe the Firefighter’s sick leave accrual must increase. Like
with vacation accruals, a comparison of the Police and Fire Units shows a 13% disparity in sick leave
in favor of the Police Officers. The Union’s proposal to increase the accrual rate by 2 hours per
month would help to close the gap between the two Units’ work hours to sick leave accrual ratios. A
review of external comparables also supports an increase in the sick leave accrual. In comparison to
all 11 comparator municipalities in the area, Monroe Firefighters accrue sick leave at 21.35% below
the average accrual rate. The average accrual rate for the comparator group is 166 hours/year.
Whereas the Monroe accrual rate is 144 hours/year. As for the Union’s proposal to increase the sick
leave sell-back provision upon retirement or resignation, the provision incentivizes unit members to
avoid and minimize the use of sick leave in order to save it and sell it back later at no more than ½
of its accumulated value.

where there is a finding that the Employer has the financial means to fund a wage and benefit

further analysis to determine whether there is an affirmative reason to recommend a more costly
package of wages and benefits. This further analysis includes the comparability of the existing wage
scale with those of other similar units, and parity with other employees under the Employer’s

35 out of the 40 bargaining unit members are paramedics. Approximately 40% of the Unit
(i.e. 16 individuals) has less than 5 years of tenure with the Department. 60% of the unit (i.e., 34
individuals) has 5 years or more of tenure. 13 bargaining unit members have 20 years of service and
will be eligible to retire in 2025. Incorporated in the bargaining unit of 40 are Lieutenants who’s
wage scale is currently 13% higher than the paramedic wage scale. In consideration of these
personnel factors it was represented to the Fact Finder that a reasonable estimate of a 1% wage
increase for the bargaining unit would increase overall personnel costs by approximately $36,000 per
year. This figure accounts for an average wage for all bargaining unit members and rollup costs of
pension, Medicare, and other such payroll costs. Furthermore, in terms of the cost of proposals and
their affordability, it was fairly represented to the Fact Finder that the cost associated with the
Union’s proposal to increase vacation accrual by 12 hours for unit members with less than five years
of tenure and by 24 hours for unit members with greater than 5 years of tenure can be calculated at
$19,000 for the additional vacation time, plus approximately $33,000 in associated overtime costs to
cover the increased days of absence. This makes the value of the Union’s vacation proposal roughly
equivalent to a 1.5% wage increase.

of the jurisdictions’ financial characteristics (e.g., tax base, revenue collections) and/or the
Departments’ demographic characteristics (e.g., square miles covered, calls per year, density and
types of structures).

In bargaining, Employers are often subject to significant pressure to maintain parity among
employee groups, most especially between the safety forces of Police and Fire. These are highly
competitive bargaining units that closely scrutinize each other’s labor agreements documenting any

perceived advantage. However, Employers also seek to rely on parity to keep advances in wages and
benefits in check. For this Fact Finder, internal parity is best when focused on general wage
increases. It makes little, to no, sense to compare the wage scales of diverse bargaining units for each
such scale is tailored to the job classification it covers. Each job classification is associated with its
own particular mix of duties, responsibilities, obligations and advantages. Just as comparing wage
scales across bargaining units is an uninformative exercise, so too is the comparison of leave benefits
across diverse units. In this case, the Union has sought to make a case for increased time off for
Firefighters based on a comparison of the difference between the number of hours worked in a
typical year by Firefighters and Police Officers. Police Officers work 2068 hours in a year, whereas
Firefighters work 2652 hours in a year - 27.5% more. Based on this calculation the Union argues that
accrued time off for Firefighters should be increased proportionately in order to maintain parity with
Police Officers. The Employer counters that Firefighters receive 2 days off after every 24-hour shift
they work. Thus, Firefighters are off work 243 days in an average year. Vacation, sick leave and
holidays are provided in addition to regularly scheduled days off resulting in Firefighters having over
100 days off more per year than Police Officers. There is simply no reasonable way to make an hour
for hour comparison across safety force units. Individuals enter their chosen career with an
understanding of the particular mix of obligations and advantages that come with the profession.
Enjoying all the advantages of one’s own profession while at the same time seeking to incorporate
the advantages of a distinctly different profession is an overreach and in this Fact Finder’s opinion a
misuse of the concept of parity.
What is of interest to the Fact Finder is that the general wage increases for the Monroe
Police and Fire Units have varied somewhat in the last two rounds of bargaining as a result of the
Police Unit going to fact finding and conciliation. In 2014 Firefighters did not receive a general wage
increase, whereas the Police Unit received a 2% general wage increase. In 2019 the Firefighters
received a general wage increase of 2.5% and the Police Unit received an increase of 3%. Overall,
the Firefighters have fallen behind an important comparator by 2.5% over the past 6 years. It would
be reasonable to consider an overall settlement for the Firefighter Unit that acknowledges this
disparity and moves to ameliorate it somewhat.

1. Wages:
The Employer has stated that “its primary goal in this fact finding is to provide a reasonable
wage increase and keep the Union’s above-average placement within the relevant comparable
jurisdictions.”15 In this instance the Fact Finder cannot validate or support the full comparator group
the Employer has used to advance its position. The relevant data provided to the Fact Finder reveals
that the Monroe Firefighter/Paramedic wage scale is below average. Perhaps not as far below
average as the Union’s data suggests; however, below average, nonetheless. With both comparator
groups having their respective outliers, the Fact Finder is cautious not to overstate how far below
average the Monroe Unit truly is. The second goal of the Employer is “to maintain operational
efficiency and avoid contractual changes that result in greater leave time for the Union.”16
Meanwhile, the Union’s primary goal, clearly stated to the Fact Finder on the day of the hearing, is
to improve upon the Units below average wage scale and restore internal parity. In consideration of
the Parties’ stated priorities the Fact Finder is recommending that the economic improvements for
this settlement be concentrated in wages.
The Employer’s across the board 2% proposal is somewhat low in comparison to SERB
data, and wage settlement data from the relevant external comparator group(s); however, it is not far
out of the ballpark for a general cost of living adjustment. Therefore, the COLA recommend by the
Fact Finder is 2.5% in each of the three years of the agreement. 2.5% is this Fact Finder’s
recommendation for inter-bargaining unit parity purposes within the City of Monroe. In addition,
the Unit’s below average wage scale and past below-parity general wage increases warrants an
additional market/equity adjustment. The Fact Finder recommends an additional 3.5% over the
term of the contract. More specifically the Fact Finder recommends an additional 1.5% in the first
year of the contract, 1% in the second year of the contract and 1% in the third year of the contract.
The Union’s proposal to reintroduce certification pay incentives for Intermediate and FDMedic levels is not supported at this time. The Fact Finder’s recommended market/equity

wage scale and payroll process. Furthermore, through mutual agreement the certification levels were
eliminated in a recent past round of negotiations. Thus, reintroduction of them is best left to the

there is insufficient evidence in the provided data to support this proposal. As for the Union’s
