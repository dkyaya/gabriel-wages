PROCLAMATION
Recognizing the Accomplishments of
Sophie Ann Baker
As a West Dundee Resident, Student, and Placing 3rd in the USA Weightlifting
National Championships
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker has already achieved extraordinary academic and athletic
feats as she enters 7th grade at Dundee Middle School; and
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker is a high-grade student, achieving A’s in her honors classes
and outperforming her competition in every sport she has involved herself with;
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker began her weightlifting and CrossFit journey at age 8 and
has demonstrated her winning attitude through various achievements such as placing 3rd place
overall in the “Snatch” and “Clean and Jerk” events in the USA Weightlifting National Championships
and placing 5th in national CrossFit competitions; and
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker has also excelled in soccer with the Fox Valley Bayern
Soccer Club, achieving 11 hat tricks, holding the single-game scoring record of 7 goals, and scoring
from the midfield on kickoff 5 times, including once within the first 8 seconds of the game; and
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker further expands her athletic abilities by participating on the
Dundee Middle School and Dundee Park District basketball teams and prepares for high school level
competition through her involvement in the D300 feeder program; and
WHEREAS, Sophie Ann Baker has shown her ambition for high achievement in all her
passions and has, at such a young age, become a commendable part of the West Dundee
Community; and
WHEREAS, the Village West Dundee would like to formally recognize Sophie Ann Baker for
her significant accomplishment of placing in the 2024 USA Weightlifting National Championships;
NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved that I, Christopher J. Nelson, acting under the virtue of
the authority vested in me by Constitution of the State of Illinois and the Laws of this Village of West
Dundee, hereby proclaim Monday, August 19th, 2024 as

SOPHIE ANN BAKER DAY

in the Village of West Dundee and urges all Village residents and businesses to join in its
observance.

In Witness Thereof, I have hereunto set my hand and affixed the great
seal of the Village of West Dundee at my office on August 19, 2024.

_______________________________
Christopher Nelson, Village President

PROCLAMATION
CELEBRATING HALF FULL NEST’S 5TH ANNIVERSARY
IN THE HEART OF DOWNTOWN WEST DUNDEE
WHEREAS, The Half Full Nest Vintage Market, located at 205 West Main Street, nestled
within the historic walls of a cherished 1882 church, has become a sight for vintage charm and
creativity to the West Dundee community since its opening in August 2019; and
WHEREAS, Angelike Ajroja, founder and curator, has brought her lifelong passion for
design and dedication to preserving the beauty of the past to our beloved town; and
WHEREAS, The Half Full Nest Vintage Market has created a space that shares vintage
styles from Farmhouse to French Country, from Industrial to Mid Century; and
WHEREAS, The Half Full Nest Vintage Market has offered not only a shopping experience,
but a journey through history, design, and the art of creating a home filled with warmth and
personality; and
WHEREAS, The Half Full Nest Vintage Market has enriched our community through events
such as the Vintage Shop Hop, the Half Ugly Christmas Sweater Soirée, and workshops that
celebrate craftsmanship and creativity; and
WHEREAS, The Half Full Nest Vintage Market has exemplified the spirit of
entrepreneurship, creativity, and dedication to quality that defines our local businesses; and
WHEREAS, the Village of West Dundee does hereby congratulate The Half Full Nest Vintage
Market on its 5th Anniversary for its commitment and contribution to the vibrancy of our West
Dundee community.
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT PROCLAIMED, that I, Christopher J. Nelson, acting under and by
the authority vested in me by the Constitution of the State of Illinois and Laws of this Village of
West Dundee, hereby proclaim Thursday, August 15th, 2024 as Half Full Nest Day.
In Witness Thereof, I have hereunto set my hand and affixed the great
seal of the Village of West Dundee at my office on August 5, 2024.

_______________________________
Christopher Nelson, Village President

SUBJECT:

305 Oregon Avenue (The Mansion Bed & Breakfast) – Consideration of an
Ordinance Approving a Special Use Permit to allow (1) Single Room Occupancy
Units and (2) Apartments on the ground floor within the R-B Zoning District

INTRODUCTION
At its March 25, 2024 meeting, the Planning & Zoning Commission (PZC) conducted a Public
Hearing to consider the petition of Mr. Steve Fang, seeking a Special Use Permit to allow (1) Single
Room Occupancy (SRO) Units and (2) Apartments on the ground floor at 305 Oregon Avenue,
within the R-B Zoning District.
Prior to that consideration, the Board will recall approving an amendment to the West Dundee
Municipal Code that added SROs and ground floor apartments as potential special uses within the
R-B District, adopted via Ordinance 2024-04 on February 19, 2024.
BACKGROUND
Public Hearing: Attached please find a copy of the approved March 25, 2024 PZC Meeting Minutes,
which offer a summary of public comments, questions and discussions. At the conclusion of the
Public Hearing, PZC members voted unanimously to recommend approval of the special use
request.
Based on the Public Hearing, a condition was added to PZC’s recommendation to limit the number
of SRO units and ground floor apartments to no more than are currently present, in an effort to
preserve the existing density and prohibit intensification of use.
Comments were received from a neighboring property owner both before and after the Public
Hearing requesting that the Village require a privacy fence along the site’s rear property line.
Following discussion, PZC did not include this within their recommendation for approval of the
special use request. Reservations were expressed in both public comments and PZC discussion
regarding extension of the proposed special use to a future operator without the benefit of additional
review.
Location / Site Context: The Mansion, as it is known today, is situated just outside of the Downtown
Business District and is surrounded by residentially zoned properties (single family residences and a
funeral home), with two on-site parking lots, accessible from Oregon Avenue and Third Street.
The existing structure was initially constructed shortly after 1900 as a single-family home and is a
significant contributing property to the Dundee Township Historic District due to both its history and
its architecture. While modifications have been made to the structure over time, including connecting
the original carriage house to the main house, the building has been largely preserved and stands as
something of a time capsule both inside and out.

Preservation: Of keen interest to the Village and community is the preservation of this stately
edifice. As market demands change over time, flexibility of use within the context of the residential
neighborhood and nearby downtown district is needed to facilitate the continued active operation
and preservation of the property.
Potential Reuse: While the site was being marketed for sale, a variety of inquiries were received
regarding reuse. Inquiries have varied from maintaining the current operations with little to no
physical improvements, restoring the main house as a single-family residence while maintaining
apartments in the carriage house, redeveloping the interior to accommodate modern apartments, an
assisted living facility (reportedly a previous use of the building), and a return to a more traditional
bed and breakfast establishment, perhaps with a small eatery. While each of these potential uses
could pose challenges, thus far, none of these inquiries have led to detailed exploration to date.
Current Licensing: The property has been licensed to operate as a Bed & Breakfast, having 8 total
rooms or units. The units offer a range of amenities, including shared cooking or bathroom facilities
in some cases. Per the municipal code, Bed & Breakfast units may be rented for a maximum stay of
15 consecutive days.
The site also includes three long term apartments (with a total of 5 bedrooms) in the carriage house,
with one 2-bedroom and one 1-bedroom apartment on the ground floor and one 2-bedroom
apartment on the second floor, each with a typical lease duration of 12 months or more. While the
apartments have been in existence for many years, the R-B zoning regulations did not allow ground
floor apartments until the adoption of Ordinance 2024-04, adding this as a potential special use.
Current Operations: The 8 aforementioned Bed & Breakfast units are currently being utilized for
stays of more than 31 days. These accommodations do not meet the standards of a full dwelling unit
and the lengths of stay exceed that of a Bed & Breakfast unit or a Short Term Rental, therefore
these 8 units are considered Single Room Occupancy units by definition.
The petitioner is seeking a special use permit to align the SRO and ground floor apartment
operations with the underlying R-B zoning.
The R-B zoning and Bed & Breakfast license provisions allow for the units in question to be rented
for 15 consecutive days or less. The requested Special Use Permit would allow the same existing
units to be rented for 31 consecutive days or more. Length of stay is the only change proposed.
Outside Review Not Supported: As a refresher, at the VBT meeting of July 15th the topic of outside
review by a third-party with professional expertise in historic preservation and adaptive reuse was
discussed, but the potential endeavor did not receive enough formal support to authorize a request
for proposals for such consultative services.
RECOMMENDATION
Rental Business Model: Broadly characterized, this residentially-focused approach to The Mansion
has not yielded complaints from the neighborhood, particularly with respect to noise or overflowing
parking, or generated a heightened volume of calls for public safety services.
In addition to contributing to neighborhood stability, the current business model has reportedly
provided a much-needed level of economic/financial certainty and predictability which in turn
generates proceeds that can be used to maintain this large, complex, and resource-intensive historic
building and property.

Consideration of Potential Conditions to Special Use Permit: If there were VBT interest in granting

(1) Approval of a Special Use Permit to allow for (a) 8 Single Room Occupancy (SRO) units, which

(2) As a condition, the Village could remove the previously agreed to Bed & Breakfast license

order to obtain licensing, whether the proposed business model may include SRO, ground floor
apartments or other uses not afforded by right within the R-B zoning district.

approve a Special Use Permit to allow up to 8 SRO units and up to 2 ground-level apartments,

The Public Hearing opened at 7:32 pm.
Chairman Martin opened the floor to Director Scott to introduce the petition. Village Planner Tibbetts
confirmed that notices regarding this Public Hearing were published according to law.
Director Scott outlined the recent Municipal Code text amendment recommended by PZC and
approved by the Village Board of Trustees (VBT) via Ordinance 2024-04, which included the addition

of single room occupancy (SRO) units and ground floor apartments as special uses within the R-B
Zoning District. He noted that a review of the property in question was the impetus for this text
amendment, to align the property’s current use with the underlying zoning regulations and facilitate
the continued active use and economic viability (and, in turn, preservation) of this significant historic
property.
Director Scott noted that the special uses requested reflect the current use of the property. He advised
that the business model in place at the property has evolved with market demands over the past few
years to facilitate longer guest stays. With no changes to the building’s layout, the portions of the
facility which previously operated as a Bed and Breakfast (maximum length of stay of 15 days by
code), have transformed to offering single room occupancy units for a minimum stay of three weeks.
It was noted that, while it was only recently brought to staff’s attention, the ground floor apartments
located in the south end of the building have been in place for many years. He further noted that there
have not been excessive calls for service to the property in concert with the current uses.
Director Scott called PZC’s attention to feedback received in advance of the meeting from two
neighboring property owners: Mr. Doug Bober of 310 Liberty Street, in support of the request, and
Mrs. Barbara Haines of 314 Liberty Street, requesting consideration be given to requiring a privacy
fence to screen the property’s parking lot from its neighbors.
Chairman Martin opened the floor for public comments.
Planner Tibbetts swore in Mr. Steve Fang (petitioner) and Ms. Eda Tomasone of 418 Oregon Avenue,
owners of The Mansion. Chairman Martin inquired whether the facility no longer operates as a Bed
and Breakfast. Mr. Fang advised that, while the property is still able to accommodate Bed and
Breakfast guests upon request / based on availability, demand for this type of lodging has become
uncommon, with interest in extended stays (SROs) prevalent. Director Scott noted that Bed and
Breakfast lodging remains a permitted use within the zoning district, with length of stay being the
primary difference between the two.
Commissioner Seaton noted that the minimum length of stay proposed for SROs is 31 days (to
differentiate SROs from both Bed and Breakfast and Short Term Rental lodging units), whereas Mr.
Fang previously stated their policy is for a minimum stay of 3 weeks (21 days). Mr. Fang indicated that
most stay longer, and he and Ms. Tomasone prefer the stability of extended stay guests, hence a 31
day minimum would be agreeable.
Commissioner Magsamen inquired whether the addition of a privacy fence had been broached prior
to the special use request, to which Mr. Fang indicated it had not. He stated that the rear property line
along the south/southwest edge of the property contains a chicken wire and chain link barrier, with a
fence of varying heights present on the neighboring property.
Planner Tibbetts swore in Mr. Michael Dewey of 411 Oregon Avenue, who spoke in support of the
request. Mr. Dewey advised that he has lived down the street from the property for 34 years with no

WHEREAS, The Village of West Dundee, Kane County, Illinois (the “Village”), is a duly organized
and validly existing home rule municipality pursuant to Article VII, Section 6(a) of the Constitution of the
State of Illinois, and the passage of this Ordinance constitutes an exercise of the Village’s home rule
powers and functions as granted in Constitution of the State of Illinois; and,
WHEREAS, the Village President and Board of Trustees have contemplated certain changes
to the West Dundee Municipal Code to define “single room occupancy” and amend R-B ResidentialBusiness Zoning District regulations within Title 10 – Zoning Regulations and to amend the permitted
uses, special uses, lot area requirements and additional regulations for the R-B District; and,

single room occupancies and R-B District regulations via public hearing on January 29, 2024, after

Authorization to Purchase Public Works Vehicle Replacements

INTRODUCTION:
Public Works is requesting replacement of two fleet vehicles; a 2002 Ford F-550 1-ton dump
truck with 78,936 miles (Vehicle # 14) and a 2007 Ford pickup with 108,372 miles (Vehicle #
33). Both vehicles are being replaced due to age, excessive engine wear, transmission
problems, suspension failures and body deterioration.
BACKGROUND:
In a typical vehicle replacement scenario, the Village works with a joint purchasing
organization that solicits competitive bids for both the vehicles and the upfitting services. The
most commonly utilized groups in recent years have been the Illinois State Bid program, the
Suburban Purchasing Cooperative (SPC) and Sourcewell (formerly the NJPA).
Unfortunately, staff discovered that there is no program from the State Bid or Sourcewell for
pickup trucks this year, leaving SPC as the sole option for joint purchasing/competitive
bidding. Public Works has been attempting to engage SPC to facilitate the ordering process
for both vehicles since mid-May of this year to no avail. Correspondence has been slow, and
they have been either unwilling or unable to provide the contract documentation necessary to
place orders despite consistent and repeated requests.
With no means in which to determine when or if SPC will cooperate, Public Works began
looking at other purchasing options starting with Monroe Trucking Company. Given their
experience working with numerous manufacturers, dealerships and joint purchasing groups,
they were able to point us in the direction of a few viable alternatives.
Vehicle # 14 Replacement
For the 1-ton dump truck replacement (#14), there are two options; 1. Order a new 2024 cab
& chassis directly from Currie Ford in Frankfort, IL and have upfitting done by Monroe or 2.
Purchase a new fully upfitted, ready-to-go 2023 truck currently on the lot at Dick Masheter
Ford in Columbus, OH. Option # 1 represents a total cost that is $91 more than the
referenced SPC joint purchasing price and a delivery time of 6 to 8 months. Option # 2
represents a total cost that is $3,604.01 more than the referenced SPC joint purchasing price
and is available immediately.
Being this late in the year, the problem with Option # 1 is that the fully upfitted truck will not
be delivered until after the upcoming winter season. With Vehicle # 14 being a front-line plow
truck and the possibility of additional snow removal responsibilities coming for Ring Road,
this option is not ideal. For this reason, staff recommends utilizing Option # 2 for the
replacement of Vehicle #14 to have the plow truck when it is needed.

SUBJECT:
Authorization to Initiate Auction for Items and Fixtures at Spring Hill Mall
__________________________________________________________________________
INTRODUCTION:
Since the acquisition of the former Sears, Macy’s, and Spring Hill Mall, the Village has explored
options as how to best distribute any items leftover by the mall or its former tenants. Staff believes
that there were items left behind that may be of value and could be re-used or re-purposed in lieu of
discarding them during demolition.
BACKGROUND:
Obenauf Auction Service Inc. of Round Lake, IL specializes in offering professional auctioneering
services, primarily to municipalities and other public entities. Staff is looking into having Obenauf
catalog items and offer them on an online auction. Village staff would tag any items that we believe
are of value and would be worth auctioning off.
In order to minimize the liability of having the public walk through the mall, staff would instruct
Winger Building Services to consolidate items into one location, preferably a truck dock. Obenauf
would catalog the items in the truck dock, and after the auction Village staff would coordinate with
Obenauf and Winger to schedule a day for the public to pick up any items they won at auction.
Using an auction appears to be the most straightforward and fair method of distributing any leftover
items at the mall. Proceeds from the auction would be used to help cover the expense of man hours
that would be needed to consolidate and catalog the items by bother Winger and Obenauf.
FISCAL IMPACT:
The intention is to have a net zero fiscal impact. Staff estimates that around 100 man hours would
be needed to consolidate and catalog the items with an expense of around $5,000. Staff will only
offer items that we feel will offset the cost of the logistics needed to auction them off.
RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends authorizing Winger Building Services and Obenauf Auction Service Inc. to
facilitate an auction of items at Spring Hill Mall that Village staff has approved for auction.
MOTION:
Move to authorize Obenauf Auction Service Inc. and Winger Building Services to initiate an auction
of items at Spring Hill Mall that Village staff approves for auction.
