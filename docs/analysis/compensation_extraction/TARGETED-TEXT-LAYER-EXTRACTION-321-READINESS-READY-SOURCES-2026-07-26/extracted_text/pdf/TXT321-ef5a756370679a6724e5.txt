INTRODUCTION

The State Employment Relations Board (SERB) appointed the undersigned as
conciliator for this public employment labor dispute on August 28, 2019 in accordance
with Ohio Revived Code Section 4117.14 (D) (1). The Parties agreed to November 7,
2019, for the hearing of this case. They filed timely position statements before the
hearing with the conciliator and served each other. The Hearing was held at the offices
of Perry Township, 622 Genoa Avenue S.W., Massillon, Ohio.

2

Mon, 12/30/2019 03:23:07 PM SERB

The conciliation process is a final offer issue-by-issue arbitration [Ohio Revised Code
Section 4117.14(G)]. By statute, it follows the fact-finding dispute resolution procedure.
Employees subject to the conciliation process do not have the right to strike. [Ohio
Revised Code Sections 4117.14(D)(2) and 4117.15(A)]

There are four bargaining units in the Township: Police Patrol Officers and Detectives
represented by the F.O.P.; Police Sergeants also represented by the F.O.P.; Road. and
Bridge Workers represented by A.F.S.C.M.E.; and the Firefighters Local 3960 I.A.F.F.
The Firefighters Local 3960 bargaining unit is comprised of twelve (12) Firefighters,
three (3) Captains and approximately 35 to 40 part-time Firefighters. The IAFF
members work a three-platoon system, one 24-hour work day on duty followed by 48
hours off-duty, averaging a work week of 53 hours split across three shifts with five (5)
members assigned to each shift.

The Employer is the Board of Trustees for Perry Township, a three-member Board. It is
the legislative and governing authority over the Perry Township Fire and Emergency
Medical Services Department. It was organized and is operating under the authority of
Ohio Revived Code Chapter 505, specifically Ohio Revised Code Sections 505.37 to
505.44.

Perry Township is located in Stark County between the cities of Canton, Ohio and
Massillon, Ohio. The Township has a population of 29,167 and covers an area of
approximately 24 square miles with roughly 350 businesses. The Township provides a
full range of municipal services including Police and Fire protection, street maintenance
and repair, park services, and other general administrative and community services.
The Township is financed by property taxes, Local Government Fund (LGF), cable
franchise fees, and fees for services.

The Parties collective bargaining agreement (CBA) expired on December 3, 2018, but
has been extended by mutual agreement. There have been five (5) bargaining
sessions, a request for mediation which was not mutually agreed to, and a Fact-Finding
hearing held on March 7, 2019 with Fact-Finder Dennis E. Minni, Esquire. The
Township rejected the Fact-Finder’s report and recommendation.

This Conciliator met with both spokespersons to explore whether or not they could use
the Fact-Finder’s report as a basis for the possibility of mediation. Both Parties were
willing to spend a few hours in mediation in an attempt to reach resolution. After an hour
and half, the main issues still were unresolved and everyone agreed to proceed to
hearing.

At the opening of the conciliation hearing, the Township wanted to introduce three
procedural issues:

1. The Employer’s response to the Union’s motion to strike the Employer’s
Conciliation position statement due to the untimely submission of the Township’s
position statement to the State Employment Relation Board.

2. The Employer’s position that the Conciliator is not bound by the Fact-Finder’s
recommendation, and that the Conciliator has considerable amount of deference
with regard to the fact-finder’s report. The Union believes the appropriate
standard is that unless there is a clear error by the Fact-Finder, the Fact-Finder’s
recommendations should stand.

3. It is the Township’s position that due to the fact that the Union did not object to
the Fact-Finder’s award, only those objections raised by the Township are
properly before the Conciliator. The Union should not be allowed to participate.

Discussion and Award:
At the hearing, Management presented arguments that going to a 50.4-hour work week
would significantly impact Kelly Days and Earned Days Off (EDO). The Union rebutted
this, and said that Management is not fully utilizing the part timers within the fire
department. It was the Union’s belief that the part time employees are not meeting the
minimum thirty (30) hours that is required for the month as well as weekend
requirements. Fact-Finder Minni found that looking at the professional fire fighter
departments in Stark county provided a fair representation of what the comparables
should be. Union Exhibit 13, Stark County’s 2018-19 Hourly and Annual Salary
Comparables, included average work week hours for the eight municipalities within
Stark County. Currently, the Perry Township work week hours are 53.0 hours. The
average for this grouping is 50.6. The Perry Township work week hours are higher than
the average of the surrounding municipalities/townships but equal to another nearby
department in North Canton. Half of the eight municipalities are at 52.0 to 53.0 hours.
These figures may have led Management to their proposal of reducing the work week to
52 hours. This Conciliator also took into consideration the complete economic package,
which included reducing the wage table, eliminating Probationary Rate, compressing
the remaining steps. After reviewing these facts as stated above and the exhibits
presented, it is this Conciliator’s decision to accept the Management proposal of a 52hour work week effective January 1, 2020 as stated above.

articles like Article 17 Hours of Work. The Union proposed no wage structure based on
a 52-hour week. While Management made a proposal based on a 52-hour shift, some

Conciliator does not want to accept a proposal that reduces annual salaries of some
employees.

Both Parties tentatively agreed to a compression of the wage table, replacing the
Probationary Step with FF/P1 year and compressing the remaining steps. This change
in the wage table provides an economic increase in and of itself.

amount is $750.00 for probationary and non-probationary employees, Management
proposed reducing the amount to $700.00 for probationary employees and $500.00 for
non-probationary employees. $750.00 is already less than the average allowance for
this benefit in Stark County so this Conciliator sees no reason to reduce the amount.

comparables, this Conciliator position it to accept the Union’s position as stated above.
