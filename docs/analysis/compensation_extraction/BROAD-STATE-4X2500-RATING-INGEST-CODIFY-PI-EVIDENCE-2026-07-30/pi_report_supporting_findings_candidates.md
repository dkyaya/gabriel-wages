# PI report supporting finding candidates

## COLA/CPI language is a recurring contract mechanism

Within the processed rated corpus, COLA/CPI and inflation-indexing language appears as a recurring contract mechanism for scheduled wage adjustment.

Evidence basis: 394 valid ratings; mean/median strength 2.07/2.00.

Boundary: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Across-the-board percentage increases recur in compensation language

Among valid rated spans, across-the-board percentage and growth values provide recurring documentary evidence of scheduled compensation changes.

Evidence basis: 1,381 valid ratings; mean/median strength 2.41/2.00.

Boundary: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Step and schedule progression provide structured wage paths

Rank, step, grade, specialization, and classification structures recur in report-usable evidence and provide plausible channels for wage progression within bargaining units.

Evidence basis: 1,429 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.24/1.00.

Boundary: Cross-unit comparison requires alignment of ranks, grades, steps, occupations, and effective periods. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Bargaining and dispute resolution are plausible institutional mechanisms

Rated evidence identifies bargaining, arbitration, factfinding, settlement, and strike/no-strike language as plausible institutional channels shaping municipal compensation terms in some documents.

Evidence basis: 1,716 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.59/1.00.

Boundary: The evidence identifies documentary mechanisms, not treatment effects or proof that dispute resolution raises wages. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Market and staffing pressures appear as compensation justifications

Within the processed rated corpus, market comparability, recruitment, retention, staffing shortages, and competing-jurisdiction language appear as candidate explanations for compensation changes.

Evidence basis: 398 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.37/1.00.

Boundary: These are local documentary justifications and cannot be generalized to all municipalities. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.
