## 4. Findings

### Non-base compensation is a distinct compensation-growth channel

Within the processed rated corpus, non-base compensation appears as a substantial compensation channel through longevity, shift, hazard, specialty, certification, education, stipend, allowance, and premium-pay provisions.

Caveat: These components require separate classification from base pay before comparative compensation analysis.

### Direct base-wage and schedule evidence is quantitatively promising

The processed rated corpus contains a substantial pool of direct compensation values and wage schedules that is potentially useful for quantitative analysis after normalization.

Caveat: Hourly/annual units, base/non-base status, occupation, rank, step, effective period, and municipality-cycle alignment remain unresolved.

### Implementation timing and retroactivity shape realized timing

Rated evidence frequently identifies implementation dates, retroactivity, settlement timing, and delayed payment as mechanisms that can affect when negotiated wage gains are realized.

Caveat: Timing language is not a normalized measure of realized growth and does not by itself establish a safety/non-safety difference.

### Automatic raises recur as a documentary wage-growth mechanism

Within the processed rated corpus, automatic wage-growth provisions—including scheduled increases, percentage raises, and indexed adjustments—form a recurring documentary mechanism.

Caveat: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality.

### Rank, step, specialization, and classification structures support progression

Rank, step, grade, specialization, and classification structures recur in report-usable evidence and provide plausible channels for wage progression within bargaining units.

Caveat: Cross-unit comparison requires alignment of ranks, grades, steps, occupations, and effective periods.

### Quantitative evidence requires normalization before comparison

The processed rated corpus contains a substantial pool of direct compensation values and wage schedules that is potentially useful for quantitative analysis after normalization.

Caveat: Hourly/annual units, base/non-base status, occupation, rank, step, effective period, and municipality-cycle alignment remain unresolved.

### COLA/CPI language is a recurring contract mechanism

Within the processed rated corpus, COLA/CPI and inflation-indexing language appears as a recurring contract mechanism for scheduled wage adjustment.

Caveat: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality.

### Across-the-board percentage increases recur in compensation language

Among valid rated spans, across-the-board percentage and growth values provide recurring documentary evidence of scheduled compensation changes.

Caveat: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality.

### Step and schedule progression provide structured wage paths

Rank, step, grade, specialization, and classification structures recur in report-usable evidence and provide plausible channels for wage progression within bargaining units.

Caveat: Cross-unit comparison requires alignment of ranks, grades, steps, occupations, and effective periods.

### Bargaining and dispute resolution are plausible institutional mechanisms

Rated evidence identifies bargaining, arbitration, factfinding, settlement, and strike/no-strike language as plausible institutional channels shaping municipal compensation terms in some documents.

Caveat: The evidence identifies documentary mechanisms, not treatment effects or proof that dispute resolution raises wages.

### Strike and no-strike constraints form part of bargaining context

Strike and no-strike provisions recur in the rated bargaining context and are plausible institutional constraints on wage-setting processes.

Caveat: The evidence identifies documentary mechanisms, not treatment effects or proof that dispute resolution raises wages.

### Market and staffing pressures appear as compensation justifications

Within the processed rated corpus, market comparability, recruitment, retention, staffing shortages, and competing-jurisdiction language appear as candidate explanations for compensation changes.

Caveat: These are local documentary justifications and cannot be generalized to all municipalities.
