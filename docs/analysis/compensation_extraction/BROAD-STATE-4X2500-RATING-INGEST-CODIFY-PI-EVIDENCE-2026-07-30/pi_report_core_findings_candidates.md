# PI report core finding candidates

## Non-base compensation is a distinct compensation-growth channel

Within the processed rated corpus, non-base compensation appears as a substantial compensation channel through longevity, shift, hazard, specialty, certification, education, stipend, allowance, and premium-pay provisions.

Evidence basis: 3,718 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.65/1.00.

Boundary: These components require separate classification from base pay before comparative compensation analysis. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Direct base-wage and schedule evidence is quantitatively promising

The processed rated corpus contains a substantial pool of direct compensation values and wage schedules that is potentially useful for quantitative analysis after normalization.

Evidence basis: 11,610 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.45/1.00.

Boundary: Hourly/annual units, base/non-base status, occupation, rank, step, effective period, and municipality-cycle alignment remain unresolved. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Implementation timing and retroactivity shape realized timing

Rated evidence frequently identifies implementation dates, retroactivity, settlement timing, and delayed payment as mechanisms that can affect when negotiated wage gains are realized.

Evidence basis: 2,230 valid ratings; mean/median strength 2.01/2.00.

Boundary: Timing language is not a normalized measure of realized growth and does not by itself establish a safety/non-safety difference. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Automatic raises recur as a documentary wage-growth mechanism

Within the processed rated corpus, automatic wage-growth provisions—including scheduled increases, percentage raises, and indexed adjustments—form a recurring documentary mechanism.

Evidence basis: 1,431 valid ratings; mean/median strength 2.57/2.00.

Boundary: This describes rated contract language; it does not establish national prevalence, comparative wage effects, or causality. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Rank, step, specialization, and classification structures support progression

Rank, step, grade, specialization, and classification structures recur in report-usable evidence and provide plausible channels for wage progression within bargaining units.

Evidence basis: 763 valid ratings; mean/median strength 2.43/2.00.

Boundary: Cross-unit comparison requires alignment of ranks, grades, steps, occupations, and effective periods. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.

## Quantitative evidence requires normalization before comparison

The processed rated corpus contains a substantial pool of direct compensation values and wage schedules that is potentially useful for quantitative analysis after normalization.

Evidence basis: 11,548 valid ratings; mean/median strength 1.45/1.00.

Boundary: Hourly/annual units, base/non-base status, occupation, rank, step, effective period, and municipality-cycle alignment remain unresolved. Does not by itself establish causality, population/national prevalence, or a normalized wage gap.
