# Reader guide

## Page 1 — The question and the comparison

### What this project asked

I asked why police and fire wages may grow differently from wages in other municipal occupations. The strongest comparison holds the municipality and compensation cycle fixed, then compares a safety bargaining unit with a non-safety unit. A local comparison is usable only when both sides have compatible roles, periods, and pay measures.

The records also show how pay is negotiated, authorized, scheduled, and implemented. Those records can establish that a compensation mechanism operates even when they cannot support a clean wage difference. The atlas therefore separates comparative results from mechanism evidence. It does not treat a large document count as a matched wage panel.

### Who is on each side

**Safety** means police, fire, or a combined public-safety unit. **Non-safety** means other municipal occupations, such as clerical, public works, sanitation, parks, libraries, transit, or health roles. **Mixed** means a record covers both sides. **Side-independent** means the process applies across municipal roles without a defensible side assignment. **Unresolved** means the retained evidence does not support a reliable side classification.

## Page 2 — Evidence types and analytical units

### Five evidence statuses

**Strict evidence** has an exact subject, municipality, compatible period, side, pay basis, source identity, and reproducible calculation. It can support precise but bounded statements.

**Bounded evidence** is analytically usable with an explicit caveat, such as a same-cycle period match or a closely comparable role. It can support conditional comparisons, not an unrestricted national estimate.

**Directional evidence** identifies a mechanism or direction without a defensible magnitude. It can show how pay pressure operates, but it cannot establish a point estimate.

**Context** explains the setting without directly supporting a claim. **Rejected evidence** has an incompatible unit, wrong subject, unresolved direction-changing conflict, missing evidence, or another defect that prevents its use.

### Keep the units separate

The atlas uses several units: source documents, municipalities, bargaining-unit cycles, implementation events, mechanism records, staffing records, local comparisons, growth records, and claims. Counts with different units are shown separately and should not be added together.

An **implementation event** is one documented municipal compensation action for a municipality, compensation cycle, compensation mechanism, and side. Corroborating sources can strengthen confidence in an event; they do not create additional events.

## Page 3 — How to read maps, counts, and claims

### Maps

A hex map groups nearby municipalities into equal-sized areas so regional patterns are easier to see. Darker areas contain more distinct documented implementation events. The maps use a fixed national frame and a separate Alaska inset. They do not show the number of workers, the size of a wage effect, or national prevalence.

### Counts and side shares

Every chart should name its analytical unit and denominator. A safety share is calculated only among events with a defensible side classification unless the figure explicitly says otherwise. Mixed, side-independent, and unresolved records remain visible rather than being silently assigned to either side. More safety-side evidence can reflect institutional differences, documentation practices, clearer department labels, uneven discovery, or some combination of these factors.

### Claims

The atlas distinguishes direct support, conditional support, mechanism support, mixed evidence, and unsupported claims. A mechanism-supported claim shows that a process can operate; it does not estimate an average effect. Local examples remain local. Adoption is not payment. No paid stage observed in the retained evidence does not mean never paid.

The project does not provide a national safety wage-gap estimate, a national prevalence estimate, a regression result, a fixed national growth advantage, or a causal effect estimate.
